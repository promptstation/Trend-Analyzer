## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Map adoption funnels onto the five-stage innovation-decision process with stage-specific bottleneck diagnosis.
2. Classify decisions as optional, collective, or authority with diffusion-speed implications.
3. Apply the channel-division rule (media for awareness, peers for persuasion).
4. Analyze the adoption-use gap and re-invention dynamics.
5. Design stage-matched interventions from information through confirmation support.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the micro-process beneath population curves: the five-stage innovation-decision process — knowledge (awareness and understanding of how it works), persuasion (attitude formation driven largely by uncertainty reduction and expected relative advantage), decision (trial-and-commit activity), implementation (putting it into use, with the reinvention dynamic where users modify innovations during adoption), and confirmation (seeking reinforcement, with discontinuance risk if contradictory messages arrive); the stages' analytic uses — locating a target segment's decision stage to select the right intervention (information versus persuasion versus trial-facilitation versus support); decision types distinguished — optional decisions (individual discretion, fastest diffusion), collective decisions (consensus-required, slowest), and authority decisions (mandated, instantly wide but shallow in commitment), with mixed realities (individual adoption inside organizational mandates); the unit of adoption — individuals, households, firms, and communities as different diffusion units with different processes; the persuasion stage deepened — why attitudes toward innovations form under uncertainty, and the evidence that peers beat media for persuasion while media beat peers for awareness (the classic division of channel labor); implementation problems — the adoption-use gap (buying without using, installing without integrating) as the most under-measured stage; re-invention — user modification's role in fitting innovations to local contexts (the mobile-money customization patterns) and its acceleration-versus-drift effects on diffusion; confirmation and discontinuance — post-adoption reinforcement needs and the two discontinuance types (replacement by better innovation, rejection after use) previewed for section 14; the organizational analog — firm-level adoption stages with additional politics and structure dimensions; and the process-mapping practice — charting a specific innovation's decision funnel by segment with stage-specific bottleneck diagnosis.
