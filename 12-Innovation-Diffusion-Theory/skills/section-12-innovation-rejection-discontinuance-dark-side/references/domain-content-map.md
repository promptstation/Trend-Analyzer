## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers what the canon underweighted: rejection — the decision not to adopt, its three types (rejection before trial, after trial, and continuous rejection), and the analysis problem that non-adopters are invisible in adoption data (the survivorship bias of diffusion curves); the reasons innovations fail to diffuse — attribute misfit (complexity, poor relative advantage against the actual incumbent), channel failures (no credible persuasion source for the segment), system mismatch (infrastructure, regulation, cultural incompatibility), and the base-rate reality that most launched innovations never reach mainstream adoption; discontinuance — later discontinuance (abandoning after use, the churn phenomenon) versus replacement discontinuance (switching to a better innovation), with discontinuance-rate measurement as adoption-quality evidence; the pro-innovation bias confronted — the field's documented tilt (assuming diffusion is desirable, inevitable, and complete), and the corrective view that some adoptions harm (addictive design's diffusion, supersession waste) while some rejections are rational; the individual-blame bias — labeling non-adopters as laggards rather than analyzing system failures (access, price, design), with the equity implications (diffusion research's historical blind spot on who cannot adopt); displaced and dying technologies — the de-diffusion side (landlines, incandescent bulbs, DVD adoption collapse) as mirror-image curves informing replacement dynamics; zombie innovations — products persisting in niche segments long after mainstream death (the long-tail survivor patterns); failed-diffusion case library — documented flops decomposed by failure cause (the segment that never persuaded, the complement that never arrived, the incumbent that blocked); the rejection-analysis method — surveying and interviewing non-adopters, analyzing trial-abandonment funnels, and reading churn reasons as diffusion diagnostics; and the balanced diffusion assessment — forecasting adoption ceilings with rejection and discontinuance mechanisms included, not just growth extrapolation.
