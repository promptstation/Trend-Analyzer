## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the structural accelerators and brakes: network effects defined — value increasing with user count, distinguished as direct (same-side: communication products), indirect/cross-side (complementary goods: platforms and ecosystems), and data network effects (learning from usage), each with different diffusion signatures; critical mass — the threshold user level where growth becomes self-sustaining, and the pre-critical-mass dynamics requiring subsidized or seeded adoption (the chicken-egg management strategies); two-sided and multi-sided markets — platform diffusion as simultaneous adoption problems across sides with subsidy-side and money-side strategy; switching costs and lock-in — installed bases as diffusion barriers for challengers and accelerants for incumbents, with compatibility standards as the battleground; threshold models of adoption — Granovetter's collective-behavior logic applied: individuals adopt when enough of their reference set has adopted, producing cascade potential and tipping dynamics (the network-course connection); complex contagion requirements — behavior adoption needing multiple social confirmations versus information spreading on single exposure (Centola-Macy), explaining why network-effect products diffuse through clustered networks; standards wars — format and protocol competition as coupled diffusions (VHS-Betamax, Blu-ray-HD-DVD lineages) where expectations drive bandwagon adoption; interoperability and bridging — adapters and open standards lowering switching costs to accelerate diffusion; the installed-base advantage versus inertia — when incumbency blocks better technologies (QWERTY-path-dependence debates with evidence nuance); measuring network effects — the empirical methods (value-per-user estimation, cross-side elasticity, churn-versus-size analysis) distinguishing genuine network effects from marketing claims; and the diffusion-strategy synthesis — sequencing adoption across sides, seeding critical mass, managing standards, and timing the self-sustaining handoff.
