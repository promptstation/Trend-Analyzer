## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the forecasting practice: adoption measurement foundations — penetration definitions (users, households, firms, units), denominator selection, the ever-adopted versus current-user distinction, and usage-depth versus adoption-breadth measurement; data sources — sales and activation data, survey panels, app-analytics cohorts, platform statistics, and industry trackers, each with coverage and incentive biases; leading indicators of adoption — trial rates, search interest (the documented correlation between search volume and subsequent adoption), social-sentiment shift, complement availability (accessories, integrations), price trajectories, and reference-customer accumulation as the early-evidence set; forecasting methods hierarchy — analog forecasting (reference-class technologies' historical curves as priors), parametric models (Bass, logistic fits with intervals), machine-learning approaches on feature-rich data, and expert-judgment adjustment, with the evidence favoring analog-plus-parametric hybrids for new innovations; the analog method in depth — selecting reference classes by attribute profile and system similarity, adjusting for era acceleration and category differences, and the base-rate discipline against optimism; milestone forecasting — timing 10/25/50% penetration with uncertainty ranges rather than point predictions; the revision cycle — re-forecasting as data arrives (the Bayesian updating practice) and the documented over-reaction to early data as the calibration enemy; scenario integration — combining adoption curves with driver scenarios (regulation, complement arrival, price drops) from the macro course; forecast communication — fan charts, milestone tables, and assumption registers for strategy audiences; the forecast audit — scoring past adoption forecasts against outcomes to calibrate the practice; and the adoption-forecast deliverable — reference-class reasoning, parametric projection with intervals, indicator dashboard, and revision schedule as one document.
