## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the curve mathematics and its varieties: the logistic S-curve — cumulative adoption following the logistic function, its parameters (midpoint timing, steepness), and the rate-of-adoption bell curve as its derivative; the epidemic-model intuition — adoption as contact-driven infection (the Griliches hybrid-corn logic: profitability-determined speed), with the simple-contagion structure contrasted against the complex-contagion reality of most behavior adoptions (network-course connection); time-to-milestones — the empirical regularities and their variance (years from launch to 25%/50%/75% penetration across technology categories, with the documented acceleration of consumer technologies — electrification decades, telephone decades, television years, smartphones years, apps months); the saturation ceiling — market potential as the asymptote, and the distinction between saturation of the adopter population versus continued growth via population turnover and replacement; diffusion pattern taxonomy — the rapid-complete, slow-complete, rapid-displacement (leapfrog), and re-anchoring patterns with real category examples; the Gompertz alternative — asymmetric growth curves fitting categories with slow late saturation; rate determinants consolidated — attribute perceptions, decision type, channel structure, change-agent effort, and social-system structure as the S-curve's drivers; curve-shape diagnostics — what steepness, inflection timing, and asymmetry reveal about the underlying diffusion mechanism (mandate-driven versus organic versus network-effect-driven); the multi-S-curve reality — technology lifecycles as sequences of overlapping S-curves (each generation re-diffusing), and the analyst's task of locating the current curve; data requirements — penetration series construction (units, users, households, firms as denominators) with denominator-choice effects on apparent rates; and the curve-reading skill — classifying an observed adoption series by pattern type, inferring its mechanism, and projecting its trajectory with the appropriate model.
