## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the field and its trend-analysis mission: diffusion of innovations defined — Rogers' formulation of how an innovation is communicated through channels over time among the members of a social system, the four elements (innovation, communication channels, time, social system) each previewed as an analytic dimension; innovation defined precisely — an idea, practice, or object perceived as new by the relevant unit of adoption, where perception of newness (not objective novelty) drives behavior; the research lineage — rural-sociology origins (Ryan-Gross hybrid corn seed diffusion as the classic study), medical-innovation diffusion (Coleman's drug-adoption work), and Rogers' 1962 synthesis that became the field's canonical framework; why trend analysts need diffusion theory — it supplies the population-level adoption logic beneath observed trends: every durable trend is a diffusion process, and the theory provides stage vocabulary, curve mathematics, and attribute diagnostics for forecasting adoption of AI, biotech, spatial computing, and consumer innovations; the S-curve introduced — the near-universal cumulative-adoption shape (slow start, steep middle, saturation) as diffusion's signature, with rate-of-adoption as its derivative; diffusion versus virality distinguished — awareness/content spread (fast, shallow, course-5 territory) versus adoption behavior change (slow, deep, requiring the complex-contagion dynamics previewed from network theory); the innovation ecosystem — firms, standards, infrastructure, regulation, and complements as the system context diffusion runs through; the central time concept — innovation-decision period, rate of adoption, and adopter sequence as the three temporalities; the field's acknowledged biases previewed — pro-innovation, individual-blame, and recall biases Rogers himself catalogued; and the course map from decision processes and attributes through adopter categories, curve mathematics, the chasm, network effects, and disruption to rejection, measurement, forecasting, and capstone.
