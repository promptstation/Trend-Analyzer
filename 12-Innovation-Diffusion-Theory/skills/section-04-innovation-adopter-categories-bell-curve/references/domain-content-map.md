## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the field's most famous and most misused construct: the adopter-classification system — innovators (roughly first 2.5% by the normal-curve convention, venturesome, financially able to absorb losses, cosmopolitan networks), early adopters (next ~13.5%, socially integrated, opinion-leader-rich, the diffusion gatekeepers), early majority (~34%, deliberate, informal peer contact), late majority (~34%, skeptical, adopting under economic necessity or peer pressure), and laggards (~16%, tradition-focused, last-resort adopters) with each category's network position and decision style; opinion leadership and change agents — the locally influential early adopters who de-risk innovations for the majority, and the external change agents (sales, extension workers, evangelists) with their credibility dynamics; homophily and heterophily — the communication principle that diffusion runs easiest among similar others, making the early-adopter-to-majority crossing a heterophily challenge; the categories as positions not personalities — the crucial correction that classification reflects adoption timing within a specific system, so the same person is an innovator for one product and a laggard for another; the bell curve's statistical status — the normal-distribution assumption as descriptive convention, with documented deviations (bimodal curves from segment splits, skewed curves from mandate effects); cross-cultural category behavior — how the same innovation reorders categories across markets (mobile money's mainstream-first adoption in Kenya versus laggard status in the US as the documented inversion); misuse catalog — the stereotype errors (laggard-blaming, innovator-as-hero narratives) and the individual-blame bias Rogers flagged; the analytic use — adopter-category mapping of a current customer base to diagnose which segment the diffusion has reached and what the next segment requires; and the vocabulary discipline — using categories as timing summaries with segment evidence, not as identity labels.
