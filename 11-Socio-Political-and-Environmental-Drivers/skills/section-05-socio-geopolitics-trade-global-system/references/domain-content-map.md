## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the international driver layer: the trade-policy toolkit — tariffs, quotas, export controls, sanctions, and investment screening as trend levers with transmission channels (prices, availability, supply-chain redesign); the deglobalization/fragmentation debate — the documented shifts (trade-policy restrictions rising since 2010s, friend-shoring and near-shoring strategies, bloc formation) versus deep integration persistence, and what fragmentation means for product availability and cost trends; sanctions regimes — their design, evasion dynamics, and market effects (energy, finance, technology flows rerouted), with sanctions-compliance as a corporate function; supply-chain geopolitics — chokepoint dependencies (semiconductors, rare earths, shipping lanes, energy transit) as structural vulnerabilities whose disruption produces instant category trends; conflict economics — wars' effects on commodity prices, energy, food security, migration flows, and defense-industry growth, analyzed through current and historical cases; economic statecraft — currency policy, debt diplomacy, and market-access weapons as slow drivers; nationalism and economic patriotism — buy-local movements as state-encouraged consumption trends, and their durability evidence; the Global South's rising leverage — resource nationalism, trade-corridor alternatives, and middle-power hedging strategies reshaping market access; technology geopolitics — the US-China tech contest (chips, AI, platforms, standards) as the decade's dominant structural driver with market-bifurcation implications; geopolitical risk measurement — indices, event data, and expert-elicitation methods with their biases; and the geopolitics brief — tracing a geopolitical development through trade, cost, availability, and sentiment channels to specific market-trend implications.
