## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers regulation's dual market power: regulation creating trends — the documented pattern that rules build industries (renewable mandates, data-privacy compliance services, accessibility requirements, sustainability reporting spawning consultancy and software sectors), and the compliance-economy concept; regulation killing or reshaping categories — bans and restrictions (single-use plastics, trans fats, incandescent bulbs, certain chemicals) forcing substitution waves and innovation, with the substitution-trend tracking method; the current regulatory wave — digital regulation (EU's DSA/DMA regime: platform obligations, transparency duties, gatekeeper rules), privacy law (GDPR's global diffusion pattern, 'Brussels effect' — regulatory export through market power), sustainability regulation (CSRD reporting, green-claims rules, due-diligence directives), and AI regulation (the EU AI Act's risk-tier structure as the first comprehensive regime) each mapped to affected trend domains; standards as invisible regulation — technical standards, certification schemes (organic, fair-trade, ISO lineage), and labeling rules shaping product evolution; regulatory divergence — jurisdictions splitting (EU/US/China rule differences) forcing regional product strategies and the fragmentation cost; enforcement and penalties — the fine-and-precedent mechanism, enforcement-wave detection, and compliance-deadline calendars as trend-timing tools; regulatory arbitrage and its closure — industries relocating to lax regimes, and crackdowns as disruption events; the analyst's regulatory-impact method — for any rule: scope (who/what covered), timeline (deadlines, phase-ins), cost (compliance burden), substitution (what replaces restricted products), and opportunity (new compliance markets); horizon scanning — consultations, draft rules, and agency signals as the earliest indicators; and the regulation brief — translating legal texts into market-trend implications for non-legal audiences.
