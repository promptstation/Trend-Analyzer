## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers how governments shape markets structurally: the state's economic instruments — taxation, subsidies, procurement, licensing, standards, and trade policy as direct trend levers (each with worked examples: sugar taxes reshaping beverage reformulation, EV subsidies creating adoption curves, procurement driving supplier practices); the policy cycle — agenda-setting, formulation, adoption, implementation, and evaluation as stages where analysts can intervene and predict, with Kingdon's multiple-streams model (problems, policies, politics converging at windows of opportunity) as the forecasting framework for when change happens; policy lags — proposal to passage to enforcement to market effect as distinct timelines, and the analyst error of treating announcement as impact; implementation realities — enforcement capacity, bureaucratic discretion, and compliance culture determining whether written policy becomes lived policy (the gap widest in low-capacity settings); regulatory regimes compared — precautionary versus risk-based approaches (EU versus US patterns in chemicals, food, tech), and their divergent market consequences; industrial policy's return — state-directed investment in semiconductors, batteries, and AI as the current structural driver reshaping supply chains; political risk taxonomy — policy risk (rules change), transfer risk (capital controls), operational risk (disruption), and sovereign risk, with commercial political-risk assessment practice; government as consumer — public procurement's trend-setting power (standards diffusion through supply chains); lobbying and interest-group influence — who shapes policy, disclosure regimes, and reading influence attempts as leading indicators; political stability and instability — regime types, unrest risk, and their investment and consumption effects; and the policy-watch skill — tracking bills, consultations, and agency rulemaking as early-warning data.
