## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the event-politics layer: election cycles and economies — the political business cycle literature (documented fiscal timing around elections, contested magnitude), and the more robust finding that uncertainty itself suppresses investment and big-ticket consumption pre-election with post-election release; elections as sentiment events — consumer-confidence responses to outcomes (the documented partisan gap: supporters' confidence rises, opponents' falls, with the asymmetry and its decay), and separating sentiment effects from policy effects; electoral realignments — voting-behavior shifts (class dealignment, education realignment, youth-versus-age divides) as slow political drivers of policy trajectories; issue salience — which issues dominate campaigns (economy, immigration, climate, rights) and how salience shifts redirect political consumption and brand-positioning pressure; populism and anti-establishment waves — their documented market correlates (protectionism, institutional distrust, media fragmentation) as a structural trend environment; the geography of elections — national, sub-national, and supranational (EU Parliament) contests with different market reach, and the election calendar as a monitoring artifact; referendum and initiative politics — direct-democracy events as concentrated policy shocks (Brexit as the documented case: currency, trade, migration, and consumption effects); post-election policy forecasting — reading mandates, coalitions, and constraints to predict actual policy versus campaign promises (the promise-fulfillment research); political polarization's consumer effects — brand and media consumption sorting along political lines, the boycott-terrain implications, and polarization as amplifier of every culture-war trend; election-adjacent misinformation — campaign information environments as trend-distortion risks; and the election-watch practice — building pre/post election impact assessments with sentiment, policy, and uncertainty channels separated.
