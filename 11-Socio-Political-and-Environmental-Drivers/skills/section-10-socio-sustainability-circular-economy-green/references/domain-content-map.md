## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the sustainability economy's structure: sustainability as market regime — the shift from niche ethics to mainstream requirement (ESG investment scale and its political backlash, corporate sustainability functions, supply-chain due-diligence duties) as structural context for green trends; the circular economy — the reduce-reuse-repair-recycle framework as economic model, its business manifestations (resale platforms' documented growth, rental and subscription models, repair-right legislation's market effects, recycled-material mandates) and the circularity-gap evidence (recycled-input shares remaining low); secondhand and resale — the fastest-moving consumer-facing sustainability trend, its platform infrastructure, generational adoption patterns, and stigma-to-status reversal as a completed meaning migration; sustainable consumption debates — the evidence that individual-consumption shifts alone cannot meet climate targets (the footprint-logic critique), and what this means for trend claims (system versus behavior); green premium realities — where sustainable options reach price parity (solar, EVs in some markets) versus persistent premiums, as the adoption-gate variable; packaging and plastic — the regulation-and-substitution wave (bans, recycled-content mandates, alternative-material innovation) as a visible category-transforming driver; food-system sustainability — plant-based categories' boom-correction cycle, alt-protein policy support, food-waste reduction movements, and labeling regimes (eco-scores); green claims governance — the tightening rules on environmental marketing (EU green-claims directive lineage, FTC Green Guides), and the compliance-driven communication shift; backlash dynamics — the anti-ESG and anti-green-regulation movements as countervailing drivers with real market effects (fund withdrawals, policy reversals); sustainability reporting infrastructure — CSRD, ISSB, and disclosure standards creating the data layer analysts use; and the sustainability brief — assessing a category's green-transition position across regulation, cost parity, consumer demand, and backlash exposure.
