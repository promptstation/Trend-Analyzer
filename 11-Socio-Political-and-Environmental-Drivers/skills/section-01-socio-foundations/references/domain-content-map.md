## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the course's domain and its place in trend intelligence: the macro-environment framework — the PESTEL/STEEP scanning tradition (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) as the analyst's checklist for forces that shape trends from outside markets, with this course covering the political, social-movement, regulatory, and environmental quadrants in depth; the transmission logic — how macro forces reach consumer behavior through identifiable channels (prices, availability, legality, social norms, risk perception, identity) rather than mysteriously; the trend-analyst's stake — socio-political and environmental drivers create trends (sustainability consumption after climate-salience rises), kill trends (regulation banning a product category), accelerate them (subsidies), and reframe them (movements attaching moral meaning to purchases); force characteristics — magnitude, duration, speed, reversibility, and geographic scope as the dimensions separating a blip from a driver; slow versus fast forces — institutional and climate change operating in decades versus elections and shocks operating in weeks, and the multi-speed monitoring this requires; the evidence base — where driver data lives (election results, policy texts, movement events, climate records, opinion polls) with each source's lags and biases; prediction humility — macro-political forecasting's documented poor record versus structural analysis's better one, setting the course's method: track conditions and channels, scenario the events; the ideology and values layer — how political worldviews shape which drivers matter to which segments (the culture-war dimension of consumption); case previews — elections redirecting spending, regulation creating industries (renewables) and destroying them (single-use plastics bans), movements revaluing brands (boycotts), and climate reshaping categories (insurance, agriculture, cooling); and the course map from politics and regulation through movements and consumption to environmental drivers, monitoring systems, and scenario capstone.
