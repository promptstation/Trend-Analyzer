## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the decision framework for uncertain forces: risk versus uncertainty — measurable probabilities versus Knightian unknowability, and why macro-political events mostly live in the second category requiring scenario methods over point forecasts; the forecasting record — documented poor accuracy of political event predictions (expert-forecasting studies, Tetlock's findings) versus better structural/demographic forecasting, calibrating humility; scenario planning method — the Shell lineage and Schwartz's discipline: identify driving forces and predetermined elements, rank by importance and uncertainty, build the two-axes scenario matrix, flesh out narratives with internal consistency, and derive implications per scenario; scenario quality standards — plausibility, distinctness, relevance, and challenge to assumptions, versus decorative storytelling; triggers and signposts — the monitoring practice that makes scenarios operational: pre-specified indicators whose movement signals which scenario is emerging, with review cadence; probability-weighted scenarios — when to assign judgmental probabilities (base rates, reference classes) versus keeping scenarios unordered, and communicating both; early-warning indicators — leading signals for macro shifts (policy consultations, elite rhetoric shifts, market pricing, movement mobilization) assembled into watchlists; stress-testing strategies — evaluating a trend thesis or business plan against each scenario for robustness (strategies that survive all scenarios versus scenario-bets); black-swan and tail thinking — fat-tail awareness without paralysis, the precautionary posture for low-probability-high-impact drivers; red-team practice — structured challenge of the expected-case narrative; the scenario deliverable — the macro-driver scenario set with signpost dashboard, implications matrix, and robustness assessment; and integration — scenarios updated as fast-signal courses (attention, sentiment) and slow-signal courses (demographics, climate) feed evidence in.
