## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers environmental drivers beyond climate: pandemic aftermath as environmental shock — the zoonotic-spillover context, documented post-pandemic consumption restructuring (remote work's durable residue, health-hygiene categories, supply-chain redesign) as the decade's biggest shock-driven trend complex; natural-disaster economics — earthquakes, floods, cyclones as regional demand shocks with reconstruction dynamics, and disaster-frequency trends independent of climate attribution; water stress — the emerging structural driver (aquifer depletion, river stress, urban water crises) affecting agriculture, industry location, and consumer categories (water-efficiency tech, bottled-water politics), with basin-level data sources; resource constraints and critical minerals — the material bottleneck of the green transition (lithium, cobalt, rare-earth supply geopolitics) as a trend driver for technology costs and availability; energy price volatility — the 2020s energy-shock episode and its documented consumption effects (efficiency adoption surges, heating behavior change, industrial relocation) as the template for price-shock transmission; biodiversity and ecosystem services — nature-related financial disclosure (TNFD lineage), deforestation-linked commodity regulation (EU deforestation regulation), and biodiversity as an emerging compliance-and-consumption theme; pollution and health — air-quality data as a consumer-visible environmental signal (purifier markets, mask norms, relocation decisions), and chemical-regulation waves (PFAS restrictions as the current substitution-driver case); agricultural and fishery shocks — yield failures, disease outbreaks (avian flu lineage), and stock collapses as food-category trend drivers; environmental migration — displacement as demographic-economic driver connecting to the migration course; the shock-monitoring practice — environmental data feeds (disaster databases, water stress indices, air quality, commodity prices) as trend-dashboard inputs; and the environmental-shock brief — assessing a shock's category, regional, and duration implications with reconstruction and substitution channels.
