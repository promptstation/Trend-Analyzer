## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the defining environmental force: the physical baseline — documented warming, the IPCC assessment framework (attribution confidence, scenario families SSPs/RCPs, regional projections), and what the science establishes as trend-relevant boundary conditions (heat days, water stress, extreme-event frequency, agricultural zones shifting); climate salience — public concern's rise and volatility (event-driven spikes, generational and geographic variation, the opinion-response-to-experience evidence), and salience as the driver of climate-linked consumption; mitigation markets — the energy transition's consumer surface (EVs, heat pumps, solar, efficiency) with policy-dependence and cost-curve dynamics; adaptation markets — the faster-growing and less-noticed trend layer: cooling (AC demand explosions in warming regions), water management, resilient construction, climate migration services, and health (heat, vector-disease expansion); climate risk pricing — insurance retreat from high-risk zones (the documented withdrawal patterns), mortgage and property-value implications, and risk disclosure regimes reshaping investment; extreme-event economics — disasters as demand shocks (immediate relief, reconstruction booms, preparedness-category growth) and their attribution-linked salience effects; climate policy as market driver — carbon pricing schemes, net-zero commitments (corporate and national), and the green-industrial-policy wave (subsidy races) creating and killing categories; climate anxiety and behavior — the documented psychological dimension shaping consumption (flight shame, family-size debates, prepping) with effect-size honesty; greenwashing and credibility — the claims-verification infrastructure, regulatory crackdown on green claims, and consumer skepticism as trend friction; sector deep-dives — agriculture and food systems (yield shifts, crop-zone moves, alternative proteins' policy-and-climate linkage), tourism (destination viability, season shifts), and retail (supply-chain climate exposure); and the climate-driver brief — translating physical projections and policy trajectories into market-trend implications by sector and region.
