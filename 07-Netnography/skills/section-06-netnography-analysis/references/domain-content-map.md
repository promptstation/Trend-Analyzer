## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches making sense of the corpus: the analysis arc — immersion and familiarization, coding, theme development, interpretation, and verification as iterative cycles rather than linear steps; coding methods — open coding (line-by-line concept labeling), focused coding, and axial connections; in-vivo codes preserving community language as analytic assets; thematic analysis — Braun-Clarke's six-phase method adapted for netnographic corpora, with semantic versus latent themes (surface meanings versus underlying cultural assumptions); grounded theory approach — constant comparison, theoretical sampling, memoing, and saturation as the Charmaz lineage, and when theory-building suits the study; analytic memos as the thinking engine — writing interpretation as it emerges; framework and matrix methods — structured comparisons across communities, time periods, and member types; semiotic reading of multimodal data — visual conventions, emoji patterns, and aesthetic codes (bridging to the semiotics course); member checking and community verification — testing interpretations against insider responses with its political caveats; negative-case analysis — actively seeking disconfirming data as rigor practice; quantification touches — simple frequency counts of codes as supporting (never replacing) interpretation; software options — NVivo/ATLAS.ti versus lightweight spreadsheets and the tool-does-not-do-thinking discipline; and the analysis audit trail — from raw data through codes and themes to claims, documented for trustworthiness.
