## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Analyze self-presentation using dramaturgic front/back-stage models across account structures.
2. Evaluate authenticity norms and detection practices within communities.
3. Apply micro-celebrity and parasocial frameworks to creator-audience identity dynamics, study identity work through sustained observation of presentation and response, and account for anonymity, context-separation, and marginalized-identity dynamics in interpretation.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the self in digital tribes: Goffman's dramaturgy online — front-stage curation versus back-stage behavior, with alt-accounts, finstas, and private Discords as structured back-stages; impression management on algorithmic platforms — the audience-invisible problem (posting without knowing who sees), and its effects on self-presentation strategy; identity work — platforms as identity laboratories (especially for adolescents), identity play, and committed identity performance; authenticity as community value — the documented authenticity norms online, authenticity performances (calculated 'realness'), and community detection of inauthenticity; the authenticity paradox — performing unperformed selves; micro-celebrity — the self-branding practices (Senft and Marwick lineage) now generalized across cohorts, and its psychological trade-offs; parasocial dimensions — creator-audience relationships as identity resources for both sides; anonymity and pseudonymity — identity dissociation enabling exploration, and the disinhibition effects (positive and toxic); multiple-account life — context separation strategies as sophisticated audience management; identity and consumption — taste as identity marker, aesthetic identities (connecting to the semiotics course), and consumption communities as identity infrastructure; gender, race, and identity online — marginalized identity expression, community formation, and the harassment dynamics shaping presentation choices; and the netnographic method — studying identity through sustained observation of presentation, interaction, and community response rather than self-report alone.
