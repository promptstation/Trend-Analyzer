## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce

2. Detect humor layers (ironic, meta, anti-humor) with verification practice.
3. Analyze discourse structures (threading, ratioing) as interaction order, and avoid outsider misreading through in-context and member-verified interpretation.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches reading community communication: internet linguistics foundations — platform-shaped registers, orthographic choices as social signals (capitalization, punctuation absence, intentional misspelling), and code-switching between community and mainstream registers; slang dynamics — in-group coinage, diffusion, and mainstream death cycles (connecting to the memetics course), with the analyst's task of tracking competence markers; humor systems — absurdist, ironic, meta, and anti-humor traditions online, layer-detection difficulty for outsiders, and humor as norm enforcement; emoji and visual language — emoji semantics by cohort (skull emoji, crying emoji generational splits), reaction-image vocabularies, and typographic tone markers (/j, /s); multimodal communication — video-native expression (TikTok's gestural and audio grammar), green-screen discourse, and format conventions as language; discourse structure — threading, quoting, ratioing, and reply dynamics as community interaction order; identity performance in language — AAVE appropriation debates, stan-speak, corporate-speak mockery, and register as authenticity signal; misreading risks — the documented failures of outsiders (brands, researchers, media) misparsing irony and slang, and verification practice (in-context reading, member consultation); language change observation — netnography as real-time sociolinguistics, capturing coinage and diffusion as it happens; documentation practice — building a community glossary with usage contexts and confidence levels; and the interpretation standard — claiming meaning only with contextual and member-verified evidence.
