## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Practice participant observation across the observer-participant spectrum managing role tensions.
2. Write thick descriptions distinguishing behavior from layered meaning.
3. Maintain multi-register fieldnotes (jottings, memos, reflexive notes).
4. Execute the fieldwork arc from entry through rapport to exit.
5. Build cultural competency in a community's references and competence markers over sustained immersion.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the fieldwork tradition netnography inherits: ethnography's core method — participant observation, with the observer-participant spectrum and the dual role's tensions (going native versus staying analytical); immersion — sustained presence over time as the mechanism that converts strange behavior into intelligible practice, and why drive-by data collection fails at meaning; thick description — Geertz's concept illustrated (the wink versus the blink): behavior plus its layered cultural meanings, as the output standard for netnographic writing; emic versus etic perspectives — insider categories and outsider analytic frames held in productive tension; fieldnotes craft — jottings, expanded notes, analytic memos, and reflexive notes as distinct registers; the fieldwork arc — entry, rapport building, routine participation, focused observation, and exit; cultural competency development — learning a community's in-jokes, reference frames, and competence markers as the researcher's rite of passage; context collapse awareness — the ethnographer's advantage of reading behavior in its native context; classic ethnographic lessons transferred online — Malinowski's imponderabilia of actual life (the small daily practices) as exactly what archives of posts preserve; time as method — longitudinal presence capturing community change, trend emergence, and norm evolution that snapshots miss; and the discipline of description before interpretation — separating observed behavior from inferred meaning in notes.
