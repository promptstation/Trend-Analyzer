## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the theory of the groups being studied: community online — the definitional debates (wellman's networked individualism versus community intact), and the working criteria of sustained interaction, shared norms, mutual recognition, and belonging; digital tribes — Maffesoli's neo-tribe concept (fluid, affective, consumption-linked groupings) applied to online fandoms, aesthetics communities, and identity niches, with tribe versus subculture distinctions (fluidity, consumption focus, multiple membership); communities of practice — legitimate peripheral participation (Lave-Wenger) as the model for how newcomers become members, directly observable in community onboarding; fandom studies — fan communities as interpretive and productive cultures (Jenkins' textual poachers lineage), fan labor, and fan-platform tensions; belonging mechanics — in-group markers (language, aesthetics, references), boundary work (who's a poser, gatekeeping), and status hierarchies (karma, roles, recognition); affective communities — shared feeling as glue (comfort communities, rage communities, cringe communities); platform-shaped belonging — algorithmic niches producing micro-tribes at scale (the 'side' cultures of TikTok: BookTok, GymTok, cottagecore spheres); tribe lifecycle — formation, norm crystallization, growth, fragmentation, mainstream exposure, and dissolution or retreat; consumption tribes — brand communities (Muñiz-O'Guinn), anti-brand communities, and taste tribes as market-relevant formations; and the trend-analyst application — mapping the tribe landscape around a trend: which communities carry it, their boundaries, status systems, and openness to outsiders.
