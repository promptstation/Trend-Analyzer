## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Applies the method commercially: consumer culture theory (CCT) — the Arnould-Thompson research program treating consumption as cultural practice, providing netnography's marketing-theory home; insight types netnography delivers — unmet needs in community language, meaning structures around products and categories, norm maps for communication, and early aesthetic/value signals; the brand-community literature — brand communities' value (loyalty, co-creation, advocacy) and their ownership feelings (communities resent corporate takeover); innovation and co-creation — lead-user discovery in communities (von Hippel lineage adapted online), user-generated innovation patterns, and community-sourced product development cases; trend-spotting practice — netnography as the qualitative front-end of trend pipelines, detecting weak signals (new vocabularies, aesthetic experiments, value shifts) before metric visibility; competitive intelligence ethics — observing public competitor communities without infiltration deception; communication strategy inputs — tone, taboo, and reference maps preventing brand missteps; the extraction critique confronted — communities' awareness of being researched and mined, backlash dynamics, and value-return practices (crediting, sharing findings, supporting community needs); client-work realities — translating ethnographic findings for marketing audiences without flattening complexity, the insight-deck versus thick-report tension; agency-versus-in-house practice; case patterns — successful community-informed launches and documented extraction failures; and the commercial research standard — insight claims traceable to community evidence with ethics documentation.
