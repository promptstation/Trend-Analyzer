## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches designing netnographic studies: question formulation — cultural questions (what does X mean to this community, how do norms work here, how is identity performed) versus metric questions, and shaping vague business briefs ('understand Gen Z') into answerable ethnographic questions; site selection — purposive sampling of communities by relevance, activity, accessibility, and diversity criteria; multi-site design — comparing communities across platforms (the same trend in TikTok, Reddit, and Discord) to separate platform effects from cultural ones; entry protocols — reading community rules and norms before participating, the disclosure decision (announced researcher versus member role, and hybrid positions), and lurker-phase duration norms; access realities — public versus gated communities, invitation cultures, mod relationships, and platform-API-independent manual fieldwork; sampling within sites — which threads, members, periods, and events to focus on (maximum-variation and critical-case logic); the iterative design of qualitative research — questions and sites evolving as understanding grows, with documented iteration rather than fixed protocols; scope discipline — bounded studies (one community, one phenomenon, one period) versus sprawling ones; triangulation design — combining archival, elicited, and observational data by plan; and the research proposal artifact — question, site rationale, participation plan, ethics statement, timeline, and outputs.
