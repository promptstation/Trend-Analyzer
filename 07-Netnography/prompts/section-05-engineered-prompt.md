Develop a comprehensive, professional-level learning module on **“Ethics of Online Fieldwork”** as a core intermediate stage of a curriculum in Qualitative digital ethnography and consumer research applied to online community behavior and the digital tribes of Gen Z and Gen Alpha. Cover the method's most distinctive responsibility: the public-private continuum — why 'technically public' does not equal 'ethically free to use', with community expectations of privacy as the operative standard (the AoIR guidelines' situated-judgment approach); informed consent in netnography — when and how to seek it (disclosed presence, interview consent, community-level permission from moderators), and the debated cases (large public communities, sensitive topics, vulnerable populations); harm assessment — identification risk from quotes and screenshots, community backlash, doxxing exposure, and the compound-harm risk when research is quoted out of context; anonymity and attribution practice — pseudonymization, image alteration, avoiding searchability of quoted text, and the tension with crediting creators (connecting to memetic attribution norms); vulnerable communities — minors (Gen Alpha research specifics), marginalized groups, and mental-health spaces requiring heightened protection; researcher safety — harassment exposure, emotional labor, and platform-account risks during immersion; dual-use awareness — commercial netnography's extraction critique, community benefit considerations, and the 'helicopter research' problem; legal overlays — GDPR/data-protection duties, platform terms of service versus research exemptions, and copyright in quoted content; ethics-by-design — building the ethics review into study design rather than bolting it on; and the ethics documentation artifact — decision log covering consent, anonymity, sensitivity, and harm-mitigation choices with rationales. The module should assume the learner is progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and should function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology.
