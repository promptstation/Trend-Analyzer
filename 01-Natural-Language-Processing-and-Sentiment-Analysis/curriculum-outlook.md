# Natural Language Processing & Sentiment Analysis — Curriculum Content Outlook

(Beginner-to-expert progression, compressed to 15 sections per the Researching-Outline method.)

**Section 1 — Foundations: Text as Data and the NLP Pipeline**

**Summary:** Introduces NLP as the trend analyst's core instrument: the field defined and its pipeline — acquisition, preprocessing, representation, modeling, interpretation, and deployment; the text sources that carry public emotional undercurrents — social media posts, reviews, comments, search queries, news, and forum threads — with each source's population, bias, and access profile; linguistic levels (tokens, syntax, semantics, pragmatics) and which trend tasks live at each level; the compressed history from rule-based through statistical to neural and LLM-era methods as working lineage; why text is hard — ambiguity, variation, context dependence, irony — and what that implies for measurement claims; setting up the analysis stack (Python, corpora, notebooks, version control); the measurement targets of trend text analytics — sentiment and emotion, topics, entities, novelty, and volume — stated as distinct questions; and the course map from representation through modeling to monitored production systems.

**Absorbed Skill:** Explain the NLP pipeline stages and what each contributes to trend measurement, characterize trend-relevant text sources with their collection biases, map trend tasks to linguistic levels, set up a working text-analysis environment, and articulate what can and cannot be validly inferred from text at scale.

**Section 2 — Preprocessing and Text Representation**

**Summary:** Covers turning raw text into analysis-ready data: tokenization — word, subword (BPE and friends), and the challenges of social text (hashtags, handles, emojis, URLs, elongation like "soooo"); normalization decisions — casing, slang handling and its trade-offs, stemming versus lemmatization, and what normalization destroys; stop words reconsidered; the vector-space model and bag-of-words representations; TF-IDF derived and its variants; n-grams, collocations, and pointwise mutual information for phrase discovery; sparsity, dimensionality, and hashing tricks at overview; feature hygiene — fitting preprocessing only on training data to avoid leakage; character-level representations for morphology and noise robustness; corpus construction discipline — deduplication, language filtering, boilerplate removal; and the bridge to models — how representation choices constrain everything downstream.

**Absorbed Skill:** Build preprocessing pipelines appropriate to social and review text, compute and interpret TF-IDF, n-gram, and collocation features, choose tokenization and normalization strategies with awareness of what each destroys, avoid preprocessing leakage in evaluation pipelines, and construct clean analysis-ready corpora from raw platform text.

**Section 3 — Classical Supervised Text Classification**

**Summary:** Teaches the workhorse modeling layer before neural methods: the classification task formalized — labels, features, and the train/validation/test discipline for text; multinomial naive Bayes with its bag-of-words independence assumption and why it remains competitive on small text datasets; logistic regression with L1/L2 regularization and coefficient interpretation as evidence; linear SVMs and the margin intuition; tree ensembles on text features at awareness level; handling class imbalance — class weighting, resampling, and threshold tuning; probability calibration and why raw scores mislead; multiclass strategies; baseline discipline — majority-class and lexicon baselines before any complex model; systematic error analysis — reading misclassifications as the primary improvement tool; when classical models still win — small data, interpretability needs, latency budgets; and the scikit-learn-style workflow with pipelines that bundle preprocessing and model.

**Absorbed Skill:** Implement and compare naive Bayes, logistic regression, and SVM text classifiers with proper evaluation splits, handle imbalanced text datasets through weighting and threshold tuning, interpret linear model coefficients as calibrated evidence, establish strong baselines before adding complexity, and run systematic error analysis to drive model improvement.

**Section 4 — Sentiment Analysis Fundamentals**

**Summary:** Covers the field's namesake task in depth: sentiment defined and decomposed — polarity, subjectivity, intensity — and the crucial three-way distinction between sentiment, emotion, and stance; lexicon-based methods — VADER for social text, SentiWordNet, NRC EmoLex, and licensed instruments like LIWC — with their assumptions and failure modes (negation, intensifiers, domain shift, sarcasm blindness); supervised sentiment classifiers and their annotation dependencies; granularity levels — document, sentence, and clause; the human-agreement ceiling and why disagreement is sometimes signal; domain dependence — "sick", "unpredictable", and "cold" flipping valence across domains; aggregating micro-level scores into population mood indices and the ecological-fallacy risks; famous claims audited — the Facebook emotional-cont contagion study controversy and Twitter-mood-predicts-markets claims evaluated against replication; validating a sentiment instrument against external ground truth (surveys, sales); and building a calibrated sentiment-monitoring product with honest uncertainty statements.

**Absorbed Skill:** Distinguish sentiment, emotion, and stance tasks and select methods accordingly, apply and evaluate lexicon-based tools including VADER with their failure modes, train supervised sentiment classifiers with domain validation, aggregate scores into population-level indices while avoiding ecological fallacies, and critically audit published sentiment-prediction claims.

**Section 5 — Word Embeddings and Distributional Semantics**

**Summary:** Teaches meaning as geometry: the distributional hypothesis and its consequences; static embedding methods — word2vec (skip-gram and CBOW with negative sampling), GloVe, and fastText's subword handling of morphology and out-of-vocabulary terms; using embeddings — similarity, clustering, and analogy arithmetic with the hype properly deflated (analogy performance depends on corpus and frequency artifacts); embeddings as cultural records — bias embedded in vector spaces (gender and ethnicity analogies) and debiasing approaches; diachronic semantics — measuring meaning change over time with alignment methods (Hamilton-style local/global alignment) as a genuine trend-early-warning signal (words acquiring new connotations before volume spikes); limitations — polysemy averaging in static vectors and the contextualized fix previewed; embedding features inside classical classifiers; training practice — corpus size, window, dimension choices; evaluation — intrinsic (similarity sets) versus extrinsic (downstream task) and why extrinsic wins; and the practitioner's view of embeddings as infrastructure across the whole trend stack.

**Absorbed Skill:** Train and evaluate word embeddings with intrinsic and extrinsic checks, use similarity and analogy operations with appropriate skepticism about artifacts, apply diachronic embedding methods to detect semantic drift as a trend signal, diagnose and mitigate bias in embedding spaces, and integrate embedding features into downstream classification pipelines.

**Section 6 — Neural and Transformer-Era NLP**

**Summary:** Covers the deep-learning shift at practitioner depth: sequence models — RNN/LSTM/GRU intuition for text and the vanishing-context problem they partially solved; CNNs over text at overview; the attention mechanism and why it replaced recurrence; transformers and BERT — pretraining and fine-tuning, masked language modeling, subword tokenization — and the GPT-style decoder lineage leading to LLMs; fine-tuning encoder models for trend tasks (sentiment, topic, toxicity, irony) with HuggingFace-style workflows including data collators, learning-rate schedules, and early stopping; the LLM alternative — zero-shot and few-shot classification by prompting, structured output, and their trade-offs versus fine-tuned small models on cost, latency, calibration, and consistency; hybrid designs — LLMs for labeling assistance and synthetic data with human verification; benchmark culture — leaderboards, dataset staleness, and contamination caveats in the LLM era; computational realities — GPU basics, mixed precision, distillation, and quantization at working level; and choosing architecture by task, volume, and budget rather than fashion.

**Absorbed Skill:** Fine-tune transformer encoders for text classification with modern library workflows, choose between fine-tuned small models and LLM prompting using cost, latency, calibration, and quality trade-offs, explain the attention and transformer shift at working level, evaluate models with contamination-aware benchmark literacy, and deploy computationally realistic NLP pipelines.

**Section 7 — Emotion, Aspect-Based, and Stance Analysis**

**Summary:** Teaches fine-grained opinion mining: emotion frameworks — discrete models (Ekman's basic emotions and their critiques, Plutchik's wheel), dimensional models (valence-arousal-dominance), and appraisal traditions, with the measurement controversy over whether text classifiers can validly detect felt emotion versus expressed affect language; emotion datasets and their annotation problems; aspect-based sentiment analysis — aspect extraction plus aspect-level sentiment (the SemEval ABSA lineage), applied to product reviews and brand feedback ("battery great, screen dim"); stance detection — favor/against/neutral toward a target, distinct from sentiment (supporting a claim versus liking it), with rumor-stance applications; intensity and comparative sentiment; intent and complaint mining for product intelligence; high-arousal emotions (anger, awe, anxiety, amusement) and their documented sharing advantage as the bridge to virality analysis; designing multi-label taxonomies for monitoring products; and evaluating fine-grained tasks where human agreement is low — soft labels and distributional annotation.

**Absorbed Skill:** Select and apply emotion frameworks with awareness of validity limits, implement aspect-based sentiment pipelines for reviews and brand feedback, distinguish and model stance versus sentiment versus emotion, design multi-label taxonomies for trend monitoring, and connect arousal-emotion findings to predictions about content spread.

**Section 8 — Social Media Text: Slang, Irony, Dialect, and Multimodality**

**Summary:** Covers the domain realities that break naive pipelines: platform vernaculars and slang dynamics — documenting drift through community sources, the speed of semantic turnover, and why static vocabularies decay; dialect and code-switching — AAVE and other dialects in social text, documented model bias against non-standard dialects (Blodgett-lineage findings), and fair-evaluation practices; emojis, emoticons, and stickers as sentiment and pragmatics carriers with platform-specific meanings; hashtags as simultaneous topics, speech acts, and irony vehicles; sarcasm and irony detection — why incongruity plus context plus audience knowledge makes it hard, datasets and performance ceilings, and pragmatic markers; multimodal posts — image-text memes, video and audio content — with cross-modal alignment at working level and OCR/captioning bridges; ephemeral formats (stories, live streams) and their data absence; platform-specific artifacts — quote-tweets as commentary layers, duets and stitches as response structures; annotation with cultural informants and community review; and the LLM-era options for vernacular handling with their own biases.

**Absorbed Skill:** Handle slang drift, code-switching, and dialect variation without shipping dialect-biased models, incorporate emoji and hashtag semantics appropriately, evaluate and apply irony detection with awareness of its difficulty ceiling, approach multimodal posts with working cross-modal methods, and build annotation protocols with culturally competent labelers.

**Section 9 — Data Acquisition: APIs, Archives, Vendors, and Ethics**

**Summary:** Teaches getting the text legally and reliably: the platform-API landscape and its erosion — the X/Twitter API pricing history, Reddit's third-party shutdown, TikTok's Research API constraints — and what each access tier actually provides; commercial archive vendors (Brandwatch, Meltwater, Talkwalker, Sprinklr) versus build-your-own collection — coverage, history depth, cost, and query-language realities; scraping — technical fundamentals, robots.txt and terms-of-service analysis, the hiQ-versus-LinkedIn legal arc, and platform countermeasures; research corpora and public datasets — the Pushshift legacy and its loss, academic data-access programs, and dataset staleness; search and trace data — Google Trends mechanics and quirks (relative scaling, sampling variability, geo aggregation, the API and its limits), Wikipedia pageviews as behavioral traces, app-store reviews, and job postings; data quality engineering — deduplication of copy-paste cascades, bot and spam filtering at overview, and volume-inflation awareness; ethics and compliance — GDPR/CCPA duties on public text, consent debates in internet research, AoIR guidelines, minimization and aggregation practices; and pipeline engineering — scheduled incremental collection, storage formats, and failure recovery.

**Absorbed Skill:** Evaluate and combine acquisition channels (APIs, vendors, archives, scraping, public traces) with legal and ToS literacy, work with Google Trends and pageview data accounting for their measurement quirks, engineer collection pipelines with deduplication and bot-aware filtering, operate within ethical and privacy frameworks for public text data, and make build-versus-buy decisions for social listening infrastructure.

**Section 10 — Trend Detection in Text Streams**

**Summary:** Covers the core operational task — spotting what is rising: trend defined for text streams — volume bursts, emerging terms and entities, topic share shifts, and sentiment shifts as distinct detectable signals; burst detection methods — Kleinberg's state-machine model, moving z-scores, CUSUM, and seasonal baselines controlling weekday, holiday, and platform-event cycles; novel-term detection — out-of-vocabulary tracking, embedding-distance-from-established-vocabulary methods, and candidate validation with human review; topic tracking over streams — incremental clustering, streaming topic models, and theme-share time series; event detection and first-story detection lineage; entity-emergence tracking for brands, products, and people; velocity and acceleration metrics as trend scores; the signal-versus-cascade discipline — distinguishing genuine interest growth from single-viral-post echoes and bot amplification; alert design — thresholds, precision-recall trade-offs in monitoring products, and analyst-review loops; historical spike archives as reference classes for calibration; and the dashboard layer — presenting detection results with context and provenance.

**Absorbed Skill:** Implement burst and novelty detection on text streams with seasonal baselining, track emerging topics and entities over time, distinguish genuine signal from cascade and bot noise in volume spikes, design trend scores and alert thresholds with precision-recall trade-offs, and build monitoring dashboards for emerging language with provenance.

**Section 11 — Topic Modeling and Latent Content Structure**

**Summary:** Teaches unsupervised theme discovery: probabilistic topic models — LDA's generative story, priors, and inference intuition, choosing topic counts, and coherence metrics with their limits; NMF as the linear-algebra alternative; the modern default workflow — BERTopic (embeddings, clustering, c-TF-IDF term extraction) and its knobs; topic interpretation discipline — labeling by reading documents, not by top-word lists alone, and stability checks under reseeding; dynamic topic models and topics-over-time for trend narratives; structural topic models with metadata covariates (how topics vary by platform, geography, time); seeded and guided topic models for monitoring predefined themes; hierarchical topic structure at overview; qualitative-quantitative blending — close reading inside clusters; evaluation — coherence versus usefulness versus construct validity; common failures — stopword-driven junk topics, over-splitting, unstable assignments; and integration into trend products — theme dashboards and narrative tracking across time.

**Absorbed Skill:** Implement and tune LDA, NMF, and BERTopic workflows with coherence and stability checks, apply dynamic and structural topic models to trend-over-time questions, label and validate topics through document-level reading, diagnose common topic-model failures, and integrate theme tracking into monitoring dashboards.

**Section 12 — Entity Recognition, Linking, and Brand Monitoring**

**Summary:** Covers who and what the text is about: named entity recognition — CRF-era sequence labeling through neural NER, off-the-shelf models' weaknesses on social text (capitalization chaos, novel entities, nicknames), and fine-tuning for domain entities; entity classes that matter for trends — products, brands, people, places, creative works, events; entity linking and disambiguation against knowledge bases (Wikipedia, Wikidata) and the canonicalization problem; alias, nickname, and fan-slang handling (the "mother" phenomenon and brand nicknames as monitoring gaps); brand monitoring stacks — mention detection covering explicit tags and implicit references, share-of-voice computation and its platform-coverage biases, competitive-set definition, and campaign tagging; aspect and feature extraction from reviews feeding product intelligence; event extraction at overview; entity co-mention networks as the bridge to network analysis; person-tracking privacy boundaries — public-figure versus private-individual treatment; and build-versus-buy decisions for monitoring infrastructure.

**Absorbed Skill:** Apply and fine-tune NER for noisy social text including emerging entities and aliases, link and canonicalize entities against knowledge bases, build brand mention and share-of-voice monitoring with bias awareness, extract product and feature signals from review text, and connect entity data to co-mention network analysis within privacy boundaries.

**Section 13 — Multilingual and Cross-Lingual Trend Analysis**

**Summary:** Teaches analysis beyond English: the multilingual reality of trend work and the resource gap — high, mid, and low-resource languages and the quality gradient that follows; multilingual transformers (mBERT, XLM-R and successors) — how cross-lingual transfer works and where it silently fails; machine-translation pipelines versus native-language modeling — translation artifacts, lost slang and pragmatics, cost, and latency trade-offs; low-resource strategies — transfer learning, transliteration, and community efforts (Masakhane as the model); cross-lingual zero-shot classification and its validation requirements; language identification in code-mixed text; culture-bound emotion and sentiment concepts — why translation equivalence fails for affect terms; regional platform ecologies (Weibo, LINE, KakaoTalk, Telegram) with their access and language realities; tracking global events across languages with alignment; working with native-speaker annotators and local collaborators for validation; and the geopolitics of datasets and models — whose languages are covered and whose are not.

**Absorbed Skill:** Choose between translation-pipeline and multilingual-model architectures with resource-gap awareness, handle code-mixed text and language identification, validate cross-lingual sentiment transfer before trusting it, work with regional platform ecosystems and their access constraints, and collaborate with native-speaker annotators for multilingual ground truth.

**Section 14 — Evaluation, Annotation, Drift, and Validation**

**Summary:** Covers the discipline that makes measurements trustworthy: gold-standard construction — annotation guideline development, labeler selection and training, adjudication, and inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's and Fleiss's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) including when disagreement is signal rather than noise; dataset documentation — datasheets and data statements, provenance tracking; offline evaluation — precision/recall/F1 with macro-versus-micro choices, calibration assessment, and error analysis as primary practice; benchmark staleness and contamination in the LLM era; drift in all its forms — concept drift (words changing meaning: "based", "sick"), data drift (platform composition, format shifts, new features), and model decay with retraining triggers; online evaluation — A/B testing NLP-driven features, human-in-the-loop QA sampling, and drift dashboards; validating aggregate indices — does the sentiment or trend index track external reality (surveys, sales, searches) with lead-lag analysis; reproducibility — seeds, pinned environments, versioned models and data; and LLM-as-judge evaluation with its documented position and verbosity biases.

**Absorbed Skill:** Build annotation protocols with guideline development and agreement measurement, conduct systematic error analysis and calibration assessment, detect and respond to concept and data drift with retraining triggers, validate aggregate indices against external ground truth with lead-lag analysis, and operate reproducible evaluation regimes including bias-aware LLM-as-judge setups.

**Section 15 — Deployment, the LLM Era, and the End-to-End Trend Monitor**

**Summary:** Prepares production practice and the frontier: production NLP architecture — batch versus streaming pipelines, model serving, feature stores for text, and monitoring as the MLOps subset that matters; cost engineering — cascade designs (cheap lexicon filter, then small model, then LLM for hard cases), distillation, and batching; LLM-era shifts — in-context classification, retrieval-augmented analysis for analyst workflows, synthetic data generation with verification discipline, and agentic monitoring pipelines; what LLMs still do poorly for trend measurement — calibrated aggregate estimates, novel vernacular, temporal grounding — and the hybrid architectures that compensate; real-time trend products — dashboards, alerting, narrative summaries with provenance and uncertainty; ethics and governance — surveillance concerns in audience analysis, opinion-manipulation risks, responsible reporting of "public mood" claims, and platform-dependency risk management; the analyst's full stack synthesized — acquisition, cleaning, classification ensembles, topic and entity tracking, drift-aware evaluation, validated dashboards; and the capstone — building an end-to-end sentiment-and-trend monitor for a chosen domain, documented to professional standard with honest uncertainty communication.

**Absorbed Skill:** Architect production text analytics with batch and streaming pipelines and cost-aware model cascades, design hybrid LLM-plus-small-model systems playing to each strength, build real-time trend dashboards with provenance and uncertainty communication, navigate surveillance and manipulation ethics in audience measurement, and deliver a complete end-to-end NLP trend-monitoring system.
