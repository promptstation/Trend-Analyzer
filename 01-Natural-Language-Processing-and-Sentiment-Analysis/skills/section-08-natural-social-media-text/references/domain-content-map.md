2. Evaluate and apply irony detection with awareness of its difficulty ceiling, approach multimodal posts with working cross-modal methods.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the domain realities that break naive pipelines: platform vernaculars and slang dynamics — documenting drift through community sources, the speed of semantic turnover, and why static vocabularies decay; dialect and code-switching — AAVE and other dialects in social text, documented model bias against non-standard dialects (Blodgett-lineage findings), and fair-evaluation practices; emojis, emoticons, and stickers as sentiment and pragmatics carriers with platform-specific meanings; hashtags as simultaneous topics, speech acts, and irony vehicles; sarcasm and irony detection — why incongruity plus context plus audience knowledge makes it hard, datasets and performance ceilings, and pragmatic markers; multimodal posts — image-text memes, video and audio content — with cross-modal alignment at working level and OCR/captioning bridges; ephemeral formats (stories, live streams) and their data absence; platform-specific artifacts — quote-tweets as commentary layers, duets and stitches as response structures; annotation with cultural informants and community review; and the LLM-era options for vernacular handling with their own biases.
