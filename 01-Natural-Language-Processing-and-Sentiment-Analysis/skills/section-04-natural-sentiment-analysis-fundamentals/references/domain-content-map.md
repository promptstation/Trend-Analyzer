## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the field's namesake task in depth: sentiment defined and decomposed — polarity, subjectivity, intensity — and the crucial three-way distinction between sentiment, emotion, and stance; lexicon-based methods — VADER for social text, SentiWordNet, NRC EmoLex, and licensed instruments like LIWC — with their assumptions and failure modes (negation, intensifiers, domain shift, sarcasm blindness); supervised sentiment classifiers and their annotation dependencies; granularity levels — document, sentence, and clause; the human-agreement ceiling and why disagreement is sometimes signal; domain dependence — "sick", "unpredictable", and "cold" flipping valence across domains; aggregating micro-level scores into population mood indices and the ecological-fallacy risks; famous claims audited — the Facebook emotional-cont contagion study controversy and Twitter-mood-predicts-markets claims evaluated against replication; validating a sentiment instrument against external ground truth (surveys, sales); and building a calibrated sentiment-monitoring product with honest uncertainty statements.
