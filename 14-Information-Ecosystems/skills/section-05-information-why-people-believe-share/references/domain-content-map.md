## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the individual-level mechanisms: the accuracy-motivation findings — Pennycook-Rand's documented result that most sharing of misinformation stems from inattention to accuracy rather than partisan reasoning (people can discern truth when prompted but don't apply it while sharing), with effect sizes and replication status; the reasoning debates — motivated reasoning and identity-protective cognition (Kahan lineage) versus classical-reasoning accounts, and the current integrative picture (both operate, context-dependent); belief versus sharing distinguished — the documented gap (sharing without believing, believing without sharing) requiring separate measurement; the cognitive mechanisms — illusory truth (repetition increasing perceived truth, one of the most robust findings), fluency effects, confirmation and myside biases, and the continued-influence effect (misinformation persisting in reasoning after correction); emotional drivers — high-arousal emotions (anger, fear, awe) increasing sharing per the virality research, with misinformation's emotional composition advantages (novelty, disgust, outrage); the conspiracy-mindset literature — the epistemic (need for certainty), existential (control needs), and social (belonging) functions conspiracy beliefs serve, and their non-pathological distribution; susceptibility variance — who is most susceptible (the finding that extremes of the political spectrum and heavy platform users show higher exposure and sharing, with older-adult sharing patterns documented in the 2016 election research), and digital-literacy moderators; the correction psychology — debunking best practices (leading with facts, warning before mentioning myths, explaining fallacies) and the backfire-effect evidence status (rare in well-designed studies but familiarity-effect risks real); inoculation and prebunking — the attitudinal-inoculation lineage through current platform-scale prebunking interventions with documented modest effects; belief-change timescales — why ecosystem-level belief shifts lag information correction by months-years; and the analyst's application — designing communication and interpreting audiences with psychology-grounded expectations rather than information-deficit assumptions.
