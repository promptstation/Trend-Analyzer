## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the audience-side capacity layer: media literacy defined — the competencies for accessing, analyzing, evaluating, and creating media, with the shift from protectionist models toward critical-empowerment frameworks; the evidence on literacy interventions — documented effects of training programs (modest, decaying, transfer-limited per the meta-analytic record), the one-shot-workshop illusion versus sustained-curriculum findings, and lateral-reading training (the Stanford History Education Group findings on how fact-checkers actually read) as the most evidence-supported specific skill; prebunking at scale — platform-delivered inoculation interventions (documented trials with small-but-real effects), their design principles (forewarning, weak-dose exposure, refutation), and decay requiring repetition; debunking practice standards — the correction design from section 5 deployed institutionally (fact-check organizations, platform labels) with reach and effectiveness measurement; community resilience — the ecosystem-level capacities: correction communities (Community Notes contributors, fact-check networks), journalistic verification desks, and informal debunking cultures as distributed immune systems; trust calibration — the goal of appropriate (not maximal or minimal) trust in sources, and source-transparency initiatives (trust indicators, the Trust Project lineage, NewsGuard-style ratings) with their own bias controversies; platform-design defenses — friction interventions (share-without-reading prompts with documented small effects, virality circuit breakers), their paternalism debates, and effectiveness evidence; demographic realities — literacy and susceptibility patterns by age, education, and digital experience requiring differentiated defenses (older-adult sharing interventions, youth critical-creation skills); the analyst's own literacy — verification discipline, source evaluation, and cognitive-bias awareness as professional requirements (the bias-course connection); and the resilience assessment — measuring an ecosystem's defense capacity (correction infrastructure, trust calibration, literacy levels, design defenses) as a structural variable.
