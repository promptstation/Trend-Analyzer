## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the state-governance layer: the regulatory landscape — the EU framework in depth (DSA's tiered obligations for platforms including VLOPs: risk assessments for systemic risks including civic discourse and public health, transparency duties, researcher access, and the disinformation Code of Practice as voluntary-to-regulatory evolution), alongside the US approach (Section 230 immunity debates and its reform proposals, FTC action on deceptive practices, state laws) and other jurisdictions' models (UK Online Safety Act's risk-based duties, authoritarian-context information laws as the control-model contrast); election-integrity measures — the specific regime around elections (political-ad transparency, candidate-protection policies, crisis protocols) with documented effectiveness variance; the platform-liability question — immunity-versus-duty frameworks and their ecosystem-shaping effects on moderation investment; enforcement realities — DSA supervision structures, fine exposure, and the compliance-industry emergence; free-expression tensions — regulation's misuse risks (government pressure on platforms to remove legitimate speech, documented in multiple jurisdictions) as the standing trade-off; AI-content regulation — synthetic-media disclosure requirements (deepfake labeling laws, the AI Act's transparency provisions) as the emerging frontier; media-policy responses — public-broadcast funding, local-news support mechanisms (documented experiments), and platform-news-payment deals (the Australia code's precedent and its aftermath) addressing the economic layer; international coordination — the Christchurch Call lineage, G7 rapid-response mechanisms, and their soft-law limits; the analyst's regulatory monitoring — tracking implementation (delegated acts, guidance, enforcement decisions) as the operative layer beyond headlines; and the policy-effect assessment — evaluating which interventions measurably changed ecosystem behavior versus symbolic compliance.
