## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the analyst's measurement practice: the measurement targets — misinformation volume and reach (the documented difficulty: platform-data access limits, sampling approaches, and the exposure-estimation debates), community structure (chamber mapping from section 8), correction infrastructure capacity, trust levels (survey programs: Reuters Digital News Report, Edelman, national surveys), and narrative flows (what stories move between communities); data sources and their politics — platform APIs and their lockdown history, data-donation and browser-extension panels (the privacy-compliant access route), third-party monitoring (listening tools, media monitors), fact-check corpora (the ClaimReview standard enabling aggregated fact-check data), and event datasets (GDELT lineage) with each source's coverage and bias profile; the claim-tracking pipeline — from detection (flagged content, anomaly alerts) through verification (section 6 methods) to lineage tracking (section 4 lifecycle monitoring) as the operational chain; narrative monitoring — tracking story-level entities (claims, frames, actors) across sources with NLP methods (the course-1 integration), topic-modeling for narrative emergence, and stance detection for position mapping; dashboard design — the ecosystem-health dashboard combining volume, reach, community, trust, and correction indicators with alert thresholds; brand and category exposure monitoring — the commercial application: tracking misinformation affecting specific brands, products, or trend categories with sentiment and claim verification; research collaboration models — working with academic partners and platform research programs for privileged data access with ethics requirements; measurement honesty — reporting what is measurable (observed platform data) versus what is not (total exposure, private messaging — the dark-social problem where WhatsApp/Telegram circulation is structurally unobservable) with appropriate uncertainty; the monitoring cadence — continuous automated tracking with periodic deep assessments; and the integration standard — ecosystem measurement feeding the trend stack as the signal-quality layer qualifying all other data.
