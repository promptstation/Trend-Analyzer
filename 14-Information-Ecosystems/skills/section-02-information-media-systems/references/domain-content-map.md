## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the structural layer: classical media-system theory — gatekeeping (the editorial filtering lineage from Lewin through White), agenda-setting (McCombs-Shaw: media salience transferring to public salience, with the platform-era evolution toward algorithmic and networked agenda-building), and framing as the interpretive layer (connecting to the attention course); media-system comparison — the Hallin-Mancini typology (liberal, democratic-corporatist, polarized-pluralist models) explaining cross-national differences in trust, politicization, and state roles, essential because ecosystem behavior varies fundamentally by country; the legacy media economy — advertising-revenue collapse since the 2000s (documented newsroom shrinkage, local-news deserts' measured civic consequences), and the subscription/patronage pivot's partial compensation; the platform intermediation shift — news discovery moving to feeds and search, platforms' traffic dependence-and-decoupling dynamics (the documented referral declines), and publishers' platform-strategy churn; digital-native media — the new producer class (newsletters, YouTube news channels, TikTok explainers, podcasts) with different economics, standards, and audience relationships; the hybrid consumption reality — audiences mixing legacy brands, platform-native sources, and interpersonal channels, forbidding simple generational-war framing (the meta-pitfall named); journalism-norms variance — verification, sourcing, and correction standards as ecosystem quality infrastructure, and their uneven presence across the new producer class; state and partisan media — public broadcasters, state-aligned outlets, and partisan ecosystems as structural actors with distinct trust profiles; the media-balance trend consequences — where trends originate now (platform-native discovery, creator amplification, legacy validation as the second wave), and what legacy coverage still signals (institutional legitimacy transfer); and the ecosystem-structure map — charting a country's or niche's producer landscape, channel shares, and trust geography as the analyst's structural baseline.
