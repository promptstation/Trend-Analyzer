## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the ecosystem frame: information ecosystems defined — the connected system of producers (journalists, creators, institutions, anonymous accounts), channels (legacy media, platforms, messaging apps), consumers, and feedback loops through which information circulates in a society, with media-ecology lineage (McLuhan's medium-shapes-message premise through platform-ecology thinking) as the theoretical root; the course's orienting claim — trends do not move through markets directly but through information ecosystems, so the health, structure, and distortions of those ecosystems determine which trends rise, how fast, and with what meaning; the trend-analyst's stakes — misinformation distorts demand signals (false product claims, fabricated scandals), echo chambers fragment audiences (the same trend reading differently across communities), and media-balance shifts change where trends originate and amplify (legacy media's agenda-setting weakened, platform-native discovery dominant); the information-disorder taxonomy introduced — misinformation (false but not deliberately harmful), disinformation (deliberate), and malinformation (genuine information weaponized) per Wardle-Derakhshan, the vocabulary precision the entire field requires; ecosystem components mapped — sources, gatekeepers, amplifiers, audiences, and correction mechanisms as the functional anatomy; the current condition — documented trust decline in institutions and media across many countries (Edelman/Reuters-lineage data), platform-dependency of news discovery, and the AI-content flood as the newest structural shift; the speed-accuracy trade-off — viral dynamics favoring speed over verification as the ecosystem's core pathology; scope discipline — the course covers circulation, belief, community-formation, and governance, not platform engineering (course 13) though they connect; the ethics frame — analysis that fights distortion without becoming surveillance; and the course map from media systems and misinformation science through psychology, detection, operations, and governance to ecosystem measurement and the capstone.
