## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers community-level information segregation: the constructs distinguished rigorously — echo chambers (social homophily plus active distrust-and-exclusion of outside sources, identity-protective dynamics) versus filter bubbles (algorithmic personalization narrowing exposure) versus epistemic bubbles (mere insulation through omission), with Ngo's and Nguyen's philosophical distinctions (chambers actively discredit outsiders; bubbles merely omit them) as the analytic upgrade; formation mechanisms — homophilous selection (people choose similar others and sources), social influence (opinions converging within networks), algorithmic personalization (the course-13 mechanisms), and identity entrepreneurship (elites and media rewarding in-group loyalty) as the four documented drivers; the evidence status — exposure studies finding most social-media users encounter more cross-cutting content than assumed while small segments consume highly partisan diets, chambers concentrated among the politically engaged, and the affective-polarization literature documenting rising hostility independent of exposure volume; measurement methods — segregation indices for information networks, source-overlap analysis across communities, cross-cutting-exposure measurement from panel data, and community-detection on sharing networks (the network-course toolset applied); chamber dynamics — internal radicalization processes (group polarization, trust-hierarchy formation, alternative-epistemic-institution building: own experts, own media, own evidence standards); chamber-external dynamics — the mistrust spiral, cross-chamber communication failures, and the debunking-rejection pattern explained by chamber membership; platform architecture effects — how group features, recommendation systems, and moderation choices enable or inhibit chamber formation; chamber lifecycle — formation, hardening, mainstreaming of chamber narratives, and fracture events; intervention evidence — bridging-exposure experiments showing backfire risks (the documented counter-attitudinal exposure polarization finding), making naive exposure prescriptions unsafe; and the analyst's chamber mapping — charting the information communities relevant to a trend domain with their sources, trust structures, and narrative boundaries.
