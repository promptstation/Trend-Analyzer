## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the goal function as trend determinant: the objective stack — business goals (revenue, retention, growth) decomposed into proxy metrics (watch time, session depth, return rate) and model objectives (engagement predictions), with the documented gaps between layers; engagement optimization and its critics — the finding that engagement proxies reward arousing, divisive, and sensational content (the Facebook Files disclosures as the case study, with interpretation disputes), and platform counter-measures (satisfaction surveys, quality classifiers, diversification mandates); watch-time supremacy — YouTube's 2012 metric shift and its documented content-ecosystem effects (longer videos, retention engineering) as the canonical objective-change case; TikTok's completion-and-rewatch emphasis — short-form objectives producing different content selection (instant hooks, loop-friendly formats) per disclosures and observed behavior; recommendation diversity objectives — stated goals of variety and serendipity versus observed narrowing, and the filter-bubble debate's evidence status (exposure studies showing mixed results); the value-alignment problem — user welfare versus engagement versus revenue as competing objectives, with 'worthwhile watch' style reweightings as documented partial corrections; objective opacity — why platforms keep formulas secret (gaming resistance, competitive advantage), and the DSA obligation to disclose main ranking parameters as the transparency counterweight; creator-side optimization — how understanding objectives reshapes content supply (the algorithmic-chase dynamic), producing homogenization and format convergence observable as trend effects; objective drift — platforms silently reweighting objectives and the ecosystem whiplash that follows; and the analyst's objective inference — combining disclosures, behavior probing, and creator-economy evidence to build working models of what a platform currently rewards.
