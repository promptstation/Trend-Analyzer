## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the self-reinforcing dynamics: feedback loops formalized — recommenders trained on data generated by their own past recommendations (selection bias, exposure bias), producing degeneracy dynamics where initial advantages compound; the popularity feedback loop — rich-get-richer amplification through ranking (popularity as feature plus engagement accumulation), its measured effects on content diversity, and platform debiasing techniques (inverse propensity weighting, popularity calibration); the interest-narrowing loop — personalization reinforcing observed preferences, reducing exposure diversity over time, and the evidence on actual narrowing (mixed findings with measurement challenges); echo chambers versus filter bubbles revisited — chambers as socially constructed homophily (network course) versus bubbles as algorithmically constructed isolation, their interaction in recommender systems, and the current empirical consensus (algorithmic personalization's isolation effect smaller than popularly claimed but nonzero and variable); rabbit-hole dynamics — sequential recommendation paths pulling users toward extremes (the YouTube radicalization claims and their mixed empirical record), with autoplay and up-next as mechanism candidates; loop breaking — platform interventions (exploration budgets, diversity injection, reset controls) and user-side strategies (feed controls, fresh accounts) with documented effectiveness; measurement methods — detecting loops through longitudinal exposure tracking, simulation studies, and audit experiments; simulation as lab — agent-based and counterfactual simulation of recommender ecosystems as the research approach for loop dynamics that cannot be ethically A/B tested on users; the trend consequence — feedback loops explaining why platform trends accelerate, homogenize, and crash faster than organic dynamics predict; and the analyst's loop literacy — accounting for algorithmic self-reinforcement when interpreting observed trend curves.
