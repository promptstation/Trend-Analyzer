## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the outside-in methodology: algorithmic auditing defined — systematic external measurement of algorithmic behavior, with the audit question taxonomy (what content gets recommended, to whom, in what order, with what sensitivity to features); sock-puppet methodology — controlled synthetic accounts with designed profiles (interests, demographics, behavior patterns) receiving recommendations as treatment-response data, the field's dominant method, with ethics and terms-of-service considerations; mystery shopping — human-auditor account protocols, and the panel-audit design (many auditors, standardized scripts) reducing individual variance; sensitivity analysis — varying one input at a time (engagement history, follow graph, search queries, location settings) to isolate ranking factors; feed-capture engineering — data collection from personalized feeds (screen recording, accessibility APIs, data-donation extensions like RegretsReporter lineage) with consent and scale trade-offs; recommendation-explanation probing — using platform-provided explanations ('because you watched X') as disclosed-feature evidence; creator-side intelligence — systematic creator-economy experiments (posting variants, analytics reading) as crowd-sourced reverse engineering, and its selection biases; observational network methods — inferring amplification from aggregate data (co-recommendation patterns, cascade shapes) without direct feed access; the replication problem — audits measure one moment of a continuously-retrained, A/B-tested system, requiring repeated measures and confidence statements; inference discipline — from observed feed differences to algorithmic claims: confound control (time, device, account age), baseline comparison (popularity-free exploration cohorts), and the limits of n-small audits; documentation standards — audit protocols, raw captures, analysis code, and uncertainty statements as the professional deliverable; and the method selection matrix — matching audit questions to methods by access level, cost, and inference strength.
