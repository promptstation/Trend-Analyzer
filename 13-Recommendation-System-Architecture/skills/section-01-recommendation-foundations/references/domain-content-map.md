## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the domain and its centrality to trend analysis: recommender systems defined — software that predicts and selects items (videos, posts, products, topics) for individual users from vast candidate pools, and the course's orienting claim: in platform-mediated culture, recommendation systems are the primary allocation mechanism of attention, making them the machinery that decides which content becomes a trend; the trend-analysis stake — what organic network spread (course 2) and memetic dynamics (course 5) describe happens inside algorithmic curation, so an analyst who cannot reason about ranking systems systematically misreads amplification as virality and personalization as popularity; the classical taxonomy previewed — content-based filtering (item features matched to user profiles), collaborative filtering (behavior-similarity patterns), knowledge-based (explicit constraints), and hybrid combinations, with modern systems as deep hybrids; the recommendation problem formalized — users, items, interactions, and the prediction task (rating, engagement probability, ranking) as the underlying structure; scale context — billions of daily users, millions of candidate items, millisecond latency budgets, and continuous retraining as the engineering constraints shaping every design choice; platform varieties — feeds (TikTok FYP, Instagram, X), search-adjacent (YouTube results, Google Discover), commerce (Amazon), and interest graphs, each with different objectives and signals; the black-box reality — proprietary ranking systems with selective official disclosures, requiring the reverse-engineering and auditing methods taught later; the two-perspective method — understanding systems from inside (architecture knowledge) and from outside (behavioral probing), with the course covering both; ethics and governance previewed — amplification responsibilities, DSA transparency duties, and researcher-access debates; and the course map from data and classical algorithms through deep architectures, objectives, platform specifics, and auditing to transparency governance and capstone algorithmic intelligence.
