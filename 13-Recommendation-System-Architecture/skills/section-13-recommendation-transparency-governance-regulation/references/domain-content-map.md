## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the governance layer shaping what analysts can know: platform disclosures — the official explainers (TikTok's For-You documentation, YouTube's recommendation guidance, Instagram's ranking explanations) as primary sources with their simplification and selective-disclosure limits; the DSA regime — EU Digital Services Act obligations including main-ranking-parameters disclosure for VLOPs, user-facing option to modify ranking, ad repositories, and researcher data-access provisions, with compliance practice and its gaps; researcher access — platform API programs, data-donation infrastructures, and the documented tension between legitimate research access and privacy/scraping restrictions (the API-lockdown waves); audit regulation — emerging expectations for independent algorithmic audits and their methodological standardization debates; transparency versus gaming — the design dilemma that disclosed ranking logic invites manipulation, explaining partial disclosure as strategic equilibrium; ranking-choice provisions — chronologic-feed options and preference sliders as user-control mechanisms with uptake evidence; ad-transparency systems — ad libraries as public-interest recommendation data; recommender-impact reporting — platforms' periodic transparency reports and their disclosure limits; cross-jurisdiction variation — EU, US state laws, and Asian regimes' differing approaches to algorithmic transparency; the governance trend line — disclosure obligations tightening over time, improving the analyst's information environment incrementally; and the compliant-intelligence posture — building platform understanding through official channels, permitted research, and ethical auditing rather than ToS-violating scraping, with the legal-risk map.
