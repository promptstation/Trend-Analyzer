## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Dissects the two canonical systems: TikTok FYP — the disclosed model (candidate generation from multiple sources ranked by weighted engagement predictions emphasizing completion, rewatch, shares; interest-graph-first design where content quality can beat follower count), the interest-graph versus social-graph distinction as TikTok's structural innovation, its cold-start pipeline (initial impression budgets for every new video, velocity-based escalation), sound/audio-centric recommendation (audio as item entity enabling format trends), and the documented behavioral signatures (rapid interest learning, niche-community surfacing, trend acceleration); YouTube — the two-stage architecture from the 2016 disclosure updated by later statements, watch-time-centric objectives with satisfaction reweightings, the subscription-feed versus recommendations split, Shorts' separate-but-bridged pipeline, search-recommendation interaction, and the creator-economy feedback (thumbnail-title optimization as ranking-response); comparative analysis — objective differences producing content-ecosystem differences (TikTok's completion focus favoring short loops, YouTube's watch time favoring retention arcs), cold-start philosophy differences, and trend-velocity differences; Instagram Reels and X as secondary cases — borrowed architectures with graph-integration variations; Google Discover at overview — the feed-style recommendation in search ecosystems; common patterns extracted — multi-stage funnels, engagement blending, exploration budgets, and policy filters as the shared skeleton; platform-specific trend mechanics — how each system's design shapes which trends emerge there first and how they migrate (the TikTok-to-Instagram pipeline as documented pattern); and the case-study method — building working models of specific platforms from disclosures, creator-community knowledge, and behavioral probing.
