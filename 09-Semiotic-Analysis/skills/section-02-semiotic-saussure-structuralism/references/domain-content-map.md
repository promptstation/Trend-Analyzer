## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the first founding tradition in depth: the Saussurean sign — signifier (sound/image pattern) and signified (concept) as two sides of one psychic entity, their bond arbitrary, and meaning arising negatively through difference within a system rather than positively from reference; langue versus parole — the underlying system versus individual utterances, and why semioticians analyze systems first (a single aesthetic post is parole; the aesthetic's conventions are langue); value — the concept that a sign's worth is its position in a system of alternatives (why 'cottagecore' means what it does partly by not being 'goblincore' or 'dark academia'); the structuralist method — analyzing cultural phenomena as systems of oppositions and differences; binary oppositions — culture's organizing pairs (nature/culture, raw/cooked in Lévi-Strauss, masculine/feminine, authentic/artificial) and their documented instability; syntagm and paradigm — the two axes of meaning: combination (what signs appear together in sequence — an outfit, a feed, a room) and substitution (what signs could replace each other — the choice set that reveals values), the single most useful analytic pair for practical semiotics; commutation testing — swapping elements to isolate meaning contributions; synchronic versus diachronic analysis — system snapshots versus historical change, with trend analysis requiring both; structuralism's legacy and limits — its power for systematic comparison and its blind spots (agency, audiences, history, power) motivating post-structuralist updates; the myth-preview — Barthes extending Saussure to culture at large (next section); and practice — describing an aesthetic as a sign system: its paradigm inventory (the choice sets) and syntagmatic patterns (the combinations).
