## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the bridge from linguistics to cultural analysis: Barthes' orders of signification — first order (denotation: literal description) and second order (connotation: cultural meanings attaching), demonstrated through his 'Rhetoric of the Image' analysis of the pasta advertisement (the Italianicity reading); myth defined — Barthes' term for connotation naturalized: cultural meanings that come to feel like facts of nature (steak-as-Frenchness, white-as-purity), with myth as the mechanism by which history passes into nature; myth analysis in practice — his Mythologies method of reading everyday cultural objects (toys, wrestling, advertisements) as ideological sign systems; the linguistics message versus coded iconic message versus non-coded iconic message — his three-message model of the image, and anchorage/relay as the text-image relation mechanisms (captions anchoring meaning, comics relaying it); the Fashion System — his analysis of fashion magazines as a written code manufacturing desire through language, foundational for fashion semiotics; connotation as trend substance — the analytic claim that trends move at the connotative layer (the same garment means different things across seasons), making connotation tracking the core trend-semiotic skill; naturalization and denaturalization — spotting where culture poses as nature (beauty standards, 'timeless' design, 'classic' taste) and reversing the pose analytically; ideology critique — semiotics revealing whose interests naturalized meanings serve, with the caveat that Barthesian readings are interpretations requiring evidence and humility; post-Barthes developments — Eco's open work and reader participation, and the encoding/decoding reminder that myths can be read oppositionally; contemporary myth inventory — current naturalized myths relevant to trends (hustle culture, 'clean' aesthetics, authenticity-as-performance); and practice — writing a Mythologies-style analysis of a contemporary trend artifact.
