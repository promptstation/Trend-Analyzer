## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Applies analysis to commercial communication: the advertisement as dense semiotic artifact — Barthes' pioneering analysis updated for the current media environment (social ads, creator content, branded entertainment); campaign analysis method — corpus assembly across executions, the campaign's core sign system (key visual, palette, casting, setting, music), and consistency as syntagmatic discipline; narrative structures in ads — the actantial roles brands claim (helper, donor, sometimes subject), transformation narratives (before/after as mini-myth), and slice-of-life versus surreal registers; visual rhetoric figures — metaphor, metonymy, hyperbole, and litotes in imagery (the Groupe Mu lineage), with worked examples of each in trend-relevant ads; copy-text analysis — headline codes, voice registers (confessional, authoritative, ironic), and the anchorage function in multimodal ads; casting and representation semiotics — who appears and how as ideological signs, the diversity-representation debates as semiotic-political contests, and tokenism detection; music and sonic branding in campaigns — song choice as connotation import (sync licensing as meaning purchase); influencer-content semiotics — branded posts' authenticity codes (disclosure labels as institutional signs, the native-versus-ads aesthetic tension); social-campaign genres — the challenge format, the duet response, meme-hijack campaigns as participatory syntax; semiotic effectiveness questions — whether campaigns' encoded meanings match decoded readings (testing methods, audience research integration); failure analysis — campaigns whose signs misfired (context-blind symbol use, tone-deaf myth deployment) as diagnostic cases; and practice — full campaign semiotic audits with meaning-intent versus likely-reading assessments.
