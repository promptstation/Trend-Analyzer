## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches systematic image analysis: Kress-Van Leeuwen's social-semiotic grammar — composition (information value of positions: left-given versus right-new, top-ideal versus bottom-real, center-margin salience), salience (what draws attention via size, contrast, color, framing), and framing (connectives and separators joining or dividing elements); the visual modality system — how images claim reality (color depth, illumination, contextualization, representation) and the hyper-real and abstract-real registers, crucial for aesthetic analysis (why cottagecore's soft-focus signals nostalgia-ideal rather than documentary-real); color semiotics — cultural color codes (white-purity/mourning variance across cultures), palette systems as aesthetic signatures, and color trend cycles; typography semiotics — typeface connotations (serif tradition, sans modernity, display playfulness) and typographic codes in meme formats; composition traditions — the photographic and painterly conventions images invoke (portrait versus landscape orientation, close-up intimacy, wide-shot context) as intertextual codes; the reading-path analysis — how compositions sequence viewer attention; interface-visual convergence — UI patterns as coded visual language (cards, feeds, dark mode); moving-image basics — shot grammar, editing rhythm, and transition codes as visual syntax in video content; multimodal ensembles — image-text-sound combinations and their interaction principles; the analysis output — visual grammar breakdowns supporting design critique and trend decoding; and practice — analyzing trend-relevant imagery (fashion editorials, moodboards, product photography, TikTok frames) with the full grammar toolkit.
