## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the second founding tradition: the triadic sign — representamen (the sign vehicle), object (what it stands for), and interpretant (the sense made of it), meaning as a three-way relation rather than a two-way bond; the icon/index/symbol typology in depth — icons signify by resemblance (portraits, maps, skeuomorphic interfaces, aesthetic moodboards), indices by causal-existential connection (smoke/fire, footprints, the photograph's indexical trace, 'authentic' wear-and-tear as index of use), symbols by convention (words, logos, flags, most fashion codes) — with mixed-mode signs as the norm (a product photo is iconic and indexical and symbolically styled); interpretant dynamics — immediate, dynamic, and final interpretants, and the practical point that meaning lives in effects on interpreters, not in signs; unlimited semiosis — every interpretant becomes a new sign, generating chains of association, which is exactly what aesthetic moodboards and brand worlds exploit; Peirce's other trichotomies at overview (qualisign/sinsign/legisign, rheme/dicent/argument) with working-level use; abduction — Peirce's inference mode (hypothesis generation from surprising signs) as the logic of trend interpretation; indexicality in digital culture — the platform-era obsession with indexical authenticity (unfiltered photos, 'photo dumps', live video as index-of-the-real) analyzed as semiotic strategy; icon-index-symbol ratios as analytic tools — comparing sign systems by dominant mode (luxury branding's symbolic density versus streetwear's indexical wear-signals); the Saussure-Peirce complementarity — systems-of-difference versus chains-of-interpretation, and using both; and practice — coding a trend's visual corpus by sign mode and tracing interpretant chains.
