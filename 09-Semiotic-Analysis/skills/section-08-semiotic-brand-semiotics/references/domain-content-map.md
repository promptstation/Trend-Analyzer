## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the commercial application: the brand as sign system — brand names, logos, colors, typography, packaging, sonic branding, mascots, and spatial design as an integrated semiotic architecture; logo semiotics — icon/index/symbol analysis of marks, logotype connotations, and the redesign genre (simplification waves, the 'blanding' phenomenon documented across tech branding, nostalgia reversion cycles) as trend evidence; packaging as semiotic medium — shelf syntax (the paradigm of competing packages), material signifiers (glass-as-craft, matte-as-premium), label codes (typography hierarchies, certification badges as indexical trust signs), and unboxing as ritual sequence; brand worlds and universes — campaign mythology, consistent code deployment across touchpoints, and world-building as extended syntagm (the luxury maison's total aesthetic); symbolic positioning — brands occupying positions in cultural meaning spaces (semiotic square application: mass/premium, traditional/innovative, natural/technical), with whitespace strategy; brand-code evolution — when and how brands shift codes (repositioning) and the semiotic costs of inconsistency; sonic and sensory semiotics — audio logos, texture, and scent as non-visual sign channels; the authenticity semiotics — indexical strategies brands deploy (heritage dates, founder signatures, craft-process imagery, imperfection-as-real) and their detection-of-faking dynamics; semiotic brand audits — the professional method: inventorying a brand's sign system, assessing code consistency, connotative alignment with strategy, and competitive differentiation; competitor semiotic mapping — positioning the category's sign space; and the trend application — reading brand semiotic shifts across an industry as a leading indicator of meaning migration (when many brands adopt the same palette or typography, a connotative trend is mainstreaming).
