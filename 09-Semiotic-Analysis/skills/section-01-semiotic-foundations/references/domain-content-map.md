## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Define signs, codes, and meaning-making as analytic objects across media.
2. Distinguish denotation from connotation in observed cultural material.
3. Apply the encoding/decoding model with awareness that analyst readings are one among many.
4. Articulate the arbitrariness principle as the reason meanings shift and trends move, and frame trend questions as meaning-migration problems solvable by sign analysis.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the discipline and its trend-analysis mission: semiotics defined — the systematic study of signs and meaning-making, where a sign is anything standing for something else to somebody in some capacity; the trend-analysis case — trends are meaning migrations: aesthetics, products, and behaviors rise when their sign-values fit emerging cultural needs (cottagecore signifying escape, cyberpunk signifying tech-anxiety), so decoding signs decodes trends before metrics confirm them; the sign vehicle catalog — words, images, objects, colors, garments, spaces, gestures, sounds, interfaces, and behaviors as meaning carriers; the two founding traditions previewed — Saussure's dyadic linguistically-modeled sign (signifier/signified within systems of difference) and Peirce's triadic logic-based sign (representamen/object/interpretant with icon/index/symbol modes), both developed in later sections; denotation and connotation introduced as the working distinction (literal reference versus cultural association) with Barthes' myth concept previewed; codes — the shared cultural rulebooks making signs interpretable, and code competence as audience-dependent; the arbitrariness principle — most sign-meaning links are conventional, hence variable across cultures and time, hence trackable as trends; encoding and decoding — Hall's model that producers encode and audiences decode with negotiated and oppositional readings, warning analysts that their decoding is one reading among many; analysis versus impression — the method promise that semiotics converts 'this gives me a vibe' into structured, defensible, replicable meaning analysis; the applied landscape — brand semiotics, cultural research, design analysis, and trend decoding as professional uses; and the course map from the two traditions through visual, brand, aesthetic, and digital sign systems to trend-forecasting practice and capstone audits.
