## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Consolidates method into a working analytic toolkit: code taxonomy — social codes (dress, manners, ritual), textual and genre codes (narrative conventions, format rules), and interpretive codes (ideological, aesthetic) as the layered rulebooks of meaning; genre analysis — recognizing and deploying genre conventions (the unboxing video, the GRWM format, the brand film, the aesthetic moodboard) as coded forms whose conventions carry meaning; narrative analysis — Greimas' actantial model (subject/object, sender/receiver, helper/opponent) mapping story roles in ads and brand narratives, and his semiotic square (opposition, contradiction, complementarity) as a systematic meaning-space mapper; the semiotic square in practice — expanding binaries (natural/artificial) into full meaning spaces revealing unexploited positions (neither-natural-nor-artificial), a strategy tool for finding whitespace; narrative programs and canonical schema — desire, competence, performance, sanction as the story logic of brand journeys; intertextuality — signs meaning through reference to other texts (memes as dense intertextuality, parody, pastiche, homage) and the audience competence required; modalities — truth, desire, and capacity commitments signs make ('this is real', 'you want this', 'you can have this'); the analysis workflow formalized — corpus definition, denotation inventory, paradigm-syntagm mapping, code identification, connotation layering, myth reading, and synthesis; commutation and substitution testing as rigor tools; documentation standards — the semiotic analysis matrix (sign, mode, codes, denotation, connotations, myth, evidence); and the validity discipline — grounding readings in cultural context, audience evidence, and systematic comparison rather than analyst impression.
