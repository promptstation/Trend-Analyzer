## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Prepares expert practice: the complete semiotic framework consolidated — both traditions (Saussurean systems, Peircean interpretation), the Barthesian connotation-myth layer, structural methods (syntagm-paradigm, semiotic square, actantial), visual and multimodal grammars, and applied domains (brand, fashion, digital, spatial, campaign) as one integrated toolkit; the semiotic audit defined — the professional deliverable systematically analyzing a brand's, category's, or trend's sign system: corpus, inventory, code analysis, connotation mapping, myth reading, competitive meaning-space position, and strategic implications; the audit workflow — scoping (question and corpus definition), collection (multi-channel, diachronic, multi-audience sampling), layered analysis (denotation → connotation → myth → ideology), synthesis (meaning maps, territory positions), translation (strategy statements), and validation (audience checks, peer critique); cross-course integration — netnography (course 7) grounding readings in community practice, subculture analysis (course 8) situating sign systems in lifecycles, memetics (course 5) tracking sign mutation, and quantitative methods (courses 1, 3) measuring semiotic indicator series; the trend-decode brief — the recurring deliverable format for emerging aesthetics (decode sheet plus lifecycle stage plus commercial translation options); quality and validity standards — evidence grounding, contextual documentation, audience-reading humility, and the replication test (could another analyst follow the audit trail to similar conclusions); the ethics overlay — representation respect, cultural-context sensitivity, and the extraction-awareness from subculture ethics; common audit failures reviewed — vibe-reading without method, connotation-as-fact, single-channel corpora, unexamined analyst position, and strategy bridges unsupported by analysis; professional practice contexts — agency semiotics, in-house brand research, trend forecasting, and academic cultural analysis, each with format adaptations; and the capstone — a complete semiotic audit: select a trend-relevant subject (a brand, category, or emerging aesthetic), build the diachronic multi-channel corpus, execute the full layered analysis with documented matrices and meaning maps, position findings in the competitive meaning space, forecast code trajectories with scenarios, and deliver a validated audit with ethically framed strategic implications.
