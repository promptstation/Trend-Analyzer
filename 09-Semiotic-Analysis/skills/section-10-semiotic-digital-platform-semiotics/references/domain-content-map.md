## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the native sign systems of digital culture: emoji semiotics — the pictographic code with generational and cultural variation (skull-as-laughter, eggplant innuendo, the professional-versus-casual emoji registers), emoji syntax (placement, repetition, combination), and emoji trends as measurable connotation shifts; meme semiotics — templates as coded syntagms with obligatory and variable slots (the deep intertextuality requiring code competence), format conventions as genre codes, and meaning drift through mutation (connecting to memetics); interface semiotics — UI patterns as conventionalized signs (hearts for likes, swipes for decisions, infinite scroll's connotations), skeuomorphism-to-flat-to-neumorphism design waves as style cycles, and dark mode as coded aesthetic choice; platform-native genres — the TikTok video grammar (hook frames, text overlays, green-screen discourse, sound-as-signature), Stories formats, and profile-as-identity-text; algorithmic semiotics — badges and labels (verified checks, 'trending' tags) as institutional signs shaping meaning; typography of the internet — copypasta formatting, intentional lowercase, alternating-case mockery, and keystroke conventions as paralanguage; notification and sound design — auditory interface signs and their attention connotations; AI-generated content semiotics — the emerging signs of synthetic media (glitch artifacts as indices, the 'AI look' as aesthetic), and detection-semiotics as a new literacy; cross-platform code translation — how signs mutate migrating between platforms (the same aesthetic performed differently on TikTok versus Pinterest); digital semiotic velocity — platform signs evolving faster than any prior sign system, requiring continuous code updating; and practice — building a digital-semiotics monitoring brief tracking emoji, meme, interface, and genre sign shifts as trend indicators.
