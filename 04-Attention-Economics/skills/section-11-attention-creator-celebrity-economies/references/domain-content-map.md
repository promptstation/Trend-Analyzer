## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers attention embodied in people: the creator economy structure — platform funds, brand deals, subscriptions, merchandise, and how creators monetize captured attention; parasocial relationships — the psychology of one-sided bonds, their intensification on always-available platforms, and why parasocial attention converts (trust, loyalty, purchase influence); influencer marketing measurement — reach, engagement, and conversion claims versus documented inflation (fake followers, engagement pods) and the incrementality evidence; micro versus macro versus mega creators — attention quality trade-offs (niche trust versus scale), and the empirical pattern of engagement rates falling with follower count; fandoms as attention collectives — organized amplification, stan culture dynamics, and fandom-driven trend explosions; celebrity in the platform era — legacy celebrity versus platform-native fame, attention arbitrage between the two, and celebrity brand launches riding personal attention stocks; creator attention cycles — burnout, hiatus, and cancellation as attention-shock events, plus audience fatigue; creator diversification — owning audiences off-platform (newsletters, communities) as attention-asset risk management; the creator as trend instrument — creators as early adopters and amplifiers whose participation signals a trend's phase; and evaluating creator partnerships — attention quality, audience-brand fit, and manipulation-risk audits.
