## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Surveys where the attention economy is heading: the content-flood thesis — generative AI collapsing content production costs, dead-internet concerns, and what abundance does to a scarce-attention market (filter value rises, discovery breaks, synthetic content competes for the same finite hours); synthetic attention — AI-generated engagement, bot-armed amplification, and the measurement arms race between generation and detection; attention in new interfaces — ambient computing, wearables, and in-car/home audio as attention surfaces beyond screens, and spatial computing's early attention economics; personalization's endgame — hyper-individual content generation (AI-tailored media) and its implications for shared attention and mass culture; platform evolution — short-video saturation signals, format fatigue cycles, and emerging platforms' attention-grab strategies; attention measurement frontiers — neuroscientific and computer-vision attention sensing, privacy-preserving measurement (on-device, aggregated), and verification standards responding to synthetic content; regulatory trajectory — tightening rules on minors, ad transparency, and recommendation-system choice, as constraints on attention allocation; economic restructuring — subscription revival, attention co-ops, and micropayment experiments as alternatives to ad-funded capture; scenario planning for attention strategists — divergent futures (abundance-collapse, regulation-reshape, AI-mediation) and robust strategies across them; and the durable principles — scarcity of human attention persists through every interface change, so the fundamentals transfer.
