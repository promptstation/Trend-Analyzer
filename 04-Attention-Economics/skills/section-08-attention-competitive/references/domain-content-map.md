## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers attention as relative position: share of attention versus share of voice — measuring a brand's or topic's fraction of category attention rather than absolute counts, and the media-mix lineage of SOV measurement; competitive set definition — who actually competes for the same attention (category rivals, adjacent entertainment, creator substitutes), and data-driven set discovery via co-search and co-engagement; benchmarking practice — attention shares by platform, format, and period, with normalization for brand size and spend; attention and growth evidence — the empirical literature linking fame/salience growth to market-share growth (Ehrenberg-Bass lineage, Binet-Field effectiveness work on fame campaigns), read with its industry-data caveats; category attention dynamics — rising categories pulling all entrants, declining categories' attention contraction, and new-category creation as the highest-leverage attention play; competitive response patterns — how attention shocks (a rival's viral moment) propagate and when to respond versus ignore; white-space analysis — attention demand with weak supply (high search, low quality content) as opportunity mapping; share-shift early warning — detecting competitors' rising attention velocity before share moves; small-brand strategy — asymmetric attention plays (niche domination, platform-native authenticity) versus outspending; and the competitive attention report — structure, cadence, and decision hooks.
