## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the intellectual foundation: the attention-economics lineage — Simon's 1971 thesis, Franck's and Goldhaber's 1990s formulations, and Davenport-Beck's managerial synthesis, with each era's emphasis (organizational, market, managerial); Tim Wu's Attention Merchants account — the two-century business history of harvesting attention and reselling it to advertisers, from penny presses through broadcast to platforms; the free-content bargain — content as bait, audiences as product, Smythe's audience-commodity concept from critical political economy; attention and the public sphere — Habermas lineage and the shift from editorial gatekeeping to algorithmic allocation; economic models of attention — competition for a fixed budget, attention as a common-pool resource, and congestion externalities; the fame/celebrity economy as an older attention market; behavioral-economics grounding — limited cognitive bandwidth, opportunity cost of attention, and choice architecture in attention contexts; the cultural-economics bridge — how attention determines which ideas get processed, remembered, and transmitted (the selection environment for memetics, previewed in course 5); critiques of the concept — attention as catch-all metaphor, measurement reification, and the debate over whether platforms maximize attention or engagement or profit; and the theoretical toolkit for later sections — who captures attention, by what mechanism, at whose cost.
