## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the machinery that distributes attention: feed ranking fundamentals — candidate generation then ranking, with engagement-prediction objectives (likes, comments, watch time, predicted satisfaction) as documented in platform disclosures and research; the recommendation objectives — what platforms claim to optimize (relevance, satisfaction, diversity) versus what proxies incentivize (engagement, retention), including the well-documented gap; ranking signals — predicted engagement, creator quality, freshness, diversity injections — and how small objective changes redistribute massive attention; feedback loops — content that earns engagement gets amplified, changing what creators make (algorithmic shaping of supply) and what audiences see (demand shaping), with runaway homogenization versus niche-support evidence; personalization versus virality — how the same system produces both filter-bubble-like experiences and platform-wide viral moments; cold-start mechanics — how new content and creators earn initial distribution, and why early engagement velocity matters (connecting to course 13's architecture depth); transparency realities — what's knowable from official documentation, audits, and leaked materials (the TikTok algorithm coverage lineage, Facebook Files), and the epistemic limits of studying closed ranking systems; algorithmic change as exogenous shock — platform updates that instantly revalue content strategies; measurement approaches — algorithmic auditing, mystery-shopping feeds, and researcher access programs; and the analyst's operating model — treating platform algorithms as the attention economy's central bank, whose policy shifts must be monitored.
