## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the temporal dynamics of attention: the attention lifecycle — emergence, acceleration, peak, decay, and residual/dormant phases, mapped onto trend curves; news cycles and their acceleration — how the 24-hour cycle compressed to platform-native micro-cycles, and cycle length by topic type; velocity measures — growth rate of mentions/views as the earliest attention signal, acceleration versus deceleration as turning-point indicators (connecting to course 3's changepoint methods); decay patterns — exponential versus power-law forgetting, half-life estimates per topic class (news events decay in hours-days, memes in days-weeks, durable interests in months-years), and the empirics of collective memory decay; attention bursts and their predictability — the finding that most bursts are not forecastable early, with content features that correlate with longevity; recirculation — old content resurfacing (nostalgia cycles, anniversary effects, recontextualization), and dormant attention reactivating; saturation and crowding — diminishing attention returns as a topic saturates, and the late-entrant problem; seasonality of attention — weekly rhythms, holiday effects, event calendars (sports, awards, elections); cross-platform attention migration — how topics move between platforms with characteristic lags (the Reddit-to-TikTok-to-Instagram pipeline), giving analysts lead-lag signal; and the operational dashboard — velocity, acceleration, decay-rate, and saturation indicators combined into attention-momentum scoring.
