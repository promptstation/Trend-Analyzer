## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the field's core thesis: Herbert Simon's formulation that a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention, establishing attention as the binding constraint of the information age; attention defined operationally — selection, sustained focus, and depth of processing, distinguished from mere exposure and from reach; the supply-demand structure — finite human attention hours against exponentially growing content supply, and what scarcity economics predicts (bidding, intermediaries, price discovery in attention markets); the historical arc previewed — from mass-media audiences through the dot-com attention-economics moment (Goldhaber, Davenport-Beck) to platform capitalism; why trend analysts care — attention is the leading indicator of cultural and commercial momentum, moving before sales, search, and adoption; the measurement problem stated upfront — attention is latent and every metric is a proxy (impressions, views, dwell time, engagement) with known biases; the actors in the attention economy — audiences (sellers of attention), platforms (marketplaces and intermediaries), advertisers and brands (buyers), creators (suppliers of content) — and the flows between them; attention inequality — the concentration pattern where tiny fractions of content capture most attention, previewed from the power-law material of network study; and the course map from theory and measurement through platform mechanics, brand and movement attention, to the dark side and frontier.
