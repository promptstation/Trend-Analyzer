## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Extends the framework beyond commerce: agenda-setting theory — media attention determining what issues the public considers important (McCombs-Shaw lineage), and its platform-era evolution from editorial gatekeeping to algorithmic and networked agenda-building; framing — how attention within an issue is directed by interpretive frames (Entman), with frame competition as attention contest; social movements and attention — how movements manufacture attention events (protests, disruptions, symbolic acts) to enter media agendas, the attention-cycle constraints movements face, and movement success correlating with sustained attention; hashtag activism — attention aggregation mechanisms, their reach-versus-depth debates, and slacktivism critiques examined against participation evidence; political attention economies — campaign strategies for earned attention, outrage's attention advantage and its democratic externalities, and attention manipulation (astroturfing, coordinated campaigns) detection; news attention to issues — event-driven spikes versus slow-burn coverage, and issue-attention cycles (Downs' model: discovery, euphoria, realization of cost, decline); attention inequality among causes — which issues win attention and the structural biases (visual drama, elite sponsorship, celebrity attachment); measurement practice — GDELT-style event data, media-monitoring for issue attention, and protest-event analysis; counter-attention — how institutions deflect or exhaust issue attention; and the civic-analyst toolkit — tracking issue attention as context that shapes commercial trends.
