## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches how brands capture and hold attention: brand salience and mental availability — the Ehrenberg-Bass framework where brands grow by being thought of in buying situations, not by attitude shifts alone; fame as the mechanism — how widely-known, talked-about brands earn attention at lower cost, and the measurement of fame (search volume, unaided recall, social mentions); distinctive brand assets — colors, sounds, characters, formats that earn attention and encode memory without the logo, and distinctiveness versus differentiation; brand attention assets on platforms — owned channels, creator partnerships, and cultural moments as attention acquisitions; the attention-to-memory chain — encoding requires attention (depth of processing), so low-attention impressions do little brand work regardless of reach claims; emotional attention — emotion's role in capture and memory, with the evidence on emotional advertising effectiveness; brand safety and attention context — adjacency effects, where attention lands matters (news context, creator context), and the measurement of context quality; cultural positioning — brands entering existing attention streams (trends, memes, moments) versus creating their own, with the authenticity risks of trend-jacking; long versus short — the Binet-Field balance finding (roughly 60/40 brand-building versus activation) as an attention-allocation heuristic with its data caveats; attention equity — cumulative fame as a stock that decays without maintenance; and brand-attention measurement plans tying fame, salience, and platform metrics to business outcomes.
