## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Turns the toolkit on the analyst's inputs: social-media data biases — self-selection into posting, platform-visibility bias (algorithmic amplification shapes what data exists), bot and astroturf contamination, and the vocal-minority problem (skewed participation distributions); survey biases in trend measurement — social-desirability responding (stated versus revealed preference gaps), acquiescence, question-order and framing effects in survey instruments, and non-response bias; recall bias — consumers misremembering adoption timing and motivations, corrupting survey-based trend histories; search-data biases — availability-driven search, news-cycle contamination of search indices, and the interpretation gap between search and purchase; sales-data confounds — promotion calendars, supply constraints, and competitor actions mimicking bias-driven spikes; observational identification problems — distinguishing social proof from genuine quality signals, cascades from coordinated campaigns, and FOMO spikes from supply-limited rationing; measurement-instrument bias — sentiment models inheriting training-data biases, engagement metrics rewarding outrage (connecting to the attention course); the analyst's own contribution — confirmation-driven query construction, selective time-windowing, and narrative-first analysis; triangulation doctrine — converging evidence from independent data sources with different bias profiles; bias-mapping documentation — recording each data source's bias profile alongside its findings; and simulation practice — testing whether a bias mechanism can quantitatively explain an observed data pattern before asserting it.

**What to teach (decompose and expand each bullet into framework-level treatment):**
