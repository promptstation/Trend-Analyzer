## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the most trend-relevant social mechanism: social proof defined — using others' behavior as evidence about reality, strongest under uncertainty and similarity; Cialdini's influence principles in working depth — reciprocity, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity, each with its experimental base and marketing instantiations; the classic evidence — Asch's conformity experiments, Sherif's norm-formation studies, informational versus normative social influence distinguished; social proof in digital environments — review counts and ratings, bestseller and trending badges, follower counts, purchase notifications, waitlists, and 'X people are viewing this' mechanics, with platform-native amplification of proof signals; manufactured social proof — fake reviews, bot engagement, purchased followers, astroturfed trends, and detection heuristics (review-timing bursts, language patterns, rating distributions); similarity and the reference-group effect — proof from in-groups persuades more (creator communities, local trends); the numbers-versus-credibility trade-off — when large counts backfire (over-commercialization signals); authority and expertise cues — verification badges, expert endorsements, credential signaling; liking and parasocial influence — creator affinity as a persuasion channel (connecting to the attention course); ethical deployment — honest proof versus fabrication, with regulatory exposure (FTC review rules and equivalents); and analysis practice — auditing a product's or trend's social-proof architecture and estimating its contribution to observed demand.
