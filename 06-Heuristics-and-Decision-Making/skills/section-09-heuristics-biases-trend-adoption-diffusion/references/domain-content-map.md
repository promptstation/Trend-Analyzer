## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Connects the bias toolkit to trend dynamics specifically: the bandwagon effect — adoption accelerating with adoption share, distinguished from genuine network externalities and from information cascades, with measurement approaches; novelty-seeking versus conformity — the two-sided adoption psychology (early adopters' distinctiveness needs versus majority safety-seeking) bridging to diffusion-of-innovations categories (detailed in course 12); the hype cycle — technology-triggered expectation inflation, peak of inflated expectations, trough of disillusionment, and slope of enlightenment as an institutional-level bias pattern, with its heuristic status acknowledged; planning fallacy and optimism in trend timing — why organizations systematically misjudge adoption speed in both directions (too early or too late); survivorship and selection biases in trend evidence — visible successes hiding the base rate of failed trends, and the availability-driven overestimation of trend frequency; narrative bias — stories as cognitive frames that make trends feel inevitable (hindsight bias in trend reports); recency and extrapolation — linear-projecting recent growth (the documented tendency to expect continuation), and mean-reversion ignorance; status-quo and adoption inertia — the flip side explaining slow starts; network externalities and coordination — adoption as coordination game with tipping points; the bias-interaction model of a trend wave — novelty-seekers start it, social proof and cascades scale it, FOMO and scarcity accelerate it, and saturation triggers reactance and anti-cascade; and the trend-analyst's bias audit — correcting one's own wave interpretation for availability, narrative, and survivorship distortions.
