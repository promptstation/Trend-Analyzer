# Ethics, Dark Patterns, and Regulation of Behavioral Influence — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **ethics, Dark Patterns, and Regulation of Behavioral Influence** within Judgment and decision science, behavioral economics, and consumer psychology applied to the biases, FOMO, herd mentality, and social proof that drive purchase spikes and trend adoption.

Subject scope: Covers responsible application: the influence spectrum — from honest persuasion (informing choice) through nudging (shaping default environments) to manipulation (exploiting biases against users' interests), with the autonomy and welfare criteria distinguishing them; dark patterns taxonomy — Mathur-lineage and Gray-lineage classifications (confirmshaming, false urgency, hidden costs, roach motels, forced continuity, social-proof fabrication), their measured prevalence in e-commerce and apps, and their documented consumer harms; legal and regulatory landscape — FTC Act unfair/deceptive standards applied to fake reviews and false scarcity, the EU Digital Services Act and consumer-protection directives addressing dark patterns, state-level US laws, and enforcement cases as precedent; platform governance — app-store and platform policies against manipulative design; vulnerable populations — children's susceptibility to persuasive design (age-appropriate design codes), addiction-adjacent mechanics, and financial-harm contexts; practitioner ethics codes — behavioral-science applications' professional standards and disclosure norms; the asymmetry problem — defenders using the same knowledge for friction, warnings, and cooling-off periods (scam interruption, purchase-delay patterns); ethical influence design — honest scarcity, transparent defaults, opt-in amplification, and the business case (trust as durable asset, dark-pattern churn evidence); whistleblowing and professional responsibility — when to refuse a manipulative brief; trend-analysis ethics — not manufacturing demand spikes through coordinated manipulation while claiming to merely observe them; and the ethics checklist — a decision tool for evaluating any behavioral-influence deployment across honesty, autonomy, vulnerability, and reversibility dimensions.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

The module deepens this section's capabilities for a learner progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and connects them to the surrounding discipline rather than teaching them in isolation.

### Condensed workshop

3. Leave behind the relevant checklists as job aids.

### Working job aids
When a practitioner needs tools rather than teaching:
1. Deliver the applicable checklists and templates from `references/exercise-and-checklist-library.md`, customized to their situation.

## Core Output Requirements

- Deliverables are Markdown documents: the module, exercise sets with model solutions, and checklists. No placeholders, no "TODO" sections.
- Ground content in authoritative sources:
- Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow, and the Tversky-Kahneman heuristics-and-biases research program
- Gigerenzer's ecological-rationality and fast-and-frugal-heuristics work (the adaptive-heuristic counterpoint)
- Cialdini, Influence — social proof, scarcity, commitment, authority, reciprocity, liking, unity
- Thaler & Sunstein, Nudge, and the choice-architecture and defaults literature
- Banerjee and Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch information-cascade models of herd behavior
- Przybylski-lineage FOMO research and the scarcity/urgency experimental literature (Cialdini's commodity theory roots)
- Mathur, Gunawan & Gray et al. on dark-pattern prevalence and regulation, plus the replication-crisis reassessments of priming-era behavioral findings
- Maintain an evidence ledger while writing: every factual claim or number is either sourced, flagged as disputed/popular account, or omitted. Never invent statistics, studies, or citations.
- Distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations, and say which is which.
- Explain each specialized term in clear language on first use.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Validation Gate

Before delivery, verify:

### Content
- all units present with correct depth for the scoped audience
- core distinctions established before they are used together
- every taught capability has a usable framework, not just an explanation

### Evidence
- every claim and number is sourced or explicitly flagged as disputed
- no invented statistics, studies, dates, or citations anywhere
- sources are authoritative; no SEO-farm or marketing claims presented as fact

### Capability
- each absorbed capability (5 in this section) is covered by teaching content AND at least one exercise with evaluation criteria
- exercises have model solutions in full-module mode

### Artifacts
- all gate checklists included and actionable as written
- template structure followed; no placeholder sections

### Quality
- trade-offs stated wherever recommendations are context-dependent
- terminology explained on first use
- reads as practitioner-written, not generic AI advice

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Treating biases as fixed laws of human nature rather than context-dependent tendencies with contested effect sizes; Ignoring the bias blind spot — assuming consumers are biased while the analyst's own trend calls are objective; Confusing correlation-with-purchase for causal persuasion effects in observed spikes; Sliding from explaining behavior into manipulating it without ethical and legal guardrails; Over-relying on single-study findings from literatures hit by replication failures; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.

## Final Principle

The goal is not more content about Heuristics and decision-making. The goal is that a practitioner could take this material and perform: Classify influence tactics along the persuasion-nudge-manipulation spectrum with autonomy criteria, operate an ethics checklist covering honesty, autonomy, vulnerability, and reversibility before any behavioral campaign — with the same evidence discipline the field's best practitioners use. The module must function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology.
