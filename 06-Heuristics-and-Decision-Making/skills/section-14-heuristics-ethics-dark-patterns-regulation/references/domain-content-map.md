## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Classify influence tactics along the persuasion-nudge-manipulation spectrum with autonomy criteria.
2. Identify dark patterns by taxonomy and assess prevalence evidence.
3. Apply the regulatory landscape (FTC, DSA, state laws) to influence deployments.
4. Design defensive friction and honest-influence alternatives.
5. Operate an ethics checklist covering honesty, autonomy, vulnerability, and reversibility before any behavioral campaign.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers responsible application: the influence spectrum — from honest persuasion (informing choice) through nudging (shaping default environments) to manipulation (exploiting biases against users' interests), with the autonomy and welfare criteria distinguishing them; dark patterns taxonomy — Mathur-lineage and Gray-lineage classifications (confirmshaming, false urgency, hidden costs, roach motels, forced continuity, social-proof fabrication), their measured prevalence in e-commerce and apps, and their documented consumer harms; legal and regulatory landscape — FTC Act unfair/deceptive standards applied to fake reviews and false scarcity, the EU Digital Services Act and consumer-protection directives addressing dark patterns, state-level US laws, and enforcement cases as precedent; platform governance — app-store and platform policies against manipulative design; vulnerable populations — children's susceptibility to persuasive design (age-appropriate design codes), addiction-adjacent mechanics, and financial-harm contexts; practitioner ethics codes — behavioral-science applications' professional standards and disclosure norms; the asymmetry problem — defenders using the same knowledge for friction, warnings, and cooling-off periods (scam interruption, purchase-delay patterns); ethical influence design — honest scarcity, transparent defaults, opt-in amplification, and the business case (trust as durable asset, dark-pattern churn evidence); whistleblowing and professional responsibility — when to refuse a manipulative brief; trend-analysis ethics — not manufacturing demand spikes through coordinated manipulation while claiming to merely observe them; and the ethics checklist — a decision tool for evaluating any behavioral-influence deployment across honesty, autonomy, vulnerability, and reversibility dimensions.
