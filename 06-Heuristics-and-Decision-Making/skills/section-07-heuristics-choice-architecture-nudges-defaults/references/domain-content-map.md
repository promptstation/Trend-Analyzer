## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the environment-design layer: choice architecture defined — every presentation of options embeds a design that shapes choice, with no neutral option; the Nudge framework — libertarian paternalism, the Thaler-Sunstein toolkit (defaults, framing, social-norm feedback, salience, friction reduction), and MINDSPACE/EAST as applied taxonomies; defaults in depth — the powerful documented effects (organ donation, retirement enrollment, privacy settings, green energy), why defaults stick (status-quo bias, endorsement inference, effort), and default design for trends (opt-in versus opt-out participation mechanics); friction and sludge — adding or removing steps as behavioral levers, with one-click purchasing and checkout-streamlining evidence; salience and ordering — position effects, decoy and compromise effects (the asymmetrical-dominance finding), and assortment architecture; social-norm feedback — descriptive versus injunctive norms, the boomerang effect when norms are miscommunicated, and 'most people like you' messaging; personalization as choice architecture — recommendation systems narrowing the option set (connecting to the recommendation-architecture course); nudge evidence quality — meta-analytic effect sizes, heterogeneity across contexts, and the replication-literate reading; sludge audits and choice-architecture audits as analytic methods; the nudge-versus-boost debate — designing environments versus building competencies (Gigerenzer's position); and applying architecture analysis — auditing a purchase funnel or platform for the choice-architecture levers driving its conversion and adoption patterns.
