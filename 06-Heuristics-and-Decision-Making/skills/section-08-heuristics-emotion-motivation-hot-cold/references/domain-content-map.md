## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the affective drivers: emotion in decision-making — the move from bias-as-error to emotion-as-input (somatic-marker lineage at overview, affect-as-information in depth), with integral versus incidental emotion distinguished; incidental-emotion effects — anxiety increasing information-seeking and safety-product demand, anger increasing risk-taking and punitive choices, sadness increasing disposition-to-change and valuation shifts (the documented 'helpless' pricing effects), excitement driving impulsive purchase; emotional arousal and narrow focus — high-arousal states degrading deliberation, the hot-cold empathy gap (underestimating future hot-state behavior), and implications for impulse-spike analysis; emotional contagion — mood spreading through networks and feeds, its measurement, and its amplification of trend emotions; mood and trend perception — analysts' own affect biasing trend calls; motivation systems — regulatory focus (promotion versus prevention orientation shaping which trends resonate), needs-based adoption (belonging, status, identity as the deep drivers under observed purchases), and self-determination at overview; identity and expressive consumption — purchases as identity signals, the symbolic-value framework, and identity-consistent trend adoption; anticipatory emotions — expected regret and nostalgia as purchase drivers; measurement practice — emotion signals in social text (connecting to the sentiment course) as demand-spike correlates, with emotion-label validity caveats; and the analysis synthesis — mapping a demand spike's emotional signature (fear, excitement, belonging) to its mechanism family.
