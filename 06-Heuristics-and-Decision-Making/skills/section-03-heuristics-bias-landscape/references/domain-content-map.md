## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Classify observed judgment patterns using an information/judgment/social/decision bias taxonomy.
2. Apply prospect theory constructs (reference dependence, loss aversion, probability weighting) to consumer choices.
3. Audit trend narratives for confirmation, survivorship, and hindsight biases.
4. Evaluate behavioral findings via meta-analyses and replication status rather than single studies.
5. Run bias audits that include the analyst's own blind spot.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Maps the wider bias family with scientific discipline: a working taxonomy — information-processing biases (confirmation, survivorship, selection), judgment biases (overconfidence, planning fallacy, optimism, hindsight), social biases (covered in later sections), and decision biases (loss aversion, framing, status quo, sunk cost); confirmation bias in depth — biased search, interpretation, and memory, its role in trend narrative lock-in, and why it resists simple instruction-based debiasing; loss aversion and prospect theory — reference dependence, diminishing sensitivity, the kinked value function, probability weighting, and the endowment effect, with the documented effect-size debates; framing effects — attribute and goal frames, gain/loss frames in marketing language; overconfidence family — calibration findings, the better-than-average effect, and expert-forecast calibration (Tetlock's findings); sunk-cost and status-quo biases in adoption and churn decisions; the replication crisis confronted — which landmark findings weakened under replication (ego depletion, social-priming effects), what survived (many heuristics-and-biases staples), and the general lesson of consulting meta-analyses over single studies; effect-size discipline — small-to-moderate effects compounding across populations versus individual determinism; bias-blind-spot and naïve realism — the finding that people see bias in others more than themselves, aimed squarely at the analyst; and using the taxonomy — a bias-audit checklist for interpreting observed consumer behavior spikes.

**What to teach (decompose and expand each bullet into framework-level treatment):**
