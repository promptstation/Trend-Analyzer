## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Evaluate judgment research by design, effect size, and external validity.
2. Read meta-analyses and multilab replications for current best evidence, spot p-hacking, HARKing, and publication-bias red flags, separate observational confounds from genuine bias mechanisms in market data.
3. Apply a personal evidence checklist before admitting bias explanations into trend analysis.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Instills the evidence skills the rest of the course assumes: experimental methods in judgment research — lab versus field experiments, vignette designs and their external-validity limits, incentive-compatible designs; effect sizes and practical significance — why statistically significant small effects still matter at population scale yet rarely determine individual behavior; meta-analysis literacy — reading forest plots, heterogeneity, publication bias (funnel asymmetry), and multilab replication projects as the current best evidence; the replication crisis mapped — categories of findings (large-priming-era effects that failed, robust heuristics-and-biases staples, contested loss-aversion magnitudes) with the Open Science reforms (preregistration, registered reports) as context; p-hacking and HARKing recognition — spotting methodological red flags in cited studies; external validity for applied use — lab bias effects translating to market behavior, the field-experiment literature, and industry A/B evidence as the applied gold standard; observational-data pitfalls — inferring bias mechanisms from sales data requires ruling out confounds (marketing spend, seasonality, supply); pre-analysis thinking — specifying what evidence would confirm versus falsify a bias explanation for an observed spike; Bayesian updating of beliefs about mechanisms — prior strength from theory and evidence quality; the consultancy-claim audit — evaluating vendor and thought-leadership claims about behavioral effects; and building a personal evidence standard — a checklist for admitting any bias mechanism into an analysis.
