## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches improving judgment, including the analyst's own: the debiasing evidence — what works (structured techniques, external viewpoint, incentives for accuracy in some contexts) versus what mostly fails (one-shot bias warnings, general education about biases), per the Morewedge-lineage review; cognitive-forcing strategies — consider-the-opposite, devil's advocacy, premortems, and checklist culture (Gawande lineage) with their documented effects and implementation decay; reference-class forecasting — the outside-view method (Flyvbjerg's work), base-rate discipline for trend-timing estimates; decision hygiene protocols — independent estimates before discussion (killing anchoring), blind analysis, and structured aggregation; forecasting tournaments and the superforecaster findings — practices of accurate forecasters (granular probability thinking, active open-mindedness, frequent updating) as trainable decision hygiene; probability calibration training — documented improvement from brief training; choice-architecture self-application — designing one's own decision environments (defaults for analysis workflows, friction against impulsive conclusions); organizational debiasing — red teams, pre-mortem rituals, and prediction markets inside firms, with the political realities; the analyst's specific risks — trend-call overconfidence, narrative lock-in, client-pleasing bias, and recency in report writing; measurement of debiasing effectiveness — tracking forecast calibration and decision-outcome quality over time; humility engineering — confidence language standards in trend reports (calibrated verbal probabilities); and building a personal and team decision-hygiene system with the course's full bias inventory.
