## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Scales from individual to collective judgment: group polarization — deliberation shifting groups toward more extreme positions, its mechanisms (persuasive arguments, social comparison) and online amplification evidence; groupthink — Janis' syndrome (cohesion, insulation, self-censorship, illusion of unanimity) with its classic cases and modern critiques, applied to trend-committee decisions and brand teams; wisdom-of-crowds — the Galton lineage, aggregation conditions (independence, diversity, decentralization), and when crowds fail (correlated errors, cascades breaking independence); prediction markets and collective forecasting — documented calibration performance versus expert panels; collective effervescence — Durkheim's concept for shared emotional arousal at events (launches, drops, livestreams) as a purchase-spike mechanism; crowd behavior modernized — from Le Bon's contagion metaphor to emergent-norm and social-identity approaches, with panic-buying as coordinated (not irrational) behavior under scarcity beliefs; committee forecasting failures — why group trend calls degrade (dominance, anchoring on first speakers, shared information bias) and structured alternatives (Delphi, nominal groups, prediction aggregation); organizational herding — firms copying firms (mimetic isomorphism, the DiMaggio-Powell finding) as trend amplification at market level; social-movement participation — threshold models of joining (Granovetter 1978) connecting individual heuristics to collective action; measuring collective judgment — aggregating social signals without cascade contamination; and the analyst's group practice — designing trend-review processes immune to the failure modes covered.
