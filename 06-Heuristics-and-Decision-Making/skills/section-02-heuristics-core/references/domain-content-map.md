## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Predict availability-driven prevalence misjudgments from recency and media exposure.
2. Diagnose representativeness errors including base-rate neglect and small-sample insensitivity.
3. Identify anchoring effects in pricing and narrative formation.
4. Apply the affect heuristic to emotion-driven preference judgments, and decompose real consumer and analyst judgments into component heuristics with testable bias predictions.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the canonical heuristics in working depth: availability — judging frequency and probability by ease of recall, its drivers (recency, vividness, media coverage, emotional intensity), and its trend consequences (recent viral events inflating perceived prevalence, media-shaped risk and demand perception); representativeness — judging by similarity to prototypes, producing base-rate neglect, conjunction fallacies (the Linda problem), and insensitivity to sample size, with trend applications (judging a product by fit to a trend prototype rather than market base rates); anchoring and adjustment — arbitrary anchors shifting estimates, anchored pricing and reference-price effects in commerce, and first-mover anchoring in trend narratives; the affect heuristic — feelings as information, how emotional tags on mental representations drive risk and preference judgments, and mood-congruent trend perception; simulation heuristic and counterfactual thinking at overview; recognition heuristic (Goldstein-Gigerenzer) as the ecological-rationality showcase; heuristic selection — which heuristic fires when, and the substitution principle (answering an easier question than the one asked); the biases each heuristic produces stated as testable predictions; cross-heuristic interaction in real judgment (an available, representative, anchored, affect-laden trend call); and practice — decomposing a real consumer judgment or analyst forecast into its heuristic components with debiasing implications.
