## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce

2. Identify cascade-enabling platform conditions (sequential observable decisions).
3. Distinguish information-driven growth from cascade-driven herding in demand waves.
4. Analyze anti-cascade backlash dynamics at saturation.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches market-level herding: information cascades formalized — the Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch result that rational agents ignoring private signals to follow predecessors can lock in wrong choices, with the fragility of cascades (a little contrary information can break them); Banerjee's model of rational herding under payoff uncertainty; cascade conditions — sequential decisions, observable actions but hidden information, and why platforms maximize cascade potential (public counts, visible adoption, feed ordering); herding versus social proof distinguished — cascades as the dynamic process, proof as the static cue; empirical cascade domains — financial bubbles and crashes, bank runs, fashion cycles, technology adoption, panic buying (documented episodes), and queue phenomena; contrarian value — information embedded in prices versus cascade distortion; anti-cascades and backlash dynamics — when visible over-adoption triggers avoidance (saturation, cringe, counter-signaling), connecting to trend lifecycles; cascade prediction research — early-behavior features of cascading versus stalling content, with modest predictive power per the literature; network position effects — cascades need bridge nodes and clusters (connecting to the network course); intervention points — slowing harmful cascades (circuit breakers, friction, disclosure) and their evidence; and the trend analyst's application — classifying an observed demand wave as information-driven growth versus cascade-driven herding versus manufactured astroturf, using observable signatures.
