## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the field and its relevance to trend analysis: bounded rationality — Simon's thesis that humans satisfice under cognitive limits rather than optimize, establishing heuristics as the adaptive response to an information-rich world; the dual-process framework — fast, automatic, associative System 1 versus slow, deliberate System 2, presented with its modern scientific caveats (a useful metaphor rather than a literal two-brain architecture); heuristics defined — effort-reducing judgment strategies that are usually adaptive but predictably biased in specific environments; the two scholarly traditions contrasted — the heuristics-and-biases program (Tversky-Kahneman: heuristics as error sources) versus ecological rationality (Gigerenzer: heuristics as fitted tools whose performance depends on environment structure), with the debate's practical lesson that context determines whether a heuristic helps; why trend analysts need this course — demand spikes and adoption waves are mass expressions of judgment heuristics, and the analyst's own forecasts are heuristic-laden too; the analysis targets named — social proof, herd behavior, FOMO, scarcity, anchoring, and availability as the bias family behind most trend surges; evidence culture — experimental methods of judgment research, effect sizes, and the replication-crisis reassessment as standing context for every finding used later; the decision-ecology frame — environments (platforms, markets) that exploit or protect against biases; and the course map from specific heuristics through social mechanisms, market-level cascades, and debiasing to the ethics of influence and a capstone demand-spike analysis.
