## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers commercial application with its evidence and risks: meme marketing defined — brands participating in memetic culture via original memetic content, template adoption, and meme-adjacent humor; why it works when it works — native format fluency earning attention and affinity at low production cost, with documented breakout cases; the participation spectrum — observing, referencing, remixing, originating — and the fluency gradient brands must climb; timing discipline — the lifecycle awareness that brand entry at peak or post-peak reads as cringe (the 'how do you do, fellow kids' failure mode); authenticity and community detection — audiences' sensitivity to corporate memetic incompetence, and the brands-as-community-members standard; risks catalogued — context blindness (adopting memes with hidden meanings or dark origins), tone mismatch during news events, appropriation of community language, legal exposure (copyright in templates, music licensing, right-of-publicity), and platform-policy issues; the measurement of meme marketing — attention earned, sentiment, remix response, and the weak evidence connecting memetic participation to sales; co-optation dynamics — what happens to a meme when brands adopt it (acceleration to exhaustion, community abandonment), making brand entry a lifecycle event for the meme itself; influencer and creator mediation — paying fluency versus faking it; brand-specific memetic assets — owned formats and characters that function as proprietary templates; case post-mortems — successes and failures decomposed by lifecycle timing, community reading, and execution fluency; and the strategy frame — memes as one layer of cultural participation, not a campaign substitute.
