## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Audit memes for dark origins and hidden harmful signification before use or reporting.
2. Apply attribution and credit norms countering origination erasure.
3. Assess harm to memetic targets with consent and privacy awareness, report harmful memetic dynamics without instructional replication.
4. Operate a pre-use ethics checklist covering origin, target, community impact, and amplification.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Confronts the dark side and the analyst's responsibilities: harmful memes — harassment campaigns using memetic formats (brigading templates, mocking formats targeting individuals), dehumanization mechanics, and documented harm cases; the targets problem — individual victims of memetic pile-ons versus public-figure contexts, and the different ethical weights; dark origins and laundering — memes whose meanings hide racist, antisemitic, or extremist signification (documented symbol-cooptation cases), and the due-diligence practice of checking genealogy before use or amplification; attribution and credit — origination erasure (especially of Black creators' memetic labor, the documented TikTok-credit controversies), and citation norms for analysts and brands; privacy in memetic subjects — unwitting people who become memes (the 'main character' phenomenon) and the consent absence; analysis ethics — avoiding surveillance of vulnerable communities, aggregate reporting standards, and not amplifying harmful templates through documentation; platform governance — moderation of harmful memes, enforcement inconsistency, and the Streisand dynamics of suppression; researcher positionality — the analyst as participant-observer with power to legitimize or expose; responsible reporting — describing harmful memetic dynamics without instructional replication; legal dimensions — defamation, harassment law, and copyright in memetic contexts at awareness; and the professional standard — a pre-use ethics checklist for memetic analysis and participation covering origin check, target check, community impact, and amplification consequences.
