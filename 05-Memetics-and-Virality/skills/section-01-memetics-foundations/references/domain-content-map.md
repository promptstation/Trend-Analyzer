## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the field: Dawkins' 1976 coinage of the meme as a unit of cultural transmission analogous to the gene — replicating through imitation with variation and selection, carrying the selfish-gene logic to culture; the core claim examined — culture evolves by differential replication, not merely by invention-and-adoption; meme defined operationally for internet contexts — a recognizable unit (image macro, sound, phrase, format, gesture, challenge) that spreads by copying and remixing, with Shifman's distinction between single viral artifacts and memetic families of many user-created variants; the internet's effect on memetics — near-zero copying cost, visible mutation trails, and measurable spread turning metaphor into data; why trend analysts care — memes are the fastest-moving layer of culture and the leading indicators of aesthetic, linguistic, and behavioral trends; the participatory premise — audiences are co-creators, and spread is driven by remaking, not just sharing; the scope of memetic phenomena — jokes and formats, but also slang, sounds, challenges, aesthetics, and behaviors; the course's honest framing that memetics is contested as a scientific theory yet powerful as an analytic lens; key vocabulary established (replication, variation, selection, fitness, mutation, template); and the course map from theory and mechanics through measurement, marketing, politics, and ethics to capstone meme-intelligence practice.
