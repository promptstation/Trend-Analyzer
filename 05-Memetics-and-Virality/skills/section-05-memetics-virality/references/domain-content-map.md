## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the sharing side of spread: virality defined — cascade-based spread through person-to-person transmission, distinguished from broadcast reach and algorithmic amplification (most apparent virality is platform-routed); the psychology of sharing — self-presentation and identity signaling, social bonding, emotional regulation, information utility, and status-seeking as documented motivations; Berger-Milkman's most-emailed findings — high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increase transmission while sadness decreases it, with practicality and surprise effects; the STEPPS framework (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories) presented as a practitioner checklist with its evidence base honestly scoped — practitioner-derived, partially validated; the novelty-and-emotion mechanism behind false-content spread advantage (Vosoughi-lineage findings) extended to memes generally; shareability features of content — identity fit, emotional charge, practical value, narrative structure, and remarkability; the social-transmission bias finding — content mutates toward party-congruent framing as it spreads; virality's base rates — most content fails, extreme right-skew of cascade sizes, and the survivorship bias in viral case studies; what virality does not predict — purchase, belief, or lasting attention; early-signal research — first-hour velocity features with weak-to-moderate predictive power per the cascade literature; and the analyst's synthesis — sharing motivation, content features, and platform routing as the three legs of any viral event.
