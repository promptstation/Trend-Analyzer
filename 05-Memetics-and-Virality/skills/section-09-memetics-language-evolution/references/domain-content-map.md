## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Extends memetics to language: linguistic memes — catchphrases, slang terms, copypastas, and phrasal templates as replicating units with measurable diffusion; slang lifecycles — in-group origination (often AAVE, gaming, and platform subcultures), diffusion to adjacent groups, mainstream adoption, and the 'dead on arrival in mainstream' pattern where adoption by out-groups kills in-group value; mechanisms of lexical spread — imitation with variation, identity signaling, economy of expression, and playful elaboration; the appropriation dynamic — mainstream and commercial uptake of marginalized-community language, with the credit and compensation debates (the 'blaccent' and Gen-Z-slang-from-AAVE discussions) as essential context for analysts; measuring linguistic diffusion — term frequency tracking (Google Books, social corpora, Urban Dictionary submission patterns), spread velocity by demographic group, and dialect geography online; internet-language evolution — abbreviations, emoji and reaction-gif semantics as non-verbal memetic systems, and platform-specific registers; copypastas and phrasal templates — long-form linguistic memes with mutation patterns; semantic bleaching and pejoration — how meanings shift through use (euphemism treadmill dynamics); language as trend indicator — slang diffusion curves preceding broader cultural adoption, making linguistic tracking an early-warning system; computational sociolinguistics methods — at working level for tracking term spread; and the ethics of linguistic trend reporting — documenting versus extracting community language.
