## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the interpretive layer: memes as folklore — communal authorship, variation in transmission, and group-marking functions connecting internet memes to oral-tradition scholarship (Coleman, Tangherlini lineage); memetic communities — subcultures whose shared templates constitute membership literacy (in-group decoding as boundary maintenance); meaning-making practice — how communities negotiate meme meanings through use, and the analyst's task of reading meaning from practice rather than text alone; identity and belonging functions — memes as social glue, affiliation signals, and collective coping mechanisms (trauma humor, generational humor); context collapse — when memes cross community boundaries and meanings break (the misreading problem), and how collapse drives both virality and conflict; the cringe economy — community policing of memetic competence, second-hand embarrassment as norm enforcement, and mainstream adoption as cringe-marking; platform-community relationships — how site cultures (imageboards, Reddit, TikTok niches) produce distinct memetic styles and ethics; memetic literacy as cultural capital — who can read and make memes fluently, and the generational dimension (Gen Z/Alpha fluency connecting to the netnography course); respect and extraction tensions — researcher and brand entry into memetic communities as extractive encounters, with ethical documentation practice; memes and collective memory — how memetic archives preserve community history; and the interpretive toolkit — reading memes as situated social practice requiring community context, not just content analysis.
