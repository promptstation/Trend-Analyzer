## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the core mechanics in analytic detail: replication — what copying looks like online (exact shares, reposts, saves-and-reuploads, template reuse, sound reuse on TikTok), and how each copying mode preserves or degrades the original; variation — the mutation sources (remix, caption change, format translation, context transposition, parody, ironic inversion) and the observation that most internet memes spread as families of variants rather than identical copies; selection — the filters acting on variants (audience attention and sharing choices, algorithmic amplification, platform norms and moderation, community gatekeeping) as a multi-level selection environment; fitness operationalized — replication rate per exposure, variant longevity, template generativity (how many new instances a template spawns), and cross-community transmissibility; inheritance with modification — how successful mutations become new templates (template lineage as the unit of study); drift and founder effects — random early spread advantages, timing luck, and the role of superspreaders (connecting to network course); mutation-selection balance — why templates accumulate conventions (font, layout, sound timing) that violate at a cost; co-adaptation and memeplexes — sounds plus dances plus hashtags as clusters that spread together; the pace difference — internet memes evolve in days what folklore evolved in decades, enabling real-time observation of cultural evolution; and applying the mechanics — decomposing any viral phenomenon into its replication modes, variation sources, selection filters, and fitness measures.
