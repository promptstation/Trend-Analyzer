## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches variation analysis as the core memetic skill: mutation types catalogued — substitution (element swapped), recombination (memes crossed), elaboration (extended formats), reduction (minimalist versions), context transposition (meme moved to new community or topic), and inversion (ironic reversal of meaning); template evolution — how a template's obligatory and variable slots shift over its life (early experimentation, convention crystallization, late-stage parody and exhaustion); meaning drift — the same form accumulating different meanings across communities and time (semantic mutation), and how analysts track it; format translation — memes crossing platforms with affordance-driven mutations (image macro to video, text to sound), and what survives translation (the meme's relational structure often survives form change); phylogenetic analysis of memes — building lineage trees of variants from timestamps and features, with the practical methods (visual similarity, feature coding, reference to archives); the irony ladder — sincere use, then self-aware use, then ironic use, then post-ironic ambiguity, as a predictable memetic lifecycle pattern and its detection difficulty; accelerated mutation — algorithmic platforms' remix tools (duets, stitches, sounds) industrializing variation; mutation and fitness — which mutations spread (novelty premiums, in-group signaling value) and which die (convention violations that break recognition); deepfakes and AI-generated variation — synthetic mutation at scale and detection challenges; and remix analysis practice — coding variant features, mapping lineage, and identifying the mutations that drove a meme's second wind.
