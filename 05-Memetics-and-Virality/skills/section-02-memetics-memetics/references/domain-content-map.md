## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds theoretical depth with the critiques that professional use requires: the memetics lineage — Dawkins' original formulation, the Journal of Memetics era (1997-2005), Dennett's philosophical expansion, and Blackmore's imitation-driven theory; the classical machinery — memes as replicators, memeplexes as co-adapted clusters, fidelity-versus-fecundity trade-offs; the major criticisms stated precisely — no clear unit of cultural replication (ideas don't copy like DNA), transmission is reconstruction not duplication (Sperber's epidemiology-of-representations alternative), memetics' weak predictive record, and its tendency toward just-so stories; the disciplinary shift — from memetics to internet meme studies (Shifman, Milner, Wiggins) treating memes as situated social practice rather than selfish replicators; cultural-evolution synthesis — dual-inheritance theory (Boyd-Richerson) and cultural-attractor approaches as the rigorous cousins of memetics, with their population-level claims; what survives the critique — variation-selection-retention as a descriptive frame, measurable mutation in template reuse, and fitness differences across formats; the gene-culture analogy held at proper strength — useful heuristic, false mechanism; the folklore lineage — memes as folk expression with communal authorship, connecting to oral-tradition scholarship; terminology hygiene — avoiding "meme" as synonym for "viral post"; and the analyst's working theory — a defensible memetics that claims patterns and mechanisms without overclaiming replicator ontology.
