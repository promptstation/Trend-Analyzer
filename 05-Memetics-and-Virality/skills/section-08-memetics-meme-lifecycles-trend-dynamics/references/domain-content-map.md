## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the temporal pattern: the lifecycle stages — latent/origin (creation and small-community circulation), emergence (cross-community breakout, often via a bridge community or creator), acceleration (template crystallization, mass participation, algorithmic pickup), peak (maximum velocity and saturation), exhaustion (convention fatigue, irony inversion, cringe marking), death (residual sporadic use), and revival/reactivation (context shifts, nostalgia cycles, anniversary reuse); lifecycle timing — typical durations by meme class (news-jacking memes in hours-days, template memes in weeks, format innovations in months, evergreen reaction images in years); measurement practice — tracking lifecycle position via velocity, template-instance counts, community spread breadth, and irony-stage signals; early-emergence detection — the signals that a community-local meme is about to break out (rising remix rate, cross-community first instances, creator adoption); peak-calling and the late-entrant problem — participating after saturation incurs cringe costs, making lifecycle timing a strategic variable; second-wind mechanics — how mutations and context shifts revive dying memes; the connection to trend hierarchies — memes as the fast layer beneath slower aesthetic and behavioral trends, with memetic velocity as leading indicator; lifecycle comparison across platforms — TikTok's fast churn versus Reddit's slower cycles versus image-board origination patterns; dormancy and the memetic seed bank — archives, sound libraries, and screenshot culture preserving variants for reactivation; case studies in lifecycle analysis — breakouts, false starts, and revivals decomposed; and the lifecycle dashboard — stage classification with confidence and recommended action timing.
