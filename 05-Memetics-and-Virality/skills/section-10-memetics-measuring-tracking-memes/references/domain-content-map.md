## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the measurement practice: the unit-of-measurement problem — memes as families of variants require tracking templates, not single posts; data sources — platform APIs and their access limits, social-listening tools, Know Your Meme timelines, hashtag and sound pages as native aggregators, and archive services; template detection methods — image-similarity approaches (perceptual hashing, embedding similarity), sound-matching on video platforms, text-template extraction, and their accuracy limits; meme genealogy reconstruction — first-seen estimation, lineage building across platforms, and the origination-attribution problem (invisible originators, private and deleted sources); spread measurement — instance counts over time, velocity, community breadth, and cross-platform migration tracking; engagement quality signals — remix rate as the key memetic metric (participation beats passive sharing), with saves and duet/stitch counts as platform-specific variants; sentiment and meaning coding — human annotation of variant meaning drift at scale, with LLM-assisted coding workflows and their validation; dashboards for meme tracking — early-warning views combining new-template detection, velocity, and lifecycle stage; evaluation of tracking systems — precision/recall on known meme sets, false-positive control (organic content misflagged as memetic); the measurement ethics — respecting community context, avoiding surveillance of vulnerable groups, and attribution norms; and the tracking stack blueprint — connecting detection, genealogy, velocity, and meaning modules into continuous meme intelligence.
