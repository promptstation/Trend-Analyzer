## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Maps the phenomenon's actual forms: the taxonomy of internet memes — image macros and their template conventions, reaction images and GIFs, video memes and formats (TikTok trends, sound-based formats, green-screen remixes), audio memes and platform sounds, challenges and participatory formats, hashtag memes, linguistic memes (copypastas, catchphrases, slang), and aesthetic memes (visual styles like cottagecore as memetic families); genre conventions — what makes an instance recognizable as the same meme (template features, obligatory elements, permissible variation ranges), and convention violation as either innovation or failure; platform-native memetics — how each platform's affordances (TikTok's sound reuse, X's quote-posts, Instagram's template carousels, Reddit's subculture norms) produce different dominant meme forms; participatory culture — Jenkins' framework, the low-floor creation tools, and memetic production as everyday practice; remix culture and intertextuality — memes as dense reference networks requiring cultural literacy to decode; the lifecycle of format innovation — novel formats earn early attention premiums before saturation; documentation practice — Know Your Meme as genealogical archive, its strengths and community-sourced biases; meme communities and norms — who owns a meme, in-group markers, and community policing of misuse; vernacular creativity — memes as folk art with aesthetic standards; and the analyst's genre literacy — recognizing forms, conventions, and platform-native mechanics as the precondition for tracking.
