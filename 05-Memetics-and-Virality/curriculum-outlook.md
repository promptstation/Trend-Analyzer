# Memetics & Virality — Curriculum Content Outlook

(Beginner-to-expert progression, compressed to 15 sections per the Researching-Outline method.)

**Section 1 — Foundations: The Meme Concept and Cultural Evolution**

**Summary:** Introduces the field: Dawkins' 1976 coinage of the meme as a unit of cultural transmission analogous to the gene — replicating through imitation with variation and selection, carrying the selfish-gene logic to culture; the core claim examined — culture evolves by differential replication, not merely by invention-and-adoption; meme defined operationally for internet contexts — a recognizable unit (image macro, sound, phrase, format, gesture, challenge) that spreads by copying and remixing, with Shifman's distinction between single viral artifacts and memetic families of many user-created variants; the internet's effect on memetics — near-zero copying cost, visible mutation trails, and measurable spread turning metaphor into data; why trend analysts care — memes are the fastest-moving layer of culture and the leading indicators of aesthetic, linguistic, and behavioral trends; the participatory premise — audiences are co-creators, and spread is driven by remaking, not just sharing; the scope of memetic phenomena — jokes and formats, but also slang, sounds, challenges, aesthetics, and behaviors; the course's honest framing that memetics is contested as a scientific theory yet powerful as an analytic lens; key vocabulary established (replication, variation, selection, fitness, mutation, template); and the course map from theory and mechanics through measurement, marketing, politics, and ethics to capstone meme-intelligence practice.

**Absorbed Skill:** Define memes operationally for internet contexts and distinguish single viral artifacts from memetic families, articulate the cultural-evolution claim of differential replication with its contested status, explain why platform copying costs and visible mutation make memes measurable, apply core memetic vocabulary (template, variation, selection, fitness) to observed content, and frame meme participation as co-creation rather than passive sharing.

**Section 2 — Memetics: Theory, History, and Its Critics**

**Summary:** Builds theoretical depth with the critiques that professional use requires: the memetics lineage — Dawkins' original formulation, the Journal of Memetics era (1997-2005), Dennett's philosophical expansion, and Blackmore's imitation-driven theory; the classical machinery — memes as replicators, memeplexes as co-adapted clusters, fidelity-versus-fecundity trade-offs; the major criticisms stated precisely — no clear unit of cultural replication (ideas don't copy like DNA), transmission is reconstruction not duplication (Sperber's epidemiology-of-representations alternative), memetics' weak predictive record, and its tendency toward just-so stories; the disciplinary shift — from memetics to internet meme studies (Shifman, Milner, Wiggins) treating memes as situated social practice rather than selfish replicators; cultural-evolution synthesis — dual-inheritance theory (Boyd-Richerson) and cultural-attractor approaches as the rigorous cousins of memetics, with their population-level claims; what survives the critique — variation-selection-retention as a descriptive frame, measurable mutation in template reuse, and fitness differences across formats; the gene-culture analogy held at proper strength — useful heuristic, false mechanism; the folklore lineage — memes as folk expression with communal authorship, connecting to oral-tradition scholarship; terminology hygiene — avoiding "meme" as synonym for "viral post"; and the analyst's working theory — a defensible memetics that claims patterns and mechanisms without overclaiming replicator ontology.

**Absorbed Skill:** Trace memetics from Dawkins through its critics to contemporary meme studies, state the unit-of-replication and reconstruction-not-duplication objections precisely, position dual-inheritance and cultural-attractor frameworks as rigorous alternatives, calibrate the gene-culture analogy to heuristic strength, and formulate a defensible working theory of memetic dynamics for analysis.

**Section 3 — The Evolutionary Mechanics: Replication, Variation, Selection**

**Summary:** Teaches the core mechanics in analytic detail: replication — what copying looks like online (exact shares, reposts, saves-and-reuploads, template reuse, sound reuse on TikTok), and how each copying mode preserves or degrades the original; variation — the mutation sources (remix, caption change, format translation, context transposition, parody, ironic inversion) and the observation that most internet memes spread as families of variants rather than identical copies; selection — the filters acting on variants (audience attention and sharing choices, algorithmic amplification, platform norms and moderation, community gatekeeping) as a multi-level selection environment; fitness operationalized — replication rate per exposure, variant longevity, template generativity (how many new instances a template spawns), and cross-community transmissibility; inheritance with modification — how successful mutations become new templates (template lineage as the unit of study); drift and founder effects — random early spread advantages, timing luck, and the role of superspreaders (connecting to network course); mutation-selection balance — why templates accumulate conventions (font, layout, sound timing) that violate at a cost; co-adaptation and memeplexes — sounds plus dances plus hashtags as clusters that spread together; the pace difference — internet memes evolve in days what folklore evolved in decades, enabling real-time observation of cultural evolution; and applying the mechanics — decomposing any viral phenomenon into its replication modes, variation sources, selection filters, and fitness measures.

**Absorbed Skill:** Decompose viral phenomena into replication modes, variation sources, and selection filters, operationalize memetic fitness via replication rate, generativity, and cross-community transmissibility, analyze template lineages as inheritance with modification, account for drift, founder effects, and superspreader timing, and identify co-adapted memeplexes spreading as clusters.

**Section 4 — Internet Memes: Forms, Genres, and Participatory Culture**

**Summary:** Maps the phenomenon's actual forms: the taxonomy of internet memes — image macros and their template conventions, reaction images and GIFs, video memes and formats (TikTok trends, sound-based formats, green-screen remixes), audio memes and platform sounds, challenges and participatory formats, hashtag memes, linguistic memes (copypastas, catchphrases, slang), and aesthetic memes (visual styles like cottagecore as memetic families); genre conventions — what makes an instance recognizable as the same meme (template features, obligatory elements, permissible variation ranges), and convention violation as either innovation or failure; platform-native memetics — how each platform's affordances (TikTok's sound reuse, X's quote-posts, Instagram's template carousels, Reddit's subculture norms) produce different dominant meme forms; participatory culture — Jenkins' framework, the low-floor creation tools, and memetic production as everyday practice; remix culture and intertextuality — memes as dense reference networks requiring cultural literacy to decode; the lifecycle of format innovation — novel formats earn early attention premiums before saturation; documentation practice — Know Your Meme as genealogical archive, its strengths and community-sourced biases; meme communities and norms — who owns a meme, in-group markers, and community policing of misuse; vernacular creativity — memes as folk art with aesthetic standards; and the analyst's genre literacy — recognizing forms, conventions, and platform-native mechanics as the precondition for tracking.

**Absorbed Skill:** Classify memes by form (image, video, audio, challenge, linguistic, aesthetic) and platform-native genre, identify template conventions and permissible variation ranges for recognition analysis, apply participatory-culture and remix frameworks to production dynamics, use Know Your Meme and documentation archives for genealogy with bias awareness, and read community ownership norms governing meme use.

**Section 5 — Virality: What Spreads, Why People Share**

**Summary:** Covers the sharing side of spread: virality defined — cascade-based spread through person-to-person transmission, distinguished from broadcast reach and algorithmic amplification (most apparent virality is platform-routed); the psychology of sharing — self-presentation and identity signaling, social bonding, emotional regulation, information utility, and status-seeking as documented motivations; Berger-Milkman's most-emailed findings — high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increase transmission while sadness decreases it, with practicality and surprise effects; the STEPPS framework (Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories) presented as a practitioner checklist with its evidence base honestly scoped — practitioner-derived, partially validated; the novelty-and-emotion mechanism behind false-content spread advantage (Vosoughi-lineage findings) extended to memes generally; shareability features of content — identity fit, emotional charge, practical value, narrative structure, and remarkability; the social-transmission bias finding — content mutates toward party-congruent framing as it spreads; virality's base rates — most content fails, extreme right-skew of cascade sizes, and the survivorship bias in viral case studies; what virality does not predict — purchase, belief, or lasting attention; early-signal research — first-hour velocity features with weak-to-moderate predictive power per the cascade literature; and the analyst's synthesis — sharing motivation, content features, and platform routing as the three legs of any viral event.

**Absorbed Skill:** Distinguish true cascade virality from broadcast and algorithmic amplification, apply sharing-motivation psychology and arousal-based evidence to spread analysis, use STEPPS as a scoped practitioner checklist, account for base rates and survivorship bias in viral claims, and decompose viral events into motivation, content-feature, and platform-routing components.

**Section 6 — Mutation and Remix: How Memes Change as They Spread**

**Summary:** Teaches variation analysis as the core memetic skill: mutation types catalogued — substitution (element swapped), recombination (memes crossed), elaboration (extended formats), reduction (minimalist versions), context transposition (meme moved to new community or topic), and inversion (ironic reversal of meaning); template evolution — how a template's obligatory and variable slots shift over its life (early experimentation, convention crystallization, late-stage parody and exhaustion); meaning drift — the same form accumulating different meanings across communities and time (semantic mutation), and how analysts track it; format translation — memes crossing platforms with affordance-driven mutations (image macro to video, text to sound), and what survives translation (the meme's relational structure often survives form change); phylogenetic analysis of memes — building lineage trees of variants from timestamps and features, with the practical methods (visual similarity, feature coding, reference to archives); the irony ladder — sincere use, then self-aware use, then ironic use, then post-ironic ambiguity, as a predictable memetic lifecycle pattern and its detection difficulty; accelerated mutation — algorithmic platforms' remix tools (duets, stitches, sounds) industrializing variation; mutation and fitness — which mutations spread (novelty premiums, in-group signaling value) and which die (convention violations that break recognition); deepfakes and AI-generated variation — synthetic mutation at scale and detection challenges; and remix analysis practice — coding variant features, mapping lineage, and identifying the mutations that drove a meme's second wind.

**Absorbed Skill:** Classify meme mutations by type (substitution, recombination, transposition, inversion), build variant lineage trees from timestamps and features, track meaning drift across communities and platforms, identify irony-ladder stage from usage signals, and assess which mutations raise memetic fitness versus break recognition.

**Section 7 — Selection Pressures: Algorithms, Audiences, and Environments**

**Summary:** Covers the filter side: the multi-level selection environment — audience choices (attention, sharing, remixing), platform algorithms (amplification and suppression), community gatekeeping (norms, moderation, subcultural policing), and inter-meme competition for finite attention; algorithmic selection — how engagement-prediction objectives favor high-arousal, quickly-decodable, remix-friendly content, and how this shapes which memes dominate (connecting to the attention-economics course); the selection-for-platforms dynamic — memes evolving traits that please ranking systems (hook structure, completion optimization, sound reuse), an evolutionary response to artificial selection; audience selection — demographic and psychographic filters determining which communities adopt which memes, and in-group signaling as a selection force (memes that mark membership); contextual selection — news events, seasons, and cultural moments as environmental shifts that suddenly favor dormant meme traits (reactivation); community selection — norm enforcement (backlash against misuse, cringe dynamics killing over-adopted memes) and saturation-driven decline; brand and commercial selection — market filtering of memetic marketing attempts; suppression and moderation — platform policy as selection against categories of memes, with enforcement variability as an evolutionary factor; the fitness-landscape metaphor applied — local optima (format conventions), landscape shifts (algorithm updates), and generalists versus specialists; and selection analysis practice — for any meme's trajectory, identifying which filters it passed or failed at each stage.

**Absorbed Skill:** Map the multi-level selection environment acting on a meme, analyze algorithmic selection for engagement-optimized traits, identify audience, community, and contextual filters shaping adoption, account for suppression and moderation as selection forces, and diagnose a meme's trajectory stage-by-stage by which selection pressures it passed or failed.

**Section 8 — Meme Lifecycles and Trend Dynamics**

**Summary:** Teaches the temporal pattern: the lifecycle stages — latent/origin (creation and small-community circulation), emergence (cross-community breakout, often via a bridge community or creator), acceleration (template crystallization, mass participation, algorithmic pickup), peak (maximum velocity and saturation), exhaustion (convention fatigue, irony inversion, cringe marking), death (residual sporadic use), and revival/reactivation (context shifts, nostalgia cycles, anniversary reuse); lifecycle timing — typical durations by meme class (news-jacking memes in hours-days, template memes in weeks, format innovations in months, evergreen reaction images in years); measurement practice — tracking lifecycle position via velocity, template-instance counts, community spread breadth, and irony-stage signals; early-emergence detection — the signals that a community-local meme is about to break out (rising remix rate, cross-community first instances, creator adoption); peak-calling and the late-entrant problem — participating after saturation incurs cringe costs, making lifecycle timing a strategic variable; second-wind mechanics — how mutations and context shifts revive dying memes; the connection to trend hierarchies — memes as the fast layer beneath slower aesthetic and behavioral trends, with memetic velocity as leading indicator; lifecycle comparison across platforms — TikTok's fast churn versus Reddit's slower cycles versus image-board origination patterns; dormancy and the memetic seed bank — archives, sound libraries, and screenshot culture preserving variants for reactivation; case studies in lifecycle analysis — breakouts, false starts, and revivals decomposed; and the lifecycle dashboard — stage classification with confidence and recommended action timing.

**Absorbed Skill:** Classify meme lifecycle stage from velocity, spread-breadth, and irony signals, estimate lifecycle timing by meme class and platform, detect early-emergence breakout signals, call peaks and second winds with late-entry cost awareness, and operate a lifecycle dashboard informing strategic participation timing.

**Section 9 — Language Evolution: Slang, Phrases, and Linguistic Memes**

**Summary:** Extends memetics to language: linguistic memes — catchphrases, slang terms, copypastas, and phrasal templates as replicating units with measurable diffusion; slang lifecycles — in-group origination (often AAVE, gaming, and platform subcultures), diffusion to adjacent groups, mainstream adoption, and the 'dead on arrival in mainstream' pattern where adoption by out-groups kills in-group value; mechanisms of lexical spread — imitation with variation, identity signaling, economy of expression, and playful elaboration; the appropriation dynamic — mainstream and commercial uptake of marginalized-community language, with the credit and compensation debates (the 'blaccent' and Gen-Z-slang-from-AAVE discussions) as essential context for analysts; measuring linguistic diffusion — term frequency tracking (Google Books, social corpora, Urban Dictionary submission patterns), spread velocity by demographic group, and dialect geography online; internet-language evolution — abbreviations, emoji and reaction-gif semantics as non-verbal memetic systems, and platform-specific registers; copypastas and phrasal templates — long-form linguistic memes with mutation patterns; semantic bleaching and pejoration — how meanings shift through use (euphemism treadmill dynamics); language as trend indicator — slang diffusion curves preceding broader cultural adoption, making linguistic tracking an early-warning system; computational sociolinguistics methods — at working level for tracking term spread; and the ethics of linguistic trend reporting — documenting versus extracting community language.

**Absorbed Skill:** Track slang and phrase diffusion with frequency and velocity measures, map slang lifecycles including in-group value dynamics and mainstream death, analyze semantic shift mechanisms (bleaching, pejoration), credit origination communities appropriately in linguistic trend reporting, and use linguistic diffusion as an early indicator of broader cultural adoption.

**Section 10 — Measuring and Tracking Memes**

**Summary:** Builds the measurement practice: the unit-of-measurement problem — memes as families of variants require tracking templates, not single posts; data sources — platform APIs and their access limits, social-listening tools, Know Your Meme timelines, hashtag and sound pages as native aggregators, and archive services; template detection methods — image-similarity approaches (perceptual hashing, embedding similarity), sound-matching on video platforms, text-template extraction, and their accuracy limits; meme genealogy reconstruction — first-seen estimation, lineage building across platforms, and the origination-attribution problem (invisible originators, private and deleted sources); spread measurement — instance counts over time, velocity, community breadth, and cross-platform migration tracking; engagement quality signals — remix rate as the key memetic metric (participation beats passive sharing), with saves and duet/stitch counts as platform-specific variants; sentiment and meaning coding — human annotation of variant meaning drift at scale, with LLM-assisted coding workflows and their validation; dashboards for meme tracking — early-warning views combining new-template detection, velocity, and lifecycle stage; evaluation of tracking systems — precision/recall on known meme sets, false-positive control (organic content misflagged as memetic); the measurement ethics — respecting community context, avoiding surveillance of vulnerable groups, and attribution norms; and the tracking stack blueprint — connecting detection, genealogy, velocity, and meaning modules into continuous meme intelligence.

**Absorbed Skill:** Select data sources and detection methods (perceptual hashing, embeddings, sound matching) for template tracking, reconstruct meme genealogies with origination-attribution humility, measure spread via instance velocity, community breadth, and remix rate, implement sentiment and meaning-drift coding with validation, and assemble a continuous meme-tracking stack with precision/recall evaluation.

**Section 11 — Memes in Marketing and Brand Culture**

**Summary:** Covers commercial application with its evidence and risks: meme marketing defined — brands participating in memetic culture via original memetic content, template adoption, and meme-adjacent humor; why it works when it works — native format fluency earning attention and affinity at low production cost, with documented breakout cases; the participation spectrum — observing, referencing, remixing, originating — and the fluency gradient brands must climb; timing discipline — the lifecycle awareness that brand entry at peak or post-peak reads as cringe (the 'how do you do, fellow kids' failure mode); authenticity and community detection — audiences' sensitivity to corporate memetic incompetence, and the brands-as-community-members standard; risks catalogued — context blindness (adopting memes with hidden meanings or dark origins), tone mismatch during news events, appropriation of community language, legal exposure (copyright in templates, music licensing, right-of-publicity), and platform-policy issues; the measurement of meme marketing — attention earned, sentiment, remix response, and the weak evidence connecting memetic participation to sales; co-optation dynamics — what happens to a meme when brands adopt it (acceleration to exhaustion, community abandonment), making brand entry a lifecycle event for the meme itself; influencer and creator mediation — paying fluency versus faking it; brand-specific memetic assets — owned formats and characters that function as proprietary templates; case post-mortems — successes and failures decomposed by lifecycle timing, community reading, and execution fluency; and the strategy frame — memes as one layer of cultural participation, not a campaign substitute.

**Absorbed Skill:** Position brand participation on the observe-to-originate spectrum with fluency requirements, time memetic entry against lifecycle stage to avoid cringe positioning, audit memes for hidden context, appropriation, and legal exposure before adoption, measure meme-marketing effects on attention and sentiment with conversion humility, and analyze co-optation dynamics when brand entry accelerates meme exhaustion.

**Section 12 — Political Memes, Propaganda, and Memetic Warfare**

**Summary:** Covers the contested terrain: political memetics — memes as persuasion, mobilization, and identity-marking instruments in campaigns and movements; the ambivalence problem — Phillips' documented finding that memetic participation does not equal endorsement, making intent inference from sharing unreliable; ironyposting and plausible deniability — how ambiguity shields political messaging, and the analytical difficulty of decoding ironic layers; memetic propaganda — coordinated template deployment, state and non-state actor use, and the documented cases and their evidence quality; the radicalization-pathway debates — claims about meme-mediated pipeline dynamics examined against mixed empirical findings; weaponized humor — dehumanization through memetic formats, and how humor lowers resistance to extreme content per the research; election memetics — template battles, deepfakes and synthetic media's emerging role, and platform moderation responses; astroturf memetics — manufactured grassroots appearance via coordinated template spread, with detection signals (synchrony, account-age patterns, cross-platform coordination); counter-memes and corrective memetics — their limited documented effectiveness; regulation and governance — meme content policies, political-ad transparency rules, and their evasion dynamics; measurement practice for political memes — tracking template deployment, coordination signals, and spread networks (connecting to the network course); and the analyst's discipline — separating observed spread patterns from claims about effects and intent.

**Absorbed Skill:** Analyze political memes with the ambivalence and irony-layer cautions, detect coordinated template deployment via synchrony and account-pattern signals, evaluate radicalization-pathway and effects claims against mixed evidence, track synthetic and deepfake memetic content, and report political memetic dynamics separating spread patterns from intent and effect claims.

**Section 13 — Memes as Folk Culture: Community, Meaning, and Belonging**

**Summary:** Teaches the interpretive layer: memes as folklore — communal authorship, variation in transmission, and group-marking functions connecting internet memes to oral-tradition scholarship (Coleman, Tangherlini lineage); memetic communities — subcultures whose shared templates constitute membership literacy (in-group decoding as boundary maintenance); meaning-making practice — how communities negotiate meme meanings through use, and the analyst's task of reading meaning from practice rather than text alone; identity and belonging functions — memes as social glue, affiliation signals, and collective coping mechanisms (trauma humor, generational humor); context collapse — when memes cross community boundaries and meanings break (the misreading problem), and how collapse drives both virality and conflict; the cringe economy — community policing of memetic competence, second-hand embarrassment as norm enforcement, and mainstream adoption as cringe-marking; platform-community relationships — how site cultures (imageboards, Reddit, TikTok niches) produce distinct memetic styles and ethics; memetic literacy as cultural capital — who can read and make memes fluently, and the generational dimension (Gen Z/Alpha fluency connecting to the netnography course); respect and extraction tensions — researcher and brand entry into memetic communities as extractive encounters, with ethical documentation practice; memes and collective memory — how memetic archives preserve community history; and the interpretive toolkit — reading memes as situated social practice requiring community context, not just content analysis.

**Absorbed Skill:** Analyze memes as folk-cultural practice with communal authorship and group-marking functions, read meaning from community use rather than text alone, diagnose context-collapse events and cringe dynamics as boundary maintenance, assess memetic literacy as cultural capital across generations, and apply ethical documentation practice when entering memetic communities as analyst or brand.

**Section 14 — Ethics, Harms, and Responsible Meme Analysis**

**Summary:** Confronts the dark side and the analyst's responsibilities: harmful memes — harassment campaigns using memetic formats (brigading templates, mocking formats targeting individuals), dehumanization mechanics, and documented harm cases; the targets problem — individual victims of memetic pile-ons versus public-figure contexts, and the different ethical weights; dark origins and laundering — memes whose meanings hide racist, antisemitic, or extremist signification (documented symbol-cooptation cases), and the due-diligence practice of checking genealogy before use or amplification; attribution and credit — origination erasure (especially of Black creators' memetic labor, the documented TikTok-credit controversies), and citation norms for analysts and brands; privacy in memetic subjects — unwitting people who become memes (the 'main character' phenomenon) and the consent absence; analysis ethics — avoiding surveillance of vulnerable communities, aggregate reporting standards, and not amplifying harmful templates through documentation; platform governance — moderation of harmful memes, enforcement inconsistency, and the Streisand dynamics of suppression; researcher positionality — the analyst as participant-observer with power to legitimize or expose; responsible reporting — describing harmful memetic dynamics without instructional replication; legal dimensions — defamation, harassment law, and copyright in memetic contexts at awareness; and the professional standard — a pre-use ethics checklist for memetic analysis and participation covering origin check, target check, community impact, and amplification consequences.

**Absorbed Skill:** Audit memes for dark origins and hidden harmful signification before use or reporting, apply attribution and credit norms countering origination erasure, assess harm to memetic targets with consent and privacy awareness, report harmful memetic dynamics without instructional replication, and operate a pre-use ethics checklist covering origin, target, community impact, and amplification.

**Section 15 — Synthesis and Capstone: Meme Intelligence in Practice**

**Summary:** Prepares expert integration: the complete memetic analysis framework — form (what the meme is), lineage (where it came from), mechanics (how it replicates and mutates), selection (what filters it passes), lifecycle (what stage it occupies), and meaning (what it signals in each community) as the six analytic dimensions; the meme-intelligence workflow — detection of emerging templates, genealogy reconstruction, velocity and remix measurement, lifecycle classification, community-meaning reading, and strategic recommendation; forecasting memetic trajectories — applying course-3 methods to template-instance series with the fast-cycle, high-noise realities; cross-course integration — network analysis (course 2) for spread paths and bridge communities, NLP and sentiment (course 1) for meaning drift at scale, attention economics (course 4) for the competitive frame, and diffusion theory (course 12) for adoption curves; brand memetic strategy — the participation decision tree covering lifecycle timing, community fit, ethics clearance, and measurement plan; case synthesis — worked full-cycle analyses of breakout memes from origination to exhaustion/revival; failure post-mortems — brand, political, and analytic failures decomposed by dimension; the reporting standard — memetic findings communicated with ambivalence caution, attribution credit, and lifecycle-timing advice; tooling blueprint — the tracking stack (detection, genealogy, velocity, meaning) as an operating system; and the capstone — a complete meme-intelligence project: detect an emerging memetic family, reconstruct its lineage and mutation patterns, measure its spread and lifecycle stage across platforms, read its community meanings and selection pressures, assess its commercial or cultural significance with ethical clearance, and deliver a monitored intelligence report.

**Absorbed Skill:** Analyze any meme across the six dimensions (form, lineage, mechanics, selection, lifecycle, meaning), operate an end-to-end meme-intelligence workflow from detection to recommendation, forecast template trajectories with fast-cycle noise handling, integrate network, NLP, and attention methods into memetic analysis, and deliver ethically cleared, attribution-conscious meme-intelligence reports with lifecycle-timed strategic advice.
