## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the heavy-tail revolution with modern rigor: power-law degree distributions — definition, the log-log visual trap, and preferential attachment as the generating mechanism (Barabási-Albert model, Price's cumulative advantage lineage, fitness extensions); hubs and their systemic consequences — robustness-yet-fragility previewed, and hub dominance of diffusion reach; the measurement revolution — Clauset-Shalizi-Newman maximum-likelihood fitting with goodness-of-fit and comparison against log-normal and stretched-exponential alternatives, and the field-wide finding that many claimed power laws fail careful testing; the universality debate — Broido-Clauset's reanalysis showing scale-free structure is rare in real networks and the current nuanced consensus; rich-get-richer dynamics beyond degree — attention inequality on content platforms, viral concentration statistics (most content spreads minimally), and superstar economics (Rosen) as the market-level pattern; exponent meaning for diffusion — how tail shape changes outbreak-size distributions; long-tail claims (Anderson) checked against empirical distributions; and measurement practice — how sampling and API truncation distort tails.
