## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Represent relational data as properly typed graphs with matrix and list representations.
2. Distinguish network explanations from attribute-based explanations.
3. Construct networks from platform and co-occurrence data with sampling awareness, compute and interpret basic graph descriptives.
4. Select analysis tools matched to task and scale.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the field and its claim on trend analysis: graphs and networks defined — nodes, edges, and the type system (directed, weighted, signed, bipartite, multilayer) — with the course's orienting thesis that ideas, content, and behaviors spread through relational structure rather than through undifferentiated populations; network thinking versus attribute thinking — the relational paradigm where position and connection carry explanatory power; exemplar networks across domains (follow graphs, repost cascades, co-mention networks, citation graphs, infrastructure); where network data comes from — platform graphs and their access realities, surveys, interaction logs, and co-occurrence construction — with each source's sampling caveats; representation fundamentals — adjacency matrices, adjacency lists, edge lists — and basic descriptives (size, density, degree distributions at first look); analysis, visualization, and modeling distinguished as three modes of network work; the tooling landscape (NetworkX, igraph, graph-tool, Gephi) at orientation level; and the course map from static structure through position and community to dynamics, diffusion, and trend intelligence.
