## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Run and interpret removal and percolation experiments on real networks.
2. Distinguish robustness from resilience in networked systems.
3. Identify structural vulnerabilities (bridges, hubs, superspreader potential) in brand, movement, and platform graphs.
4. Design redundancy and diverse-seeding strategies with cost awareness.
5. Assess fragility of the trend-monitoring systems themselves.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches resilience through the network lens: node and edge removal experiments — random failure versus targeted attack on hubs, the Albert-Jeong-Barabási robustness-yet-fragility finding, and removal curves as robustness measures; percolation on networks — giant-component emergence and disappearance, critical thresholds, and the mapping between percolation and epidemic thresholds; cascading failure models — load redistribution and threshold cascades with infrastructure analogies (power grids) and platform analogies (API dependency chains); sociotechnical fragility — bridge-node vulnerability (who holds discourse communities together), key-person risk in movements and brands, and platform-dependency risk for monitoring operations; superspreaders and overdispersion — the k-parameter view of why most seeds spark nothing and few ignite everything; robustness interventions — redundancy, diverse seeding, multi-platform presence — and their costs; the robustness-versus-resilience distinction (resistance to shock versus recovery after it); and applying fragility analysis to trend systems themselves — what breaks a monitoring pipeline when an API or platform changes.
