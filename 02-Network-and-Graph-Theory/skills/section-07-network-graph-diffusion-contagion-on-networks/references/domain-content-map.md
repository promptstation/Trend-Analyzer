## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the core mechanics of how ideas spread: epidemic-style compartment models on networks — SI, SIS, SIR — with network-dependent thresholds (the spectral-radius condition intuition) and why homogeneous-mixing models misestimate spread on clustered, heavy-tailed graphs; discrete cascade models — independent cascade and linear threshold (Kempe's formalization) and their assumptions; simple versus complex contagion (Centola-Macy) — information that spreads on single exposure versus behaviors requiring social reinforcement, with the experimental evidence and the clustering paradox (clusters help complex contagion, hinder simple); weak ties — Granovetter's bridges carrying novel information; threshold models of collective behavior (Granovetter 1978) and informational cascades (Bikhchandani-lineage) as the classic foundations; cascade data analysis — reconstructing diffusion trees from timestamps, cascade-size distributions and their heavy tails, and broadcast-shaped versus virally-shaped cascades; competing and cooperating memes — co-diffusion and interference at overview; and the contagion-metaphor audit — homophily-versus-influence confounding (the Christakis-Fowler obesity-contagion claims and the Shalizi-Thomas critique) as the standing warning for trend claims.
