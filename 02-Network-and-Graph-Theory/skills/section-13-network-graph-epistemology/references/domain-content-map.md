## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers belief and information as network phenomena: precise definitions — echo chambers (social homophily plus active exclusion of contrary sources) versus filter bubbles (algorithmic personalization) — and the evidence status of each (exposure studies showing most online exposure is more diverse than assumed, with cross-cutting-exposure findings and recent reassessments); measuring segregation and fragmentation in platform data; polarization — affective versus issue-based, and network signatures of each; opinion-dynamics models — DeGroot averaging, bounded-confidence models (Deffuant, Hegselmann-Krause), and stubborn-agent variants as tools for reasoning about belief convergence and divergence; misinformation diffusion findings — the Vosoughi-Roy-Aral falsehood-spreads-farther result with its novelty-and-emotion mechanisms and the interpretation debates it spawned; radicalization-pathway claims (YouTube rabbit holes) and their mixed empirical record; epistemic network analysis at overview; interventions — bridging exposure experiments and documented backfire effects; and the trend-analyst application — tracking narrative communities and their divergence as an ecosystem-level indicator.
