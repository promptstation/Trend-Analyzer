## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers models as the testing and explanation layer: why null models matter — structure is only surprising relative to a baseline; Erdős-Rényi random graphs — their properties (binomial degrees, sharp giant-component transition) and the catalog of ways real networks differ (heavy tails, clustering, assortativity); the configuration model as the practical degree-preserving null for significance testing; small worlds — Milgram's letter experiment read critically (completion bias), Watts-Strogatz rewiring producing high clustering with short paths, and small-worldness measurement practice; statistical network models — stochastic block models and degree-corrected variants as the modern community-inference workhorses, exponential random graph models (ERGMs) with their what-generates-structure question and degeneracy history; latent-space and network-regression approaches at overview; generative versus statistical versus mechanistic modeling distinguished; fitting, simulation, and goodness-of-fit practice; and using nulls for trend claims — testing whether an observed co-mention cluster exceeds degree-preserving expectation.
