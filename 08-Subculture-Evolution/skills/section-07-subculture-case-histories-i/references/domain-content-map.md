## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Studies the canonical commercial-pipeline cases in depth: punk (1970s) — origination in NYC and London scenes, style-as-bricolage shock tactics, media moral panic and the Sex Pistols spectacle, rapid fashion incorporation (Vivienne Westwood's boutique-to-runway path), and the enduring template of subcultural shock-to-merchandise cycles; hip-hop — Bronx party origins, the four elements (DJing, MCing, breaking, writing) as integrated culture, the longest and richest commercial pipeline in modern music history, streetwear's birth from hip-hop style (Dapper Dan's luxury-logo remixing as appropriation-in-reverse, later legitimated by actual luxury collaboration), and the credit-and-extraction controversies as the standing ethics case; grunge — Seattle scene origins, the sudden mainstream explosion via Nevermind, the whiplash incorporation (Marc Jacobs' Perry Ellis grunge collection fired for being too early, then validated), and Cobain's resistance-to-fame as the selling-out drama personified; skate culture — decades-long pipeline from countercultural nuisance (pool skating, trespass associations) through fashion absorption (Vans, Supreme's boutique-to-LVMH arc as the full commercialization trajectory) to Olympic sport, with skateboarding's authenticity debates at each stage; cross-case patterns extracted — media amplification preceding commercial absorption, brokers translating scenes to markets, dilution gradients, and originator compensation patterns; the case-method discipline — using history as calibrated priors for contemporary pipeline prediction rather than as deterministic templates; and the counterfactual habit — asking what would have happened without specific amplification events, to identify genuine pipeline drivers versus luck.
