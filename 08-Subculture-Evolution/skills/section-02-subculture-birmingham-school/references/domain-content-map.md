## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the founding theoretical tradition with its critiques: the CCCS lineage — Hall-Jefferson's Resistance Through Rituals framing subcultures as symbolic working-class resistance through leisure and style; Hebdige's Subculture: The Meaning of Style in depth — style as signifying practice, the subcultural career from spectacular emergence through commercial diffusion; the key concepts — bricolage (recombining mundane objects into charged style: safety pins, swastikas-as-shock), homology (the fit between a group's values and its style choices), and incorporation (the two-stage neutralization: the commodity form converting style to merchandise, and the ideological form redefining it through media labels); the working-class anchoring of CCCS analysis and its documented limits; the critiques that professional use requires — McRobbie's feminist critique (girls' invisibility in subcultural accounts and the bedroom culture finding), the race critique (Black cultural production treated as separate from 'subculture'), and the class-reduction objection (style as automatic resistance); moral panic theory — Cohen's folk-devils framework, media amplification of subcultural threat (mods and rockers lineage), and the pattern's persistence in digital outrage cycles; media's double role — panicking about subcultures while spreading them (Thornton's point that media constitute rather than merely report scenes); applying CCCS today — where resistance readings still fit (activist scenes, countercultures) versus where they overreach (aesthetic-first communities); and the analytic legacy — style as readable text, incorporation as the co-optation model, and amplification cycles as the visibility engine.
