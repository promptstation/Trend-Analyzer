## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the classic incubation infrastructure: music scenes as subculture engines — the documented pattern that durable subcultures typically anchor in music practice (punk, hip-hop, rave, grunge origins), with music providing gathering rhythm, identity content, and style soundtracks; venue ecosystems — clubs, DIY spaces, record stores, and venues as physical infrastructure whose closure patterns (documented in multiple cities) reshape scene viability; rave and club culture — the acid-house-to-EDM pipeline as a completed mainstreaming arc, PLUR culture, festival commercialization, and the underground persistence (warehouse revival patterns); scene geography — local scenes versus translocal scenes (Straw), hub cities' role (NYC, London, Berlin, Atlanta, Seoul as successive music-fashion engines), and digital platforms' partial substitution for geography; DJ and producer culture — taste-brokering as the music-scene version of coolhunting, radio and mixtape lineage to playlist and SoundCloud curation; fan-production infrastructures — zines, mixtapes, fan edits, and their digital successors (discographies, edit accounts, fan wikis) as scene memory and diffusion channels; dance and embodiment — dance styles as subcultural practice with distinct pipelines (breaking to Olympics, TikTok dance challenges as micro-formations); live-event economies — festivals, raves, and concerts as visibility amplifiers and commercialization milestones; the streaming-era shift — platform discovery bypassing local-scene incubation, producing internet-first artists without scene grounding, and what this changes about pipelines; scene-health indicators — venue density, release velocity, event frequency, and newcomer integration as measurable formation-stage signals; and the cross-domain flow — music scenes seeding fashion, slang, and aesthetics that carry the wider trend (the multiplier effect).
