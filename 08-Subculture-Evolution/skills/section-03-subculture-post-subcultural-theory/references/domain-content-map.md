## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the theoretical update: the post-subculture turn — Muggleton's Within Subculture arguing for fragmented, stylistically promiscuous, media-literate identities replacing bounded subcultures; scenes theory — Straw's and Bennett's frameworks for locally organized, translocally connected cultural clusters (music scenes as the paradigm), with scene participation's flexibility versus subcultural commitment; neo-tribes — Maffesoli's concept via Bennett: fluid, affective, consumption-centered gatherings with multiple simultaneous memberships (connecting to the netnography course); the lifestyle concept — identity as curated stylistic bricolage across domains rather than total subcultural immersion; the authenticity debates — post-subcultural claims that authenticity died examined against evidence that authenticity policing intensified online (gatekeeping as the constant); superdiversity and intersection — contemporary formations organized around race, gender, sexuality, and disability identity as well as taste, which CCCS underweighted; digital-native formations — platform-shaped communities (TikTok aesthetics, Discord scenes) whose boundaries are algorithmic and participatory rather than geographic or class-based; the taste-public lineage — Gans' taste cultures and Bourdieu's distinction (cultural capital, taste as class marker) as the sociological substrate both traditions share; what persists across the debate — style signaling, in-group/out-group boundary work, commercial absorption, and generational differentiation; and the working synthesis — a layered model where scenes, tribes, aesthetic communities, and committed subcultures coexist, and the analyst classifies rather than assumes.
