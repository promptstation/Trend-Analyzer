## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the fashion industry as the pipeline's main engine: the fashion-cycle literature — Davis's ambivalence-driven cycles, Simmel's class-imitation logic and its modern revisions, and the seasonal-system structure (runway-to-retail timing, pre-collections, drops); trend-forecasting industry — WGSN and agency practice as professionalized pipeline scanning (their methods: street scanning, trade shows, cultural analysis, color and fabric direction), and the self-fulfilling-prophecy effect when forecasts coordinate industry behavior; trickle mechanisms in current operation — bubble-up's dominance in streetwear eras (luxury hiring from street culture, the Supreme-Nike-LVMH arc), trickle-down persistence in formalwear, and trickle-across in fast fashion; fast fashion as acceleration engine — Shein-lineage ultra-fast models compressing design-to-availability to days, their subcultural-mining practices, and the resulting pipeline collapse (underground signals absorbed before scenes consolidate); luxury appropriation patterns — the design-house mining of subcultural style (documented controversies and their resolution patterns: credit, collaboration, or backlash); the coolhunter and trend-scout function — professional broker roles, their sourcing networks, and ethical practice variance; retail translation — how elements dilute through assortment (statement piece to basic), price tiering, and marketing framing; influencer and creator mediation — creators as the modern broker layer with faster but shallower translation; secondhand and archival markets — vintage as pipeline feedback (mainstream re-mining past subcultural styles) and archival-fashion culture as a connoisseur economy; resistance and licensing — scenes' responses (anti-fashion stances, controlled collaboration) and IP questions in style; and the absorption-analysis toolkit — tracking a signal from scene origin through forecast appearance, runway, fast fashion, and mass retail with timing benchmarks per tier.
