## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the status economy inside subcultures: subcultural capital — Thornton's concept (cool, knowledge, credentials, connections valued within the scene, convertible to mainstream status only imperfectly), and its accumulation routes (time invested, obscure knowledge, production skills, network position); gatekeeping — boundary defense against poseurs and tourists, its mechanisms (competence tests, reference checks, style policing, cringe accusations), and its functions (protecting meaning, preserving distinction value); selling out — the betrayal framework: commercial success as authenticity loss, the documented cases (underground artist mainstreaming backlash), and its asymmetry (audiences forgive some crossings, never others); the hipster dynamic — mainstreaming-by-discovery: early adopters abandoning signals once widely copied, driving continuous symbol churn (the mechanism behind aesthetic acceleration); authenticity work — how members and commercial adapters perform authenticity, and the detection arms race; scene hierarchy mapping — core (creators, elders), active participants, peripheral members, tourists — each with different influence on evolution; the commercial-adapter's dilemma — brands need subcultural legitimacy but their participation drains it; insider-outsider brokers — the specific people (A&R scouts, coolhunters, creators who cross over) who translate between scenes and markets, and their credibility economics; platform-era capital shifts — follower counts as convertible-but-suspect capital, and algorithmic visibility versus scene respect as competing currencies; the lifecycle connection — gatekeeping intensity as a measurable stage indicator (low at formation, peaking at growth boundary, dissolving or hardening at mainstreaming); and analysis practice — mapping a scene's capital system, hierarchy, and gatekeeping intensity as trend-stage evidence.
