## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the quantitative layer: the detection problem — subcultural signals are by definition low-volume, high-noise, and pre-mainstream, requiring sensitivity that mainstream trend metrics lack; the signal hierarchy — linguistic (new slang, naming events), aesthetic (visual style clusters), behavioral (new practices, venue/platform migrations), commercial (early products, small-brand adoption), and media (niche-press coverage) signals, each with detection lead times; data sources ranked by earliness — scene-internal platforms (Discords, forums, imageboards) earliest but access-limited and ethics-heavy, then niche social (TikTok niches, Tumblr successors, Pinterest boards), then search data (Google Trends inflections), then mainstream social volume, then retail data last; tracking methods — search-index monitoring for subcultural terms, hashtag and sound velocity on TikTok, aesthetic-wiki change logs, Bandcamp and Discogs release tagging, resale-market prices (signal commodification), venue and event listings, and small-brand product monitoring; the false-positive problem — distinguishing genuine formation signals from platform artifacts, bot activity, marketing plants, and one-off stunts, with validation triangulation (multiple independent sources plus netnographic ground-truthing per course 7); signal-strength scoring — combining volume, velocity, breadth (community spread), depth (commitment evidence: production not just consumption), and durability indicators into composite early-warning scores; lead-time evidence — documented examples where subcultural signals preceded mainstream visibility by measurable months, calibrating expectations by category; the tracking stack — tools and workflows for continuous monitoring (listening tools, trend platforms, custom scrapers where permitted, human scene contacts as sensors); dashboard design — the subcultural watchlist with lifecycle-stage classification, signal scores, and alert thresholds; analyst network building — relationships with scene members, brokers, and niche journalists as irreplaceable sensors; and the integration standard — measurement claims always paired with ethnographic verification before commercial action.
