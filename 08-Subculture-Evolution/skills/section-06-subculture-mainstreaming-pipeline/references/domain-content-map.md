## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the core dynamic of the course: the pipeline stages — origination (scene innovation solving identity/distinction needs), visibility (media, brokers, platform algorithms surfacing it), adaptation (commercial actors simplifying, combining, and deracinating elements), mainstream diffusion (mass-market availability and adoption), and post-mainstream fate (residual persistence, nostalgic revival, or death); the diffusion models applied — trickle-down (Simmel: elites imitated, masses adopt, elites move on), bubble-up (Polhemus: street style absorbed upward by fashion systems), and trickle-across (simultaneous multi-class diffusion) — with evidence that all three operate depending on category and era; commodity incorporation — Hebdige's first neutralization: style elements converted to purchasable goods, separating sign from lived meaning; ideological incorporation — the second neutralization: media relabeling (from threat to trend to costume); the dilution gradient — what survives mainstreaming (visual surfaces) versus what doesn't (values, contexts, competencies), explaining mainstream versions' aesthetic-without-meaning character; speed analysis — historical pipeline durations (decades for denim, years for hip-hop fashion, months for digital aesthetics) and the compression drivers (fast fashion, platform discovery, creator economy); who profits and who pays — value extraction patterns, originator compensation failures, and the cultural-debt critique; resistance responses — scene retreat (going darker), re-innovation (moving to new signals), and public call-out campaigns; partial absorption cases — elements adopted while the scene persists intact; and the prediction target — using pipeline logic to forecast what mainstreams next, when, and in what diluted form.
