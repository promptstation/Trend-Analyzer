## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Extends analysis beyond style-and-music formations: hacker and maker cultures — the tech subculture lineage (hacker ethic, open source, maker spaces, cyberpunk fiction's feedback into tech aesthetics), their value systems (competence, sharing, anti-authority) and commercial absorption (startup culture's appropriation of hacker signifiers); gaming subcultures — platform and genre scenes (fighting-game community, speedrunning, modding, esports), their deep competence hierarchies, and mainstreaming arcs (gaming aesthetics in fashion, streamer-as-celebrity); internet-native formations — imageboard cultures (4chan's documented influence pipelines and toxicity entanglement), Tumblr-era fandom cultures and their diaspora patterns, TikTok micro-communities (the 'sides'), and Discord scene-building; AI and emerging-tech subcultures — the current formations around AI art, local-model tinkering, and tech-optimism/pessimism tribes, as live examples of formation and consolidation stages; crypto and fintech tribes — normies-versus-CT dynamics, memecoin cultures as finance-aesthetic hybrids, and their boom-bust lifecycles; fitness and biohacking scenes — GymTok, running culture's fashion absorption (the gorpcore-adjacent arc), and body-modification communities; maker-to-industry pipelines — hardware subcultures (drones, 3D printing, e-bikes) with distinct commercialization paths; the competence-core pattern — tech subcultures organized around skill demonstration rather than style display, changing their gatekeeping and absorption dynamics (skills absorb into products rather than aesthetics into fashion); platform dependency risks — internet-native formations' vulnerability to platform death (Tumblr's 2018 content-policy collapse scattering its cultures as the documented case); and detection practice — monitoring formation signals in tech subcultures (new forums, jargon, tool ecosystems, gathering platforms).
