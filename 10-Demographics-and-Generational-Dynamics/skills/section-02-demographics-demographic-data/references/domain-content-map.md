## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches the evidence base: censuses — the decennial headcount as the gold standard for structure and small-area detail, its coverage gaps, political controversies, and the shift toward register-based censuses in some countries; vital registration systems — birth and death recording, completeness variation by country (near-universal in high-income, partial in parts of Africa and Asia per UN assessments), and what incomplete registration does to statistics; sample surveys — the Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) program, labor-force and household surveys, and their design strengths (representativeness, frequency) and limits (sample size, recall bias); administrative and digital-trace data — school enrollment, tax records, mobile-phone and platform data as emerging demographic sources with coverage biases (the platform-user-is-not-the-population problem); the major projection datasets — UN World Population Prospects (methods, variants, revision cycle), IHME/Lancet forecasts (education-and-met-need-driven models), Wittgenstein Centre (education structure), and how they disagree and why; national statistical offices — navigating their releases, and Kenya-type examples of national census and survey infrastructure at working level; data quality assessment — coverage error, age-heaping (digit-preference artifacts), sex ratios as plausibility checks, and revision histories; access practice — pulling WPP data, DHS microdata, World Bank indicators, and national statistics with proper citation and vintage tracking; comparability traps — differing definitions (urban, household, employment) across countries and years; and the analyst's data plan — matching demographic questions to the best available source with documented quality caveats.
