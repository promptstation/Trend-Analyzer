## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Teaches reading population shape: the population pyramid — construction, reading, and the characteristic shapes (expansive/youth-bulge, constrictive/aging, stationary) with real national examples; cohort analysis — birth cohorts as the analytic unit, cohort size waves (echo generations, baby booms and busts) propagating through life stages over decades; the aging-into logic in practice — tracking cohort movement into key consumption stages (teens, first-job, family-formation, peak-earning, retirement) as the mechanic of market growth and decline; dependency ratios — youth and old-age dependency defined, interpreted, and their economic-growth debates (demographic dividend conditions: when large working-age shares convert to growth versus burden); median age as summary measure — its global spread (teens in youngest countries, 40s in oldest) and trend-market implications; age-structure determinism checks — the same structure with different institutions and technologies produces different markets; sex structure — sex ratios at birth, migration-selectivity effects, and marriage-market squeeze dynamics (documented consequences of skewed ratios); the sub-national layer — age structure varies dramatically within countries (urban-rural, regional), requiring disaggregation for real analysis; life-stage versus chronological age — the analyst's translation layer (delayed milestones shifting the mapping); cohort-size effects on culture — the documented hypothesis that large cohorts shape institutions and that small cohorts face different labor and housing markets; and the market-shape toolkit — converting any pyramid into market-size trajectories by age-band with cohort-flow reasoning.
