## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the engines of population change: fertility — TFR measurement and interpretation, the global fertility decline (halved since the 1960s, with below-replacement now spanning most of the world outside sub-Saharan Africa), drivers (female education, child survival, contraception access, urbanization, costs of raising children), and the low-fertility trap debates; fertility surprises — the post-2010 acceleration of decline in previously high-fertility countries and forecast-revision lessons; mortality — life expectancy measurement and decomposition (infant mortality's outsized effect), the longevity revolution, and mortality shocks (pandemics, conflict, HIV's documented demographic scar in southern Africa); cause-of-death structure — the epidemiological transition from infectious to chronic disease dominance and its consumption implications; migration — the three processes' wildcard: international migration stocks and flows, remittances as economic-demographic links, internal migration (rural-urban as the dominant global flow), and migration's disproportionate effect on young-adult age structures; migration measurement problems — undocumented flows, return migration, and diaspora dynamics; net migration's role in projections — why high-income countries' futures depend heavily on migration assumptions while fertility dominates elsewhere; replacement migration concept and its limits; population momentum — the built-in growth or decline from current age structure regardless of immediate rate changes, the most counterintuitive and most important forecasting concept; and the process-interaction view — how fertility decline, longevity gain, and migration selection combine into the age-structure shifts that drive trend markets.
