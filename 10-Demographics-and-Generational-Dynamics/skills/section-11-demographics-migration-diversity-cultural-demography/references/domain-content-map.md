## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers migration's demographic-cultural effects: international migration scale and patterns — the ~300-million-range international migrant stock, dominant corridors, refugee versus labor versus family migration categories, and the selectivity profile (young, educated-skewed) reshaping both origin and destination age structures; diasporas as trend bridges — transnational communities maintaining dual consumption cultures, remittance economies, and diaspora-mediated trend flows (food, fashion, media circulating through diaspora networks ahead of mainstream adoption); diversity trajectories — the documented composition shifts in high-income countries, 'majority-minority' projections and their category-dependence, and superdiversity (Vertovec) as the analytic upgrade beyond ethnic binaries; immigration and cultural change debates — assimilation versus pluralism models, segmented assimilation evidence, and the second-generation convergence patterns relevant to trend forecasting (second generations as cultural brokers); anti-immigration politics as trend context — the demographic anxiety discourse and its market and media effects; internal migration cultures — rural-urban migrants' consumption transitions (the documented spending-pattern shifts with urbanization); migration and labor markets — sector concentration (care, construction, tech, agriculture) and the demographic-dependence of high-income economies on migration for workforce maintenance; data politics — migration statistics as contested terrain (undocumented estimation methods, definitional variance, politicized numbers) requiring source literacy; the brain-circulation update — from brain-drain to circulation models with diaspora-skill networks; and the cultural-demography brief — projecting diversity-driven market segments with second-generation timing and diaspora-channel awareness.
