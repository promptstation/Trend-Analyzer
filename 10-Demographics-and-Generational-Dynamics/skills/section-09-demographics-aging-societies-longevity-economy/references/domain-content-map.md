## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the dominant structural shift of the century: population aging measured — share-over-65 trajectories (doubling globally this century per UN), the super-aged societies (Japan, Italy, Korea lineage) and the pace differences (East Asia aging faster than Europe did); aging drivers — low fertility plus longevity gains, with momentum meaning aging continues even if fertility recovers; the old-age heterogeneity revolution — 'the elderly' spanning 30+ years of life stages (young-old active, old-old, oldest-old), making age-band disaggregation essential; longevity economy — the market response framework: health and care demand, the retirement-finance gap, age-tech, and the consumption patterns of longer lives; work and aging — extended working lives, the pension-policy debates, ageism in labor markets, and the skills implications; the care-economy expansion — care work demand (formal and informal), its gendered structure, and its market formation as a major trend sector; loneliness and social infrastructure — documented aging-social-isolation patterns and the community/tech responses; marketing to older cohorts — the representation gap (advertising's youth bias versus purchasing reality), cohort-specific aging (Boomers aging differently than Silents did), and the 'perennial' strategy debate; aging in developing countries — aging before rich (China's scale, India's trajectory, Africa's later window), with policy-preparedness variance; the second demographic dividend — aging societies' asset-accumulation dynamics; death and end-of-life markets — the mortality-composition shift (more deaths at older ages), funeral and memorial innovation, and digital legacy; and the aging trend brief — separating age effects from cohort effects in older-consumer analysis, the recurring discipline.
