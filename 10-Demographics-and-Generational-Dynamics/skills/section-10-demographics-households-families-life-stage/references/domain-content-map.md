## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the micro-structure of consumption: household composition trends — shrinking household size globally, single-person household growth (documented rates in high-income and rising in middle-income countries), single-parent households, and multigenerational living's partial return (economic-driven, documented in several countries post-2008); the demographic drivers — later marriage, lower fertility, longer lives, and union-instability each reshaping household statistics; milestone delay — the documented shifts in median ages of leaving home, completing education, first partnership, first birth, and home purchase across cohorts, and the 'transitions to adulthood' research framing; fertility timing versus fertility quantum — births postponed (mean age at first birth rising) versus births foregone (completed cohort fertility falling), distinguished because they have different market implications; the second demographic transition — the value-shift framework (individualization, cohabitation, non-marital childbearing) with its theoretical status debated; family structure and consumption — household as consumption unit (bulk versus single-portion economics), child-related spending concentration in fewer children (the 'quality-quantity' shift), and pet-economy growth as family-substitution evidence; divorce, repartnering, and blended households — their consumption-pattern complexity; housing-type implications — unit-size demand, rental-market pressure, and senior-housing formation; cross-national variance — the Nordic, East Asian, Southern European, and African household regimes differ fundamentally, forbidding template application; the life-stage marketing layer — traditional life-stage segmentation updated for delayed and de-sequenced milestones; and the household brief — projecting composition shifts and their consumption consequences for a target market.
