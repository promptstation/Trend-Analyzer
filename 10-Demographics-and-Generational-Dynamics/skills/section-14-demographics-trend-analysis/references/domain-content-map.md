## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Connects demographics to the full trend workflow: the demographic-conditions framework — every trend sits in a structure of who exists, where, how old, how rich, and how they live, conditioning the trend's ceiling, geography, and timing; the aging-into forecast — the primary integration tool: projecting cohort entry volumes into trend-relevant life stages to time market windows (when does Gen Alpha hit teen consumption, when does the youth bulge hit labor markets); market sizing with demographics — population × incidence × spend architectures for category forecasts, with demographic growth decomposition (how much of market growth is population versus penetration versus price); the demographic-segmentation upgrade — moving beyond generation labels to structure-based segments (life-stage × income × geography × household type) with generational overlays as communication skins; combining with other course signals — demographics (slow) with attention (fast), sentiment (medium), and diffusion (structural) as the multi-speed trend model, with demographics setting baselines the faster layers modulate; geographic targeting — layering demographic data onto trend maps for where-not-just-what analysis; the demographic headwind/tailwind audit — for any trend thesis, listing the structural forces supporting and opposing it (aging against youth-product growth, urbanization for convenience services); data-pipeline practice — building demographic data feeds (WPP, national stats, surveys) into trend dashboards with vintage tracking; visualization craft — pyramids, cohort-flow diagrams (the Norton-style animation of populations aging), and map layers that communicate structure to strategists; the counterfactual discipline — checking whether a trend claim survives demographic reality (a youth trend in an aging market); and the demographic-strategy memo — the professional deliverable stating structural conditions, timing windows, and geographic priorities for a trend decision.
