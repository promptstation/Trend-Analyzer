## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Applies the framework to the world's divergent regions with emphasis on the fastest-changing: sub-Saharan Africa — the last high-fertility region (TFR declining but momentum huge), the world's youngest population (median ages under 20 across many countries), projected population doubling by 2050 per UN medium variant, the youth-bulge opportunity-and-risk structure (demographic dividend conditions: education, jobs, governance — otherwise instability), rapid urbanization with informal-economy dominance, and the mobile-first digital adoption creating distinct trend ecologies (mobile money, creator economies, Afrobeats and Amapiano's global export as documented cultural-diffusion cases); Kenya and East Africa specifics at working level — census and data infrastructure, urban youth culture, and the region's tech-creative hubs; North Africa and Middle East — fertility transitions largely complete, youth bulges maturing, migration pressures, and rentier-economy consumption patterns; South and Southeast Asia — India's demographic-window peak (now the largest country by population), its regional variance, and Southeast Asia's middle-class expansion; East Asia — the extreme-aging frontier (Korea's sub-0.8 TFR as the documented extreme, Japan's super-aged normalization, China's post-one-child contraction), with global implications for savings, innovation, and care markets; Europe — shrinkage-and-aging management through migration, regional east-west variance; the Americas — US diversity and Millennial-Gen Z ascent, Latin America's fast aging before rich; the divergence synthesis — one world, five demographic regimes, forbidding single-narrative global trend analysis; the data caveat — projection uncertainty widest exactly where change fastest (Africa's fertility assumptions dominate global totals); and the regional brief format — structure, trajectory, dividend risk, and trend-ecology profile per region.
