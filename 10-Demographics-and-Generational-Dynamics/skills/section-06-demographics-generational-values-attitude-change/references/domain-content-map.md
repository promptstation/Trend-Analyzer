## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the evidence on cohort value differences: the World Values Survey / Inglehart-Welzel framework — the two-axis map (traditional-versus-secular-rational, survival-versus-self-expression) with intergenerational value change as societies develop, and the theory's scope and criticisms; documented cohort-value patterns with evidence grading — younger cohorts' consistently higher climate concern, LGBTQ+ acceptance, and mental-health openness across many surveys; the religiosity-decline cohort pattern (secularization as generational replacement more than individual switching); economic-attitude differences — younger cohorts' housing pessimism, institutional distrust, and work-meaning shifts, with period-effect confounds acknowledged (they face genuinely different conditions, not just different values); the values-versus-behavior gap — stated values translating unevenly into purchasing (the documented attitude-behavior gap in sustainable consumption); socialization mechanisms — why cohort values differ: formative-period imprinting, technology-mediated socialization, parental transmission with modification, and education expansion; measurement instruments — WVS/EVS, Pew, Gallup, and regional barometers (Afrobarometer, Latinobarómetro) as the analyst's value-data sources, with question-wording comparability cautions; value change within cohorts — individuals do change (aging effects on values), so cohort averages move; the culture-versus-economy debate — whether value shifts drive or follow economic development (modernization theory versus revision); applying values to trends — value clusters as demand predictors (self-expression values predicting experience and identity consumption), and value-shift velocity as trend-timing input; and the values brief — documenting a cohort's value profile with evidence grades, confound notes, and behavioral-translation caveats.
