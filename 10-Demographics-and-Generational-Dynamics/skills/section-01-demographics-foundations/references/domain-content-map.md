## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Define core demographic measures (TFR, life expectancy, median age, dependency ratios) with precision.
2. Explain the demographic-transition stages and locate countries within them.
3. Formulate the four demographic trend questions (aging-into, concentration, purchasing power, cohort values).
4. Position demographics as the slow predictable layer conditioning trend scale and geography, and avoid demographic determinism by naming mediating factors.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces demography and its trend-analysis role: demography defined — the statistical study of populations (size, structure, distribution) and the three processes driving change (fertility, mortality, migration); the course's orienting claim — demographics are the slowest-moving and most predictable layer of trend analysis: today's 15-year-olds are 2035's 25-year-old consumers, and population structure constrains which trends can scale, where, and for whom; the demographic transition — the historical shift from high birth/death rates to low birth/death rates, its four stages, and why countries sit at different stages today (sub-Saharan Africa early-transition with youth bulges; East Asia and Europe post-transition with aging); key measures at first contact — crude birth and death rates, total fertility rate (TFR), life expectancy, median age, dependency ratios — defined precisely because loose usage corrupts analysis; the trend-analyst's demographic questions — who is entering key life stages (the 'aging into' logic), where population is growing and concentrating, which cohorts hold purchasing power, and how values differ across cohorts; demographics versus destiny — the discipline that structure conditions but culture, technology, and policy mediate outcomes; the data landscape previewed — censuses, vital registration, surveys, and projections (UN WPP as the standard); the age-period-cohort problem stated upfront as the field's central methodological challenge; scale awareness — global (8+ billion, peaking this century per UN medium variant), regional divergence, and the analyst's duty to disaggregate; and the course map from data and processes through generations, urbanization, aging, migration, and forecasting to the capstone demographic trend analysis.
