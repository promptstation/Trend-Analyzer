## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the economic-demographic layer: income versus wealth versus consumption distinguished — the three lenses on purchasing power with different cohort stories; the Millennial wealth debate — the documented findings (lower wealth-at-age than prior cohorts in several countries, contested causes: 2008 timing, housing costs, student debt versus 'avocado toast' narratives) analyzed with method care; cohort economic scarring — labor-market-entry recessions' persistent earnings effects (the Oreopoulos-lineage evidence), making entry timing a cohort-defining economic variable; housing as the generational fault line — affordability deterioration, ownership-age shifts, rent-versus-own cohort divides, and housing's downstream consumption effects; the consumption lifecycle — age-profiles of spending (formation-stage housing and children, mid-life peak, retirement shifts) as the predictable layer, versus cohort-specific tastes as the variable layer; purchasing-power concentration — current global patterns (Boomer/Gen-X wealth dominance, Millennial prime-earning ascent, Gen Z entry) and their market implications; emerging-market dynamics — youth bulges with income growth as the largest consumer-expansion story (the African and South Asian urban youth markets), and purchasing-power-parity framing; the inflation and cost-of-living period effect — 2020s price shocks hitting cohorts differently by life stage; spending-structure shifts — services-versus-goods, experience consumption, and digital-goods shares by cohort; data sources — national accounts, household surveys (LSMS lineage), consumption panels, and platform spending data with biases; and the purchasing-power brief — combining lifecycle prediction with cohort-specific economic conditions for market timing.
