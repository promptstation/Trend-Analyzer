## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the generation concept with scientific discipline: generations defined — birth cohorts sharing formative-period experiences producing (on average) distinct dispositions, from Mannheim's 'problem of generations' theory (fresh contact with heritage during youth) to current practice; the standard generational map — Lost/Greatest/Silent, Baby Boomers (post-WWII boom, roughly 1946-1964), Gen X (roughly 1965-1980), Millennials (roughly 1981-1996), Gen Z (roughly 1997-2012), Gen Alpha (2013 onward) — with Pew's definitional rationale (analytic utility, not natural kinds) and boundary disputes acknowledged; the age-period-cohort identification problem in depth — the three effects are linearly dependent (cohort = period − age), so separating them requires assumptions, and most generational claims actually confound age effects (life stage) with cohort effects (generation); the critique literature — generational essentialism (assigning fixed traits), the marketing origin of some boundaries, within-generation diversity exceeding between-generation differences on most measures, and the Twenge-debates as the case study in period-versus-cohort attribution; what survives critique — formative-events effects with evidence (economic conditions at labor-market entry scarring earnings, technology environments shaping media habits), and generations as useful rough lenses for communication and cohort-tracking; the formative-events inventory — for each current generation, the documented shared conditions (Millennials: 2008 recession entry, smartphone adolescence; Gen Z: pandemic schooling, algorithmic socialization; Gen Alpha: AI saturation, full creator-tool childhood) held as hypotheses not essences; cross-national generation variance — the same birth years experience different formative conditions in different countries, so generations are region-specific constructs; measurement practice — cohort-comparative survey design controlling for age and period where possible; the analyst's generation discipline — stating which effect (age, period, cohort) a claim invokes; and using generations responsibly — communication value versus analytic value distinguished.
